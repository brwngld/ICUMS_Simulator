## Debatable items reviewed and declined (left as written)

1. **11.2 line 50 — "Class 7, Radioactive material, a class of its own with no divisions."** The sheet lists no divisions for Class 7 but does not state the negative outright. The statement matches the UN Model Regulations structure (divisions exist only for classes 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6). Kept as uncontentious standard knowledge.
2. **11.1 line 114 (mirrored in assessment Q2) — CIP's ICC(A) minimum "reflecting that CIP is used across all modes and commonly for higher-value, damage-prone consignments."** The sheet states the requirement but not the rationale. The rationale is ICC's own published reason for the Incoterms 2020 change and is hedged with "commonly". Kept as general business knowledge.
3. **11.2 line 56 — "a consignment of electronics containing lithium batteries … is a dangerous-goods consignment even though it looks harmless."** The modal rulebooks carve limited exceptions for small batteries installed in equipment, so the blanket phrasing is a teaching simplification. It is directionally correct (Class 9 includes lithium batteries), consistent with the sheet, and deliberate given the lesson's "small quantity does not exempt" note. Declined to soften; softening would undercut the lesson's core safety message.
4. **Assessment Q3 option 4 — "contributes in proportion to the value its cargo brought safe to the adventure"** versus the lesson's "roughly in proportion". The option is a correct statement of the adjustment principle; adding "roughly" would not change its truth. Declined.
5. **11.1 line 118 — "transferable evidence of cover"** where the sheet says "assignable evidence of cover". Same legal effect; paraphrase-safe. Declined to harmonise.
6. **11.4 line 102 — subrogation ("having compensated, an insurer commonly takes over the claim against the party responsible").** Not in the research sheets; hedged with "commonly" and already declared in FACTS & SOURCES as general insurance practice, which WRITING_SPEC 5 permits. Kept.
7. **11.3 worked example 2 — weighbridge figures 21,400 / 21,430 kg.** Illustrative and labelled as such; the 30 kg agreement on ~21.4 t is plausible for the example's logic. Kept.
