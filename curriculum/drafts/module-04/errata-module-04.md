## CHANGES MADE (5)

1. lesson-4.1.md (transit-controls block) — "the Inter-State Road Transit Convention, the ISRT,
   in force since 1982 among ECOWAS member states" changed to "adopted in 1982 by ECOWAS member
   states". Rationale: the sheet verifies adoption on 29 May 1982 (Convention A/P4/5/82); adoption
   and entry into force are different legal events and the sheet does not verify an in-force date.

2. lesson-4.1.md (FACTS & SOURCES) — corrected the self-description that claimed no
   prescribed-routes content is stated in the lesson. The lesson does present "prescribed routes
   and time limits" generically as the fourth transit ingredient; the note now says so and scopes
   the DO-NOT-CLAIM item to exact operational specifics (document forms, guarantee amounts, named
   routes, digitisation status).

3. lesson-4.3.md (FOB block) — "Under FOB, unusually among the rules taught in this lesson, the
   two lines sit together" changed to "Under FOB the two lines sit together; the split comes with
   the C rules, where the cost line runs on while the risk line does not." Rationale: the
   comparative was inaccurate — the cost and risk lines also coincide under EXW, FCA, DAP and DDP
   (all taught in the lesson); only group C splits them, as the lesson's own group-C block
   correctly states.

4. lesson-4.3.md (CFR/CIF block — the flagged line) — "A CIF price therefore buys the buyer more
   than an FOB price at the same load port, because freight to the destination sits inside the
   seller's costs" changed to "A CIF price is therefore a higher price than an FOB price at the
   same load port, because the freight to the destination sits inside the seller's costs."
   Rationale: "buys the buyer more" invited a value-for-money or protection reading that the
   lesson elsewhere explicitly dispels (risk stays at shipment under the C rules); the certain,
   sheet-supported fact is price composition. No pedagogical point lost.

5. lesson-4.4.md (documents/discrepancies block) — "a claused bill showing defects will be
   rejected" changed to "will normally be rejected", matching the sheet's phrasing ("banks will
   usually reject it"); the categorical form was an unsourced strengthening.
