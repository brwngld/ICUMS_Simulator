## 1. Changes made

### 1.1 lesson-9.4.md — "Four different creditors" corrected

- Location: Important Note "Time-based charges are a separate family from
  freight, duties, and taxes", closing sentence.
- Was: "Four different creditors, four different bases, four separate invoices."
- Now: "Four different charge families, four different bases, four separate invoices."
- Reason: the sentence's own preceding list shows only three creditor
  organisations — the shipping line charges both the ocean freight and
  demurrage/detention; the terminal charges storage; customs charges duties
  and taxes. "Four different creditors" contradicted the sentence directly
  above it and would teach a countable error. The four-item framing (freight,
  demurrage/detention, storage, duties) is itself correct, so the minimal fix
  was to name the families, not the creditors.

No other edits were required. All other claims in the five files resolved to

## 3. Debatable items reviewed and KEPT (no change)

These were considered for correction or softening and deliberately left as
written. Logged so the owner can revisit.

1. **Part-of-a-day billing presented as "the part-of-a-day rule"**
   (lesson-9.4, chargeable-periods block; used in the permit worked example
   and assessment Q12). Billing any part of a day as a full day is common
   industry practice but is not stated in the research sheet. Kept because
   the lesson's own definition of the daily rate ties it to "according to
   the applicable tariff", the tariff-variability caution opens the lesson,
   and every figure applying the rule is labelled illustrative. If the
   owner wants maximum conservatism, it could be reworded to "tariffs
   commonly bill any part of a day as a full day".

2. **Free time "commonly starts when the container is discharged and made
   available for collection"** (lesson-9.3 closing note; lesson-9.4 opening
   block and worked examples). The sheet defines free time but does not
   state its start event. Kept because the statement is hedged with
   "commonly", 9.4's caution explicitly names "the event that starts the
   clock" as tariff-specific, and the 9.3 facts block already discloses
   that the phrasing follows the lesson tasking.

3. **Assessment Q1 assigns berth assignment to the port authority.** In
   concessioned container terminals the operator commonly participates in
   berth planning, but the lesson (9.1) teaches that the authority
   "controls where vessels berth", and WRITING_SPEC section 11 requires
   questions answerable from the lesson text. Kept as lesson-faithful.

4. **"Shipping lines also commonly require outstanding time-based charges
   to be settled before they authorise release"** (lesson-9.4) and the
   lighter lien sentence in lesson-9.3. Not in the sheets; general industry
   practice, hedged with "commonly", consistent with the release-chain
   teaching in 9.3. Kept.

5. **"Customs systems process declarations around the clock"** (lesson-9.1,
   24-hour-chain note). Generic statement about ports generally, not
   attributed to Ghana. Kept.

6. **Combined demurrage-and-detention "common in some trades and regions"**
   (lesson-9.4). The sheet marks regional practice detail medium
   confidence; the lesson deliberately asserts no regional specifics and no
   day counts. Kept as the correct conservative rendering.
