## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the electronic exchange of logistics information and the transition from paper-based to digital documentation. Learners will explore electronic data interchange, structured messages, electronic invoices, transport records, and document management systems. The lesson also explains how standardised data exchange reduces manual processing, improves accuracy, and supports coordination among logistics stakeholders.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define electronic data interchange and explain its purpose.



Describe how electronic information exchange supports logistics operations.



Identify common documents and messages exchanged electronically.



Distinguish between paper-based and digital documentation processes.



Explain the importance of data standards and consistent information formats.



Describe how digital documentation can reduce processing errors and delays.



Identify the role of document management systems in logistics.



Explain the importance of electronic authentication and document integrity.



Recognise security and compliance considerations associated with digital records.



Describe how digital systems can improve communication among logistics stakeholders.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) :: The structured electronic exchange of business information between computer systems using agreed standards.

Digital Documentation :: The creation, processing, exchange, and storage of documents in electronic form.

Electronic Invoice :: An invoice created, transmitted, or processed electronically.

Electronic Bill of Lading (eBL) :: An electronic transport record designed to perform the relevant functions of a bill of lading under applicable legal and contractual arrangements.

Electronic Data Standard :: An agreed specification governing the structure and interpretation of exchanged information.

UN/EDIFACT :: A United Nations standard for electronic data interchange in administration, commerce, and transport.

XML :: Extensible Markup Language, a structured format used to represent and exchange information.

JSON :: JavaScript Object Notation, a structured data format commonly used for information exchange between software applications.

Document Management System (DMS) :: Software used to organise, store, retrieve, and control electronic documents.

Digital Signature :: A cryptographic mechanism used to support verification of a digital message or document's origin and integrity.

Data Validation :: The process of checking information against defined requirements or rules.

Interoperability :: The ability of different systems to exchange and use information effectively.

Electronic Audit Trail :: A digital record of transactions, changes, and user activities that supports traceability.
