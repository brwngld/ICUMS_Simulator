## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces the functions, infrastructure, and operational activities of commercial seaports. Learners will examine how ports facilitate international trade through vessel berthing, cargo loading and unloading, cargo handling, and temporary storage. The lesson also explores the roles of port authorities, terminal operators, stevedores, and other service providers, including the importance of safety, operational coordination, and efficient cargo movement.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define port operations and explain their importance in international logistics.
Identify the major facilities and infrastructure found within commercial seaports.
Explain the roles of port authorities, terminal operators, and stevedoring companies.
Describe the procedures involved in vessel arrival, berthing, and cargo handling.
Distinguish between loading, unloading, and cargo transfer operations.
Identify common cargo-handling equipment used at seaports.
Explain how different cargo types influence port-handling procedures.
Recognize common safety requirements and operational risks associated with port activities.
Describe how port stakeholders coordinate to facilitate cargo movement.
Explain the importance of port efficiency in reducing transportation delays and logistics costs.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Seaport :: A coastal or inland-waterway facility equipped to accommodate vessels and support maritime transportation and cargo-handling activities.
Port Operations :: The activities involved in managing vessel movements, cargo handling, storage, and related services within a port.
Port Authority :: An organisation responsible for administering, regulating, or managing port facilities and activities within its mandate.
Terminal Operator :: A company or organisation responsible for managing cargo-handling and related activities at a designated terminal.
Stevedoring :: The work of loading, unloading, stowing, and handling cargo on or around vessels.
Stevedore :: A worker or company engaged in loading, unloading, and handling cargo aboard vessels.
Berth :: A designated location where a vessel is secured for loading, unloading, or other port operations.
Quay :: A structure along a waterfront where vessels can berth and cargo-handling activities can take place.
Cargo Handling :: The physical activities involved in loading, unloading, lifting, transferring, and securing goods.
Ship-to-Shore Crane (STS) :: A large crane used primarily for loading and unloading containers between vessels and container terminals.
Reach Stacker :: A mobile lifting machine used for handling and stacking freight containers.
Port Congestion :: A condition in which demand for port facilities or services exceeds available operational capacity, causing delays.
Vessel Turnaround Time :: The period between a vessel's arrival at a port and its departure after completing required port activities, as defined by the applicable measurement method.
