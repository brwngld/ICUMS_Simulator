## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the operations of container terminals and inland container depots as important components of modern logistics networks. Learners will explore how containers are received, identified, inspected, stacked, stored, and transferred between vessels, trucks, and other transport systems. The lesson also explains the functions of container yards, freight stations, and inland depots, including how these facilities support cargo consolidation, deconsolidation, customs-related processes, and onward transportation.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define container terminals and inland container depots.
Explain the role of container terminals in international cargo transportation.
Identify the major operational areas within a container terminal.
Describe the procedures involved in receiving, stacking, storing, and dispatching containers.
Explain the purpose and functions of inland container depots.
Distinguish between container yards, container freight stations, and inland container depots.
Identify common equipment used in container terminal operations.
Describe how containers are transferred between vessels, trucks, and rail systems.
Explain the importance of container identification, tracking, and inventory control.
Recognize the role of inland logistics facilities in reducing congestion and supporting cargo distribution.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Container Terminal :: A facility designed for receiving, handling, storing, and transferring freight containers between transportation systems.
Inland Container Depot (ICD) :: An inland facility used for handling and storing freight containers and, where authorised, providing customs-related services.
Container Yard (CY) :: A designated area used for storing and managing freight containers before loading, collection, or onward transportation.
Container Freight Station (CFS) :: A facility where cargo may be received, consolidated, deconsolidated, stored, and prepared for onward movement.
Dry Port :: An inland logistics facility connected to a seaport through road, rail, or other transport links and providing specified port-related services.
Container Stacking :: The arrangement of freight containers in designated storage positions within a terminal or depot.
Yard Planning :: The organisation of container storage locations and movement activities to support efficient terminal operations.
Rubber-Tyred Gantry Crane (RTG) :: A mobile gantry crane commonly used to lift and stack containers within container yards.
Rail-Mounted Gantry Crane (RMG) :: A gantry crane operating on fixed rails and used for handling containers in terminals or intermodal facilities.
Empty Container Depot :: A facility used for storing, inspecting, maintaining, and managing empty freight containers.
Intermodal Terminal :: A facility where cargo or transport units are transferred between different transportation modes.
Container Gate-In :: The recorded entry of a container into a terminal, depot, or designated facility.
Container Gate-Out :: The recorded exit of a container from a terminal, depot, or designated facility.
Container Tracking :: The monitoring and recording of container locations, movements, and operational status.
