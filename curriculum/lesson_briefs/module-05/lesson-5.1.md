## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces freight forwarding as an essential service within domestic and international logistics. Learners will examine the role of freight forwarders in organising cargo transportation, coordinating logistics service providers, managing shipment information, and supporting importers and exporters. The lesson also explains the distinction between freight forwarders, carriers, and customs brokers, and highlights the professional responsibilities involved in handling customers' shipments.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define freight forwarding and explain its importance in logistics operations.
Identify the primary responsibilities of a freight forwarder.
Explain how freight forwarders coordinate transportation between origins and destinations.
Distinguish between freight forwarders, carriers, shipping agents, and customs brokers.
Describe the relationship between freight forwarders and importers, exporters, and other logistics service providers.
Identify the information and documents freight forwarders require to arrange shipments.
Explain the importance of professional conduct, accurate information, and customer service in freight forwarding.
Recognise common operational challenges encountered by freight forwarders.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Freight Forwarding :: The service of organising and coordinating the transportation of goods on behalf of customers, often together with related logistics services.
Freight Forwarder :: A person or company that arranges and coordinates the transportation of goods and associated logistics services.
Shipper :: The party that tenders goods for transportation, commonly identified as the consignor in shipping documents.
Consignee :: The person or organisation designated to receive goods under a transport document.
Carrier :: A person or company undertaking responsibility for transporting goods under a contract of carriage.
Customs Broker :: A person or company authorised, where required by national law, to represent clients or assist them with customs declarations and clearance procedures.
Shipping Agent :: A person or company appointed to represent a shipping line or vessel operator in a designated port or geographical area.
Freight Forwarding Agent :: A logistics intermediary acting on behalf of a customer or another principal in arranging freight transportation services.
Contract of Carriage :: An agreement under which a carrier undertakes to transport goods between specified locations under agreed conditions.
Logistics Coordination :: The organisation and management of activities involving transportation, documentation, cargo handling, and communication among logistics participants.
Shipment Management :: The planning, coordination, monitoring, and administration of cargo movements from origin to destination.
