## Summary (from curriculum)
his lesson introduces shipping instructions and cargo consolidation as important activities in freight forwarding operations. Learners will explore how shipping instructions are prepared and submitted, the information required for transport documentation, and how multiple consignments may be combined to improve transportation efficiency. The lesson also explains the differences between full container load and less than container load shipments, and the responsibilities involved in coordinating consolidated cargo.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define shipping instructions and explain their purpose in freight forwarding.
Identify the essential information commonly included in shipping instructions.
Explain how shipping instructions support the preparation of transport documents.
Describe the purpose and benefits of cargo consolidation.
Distinguish between full container load and less than container load shipments.
Explain the basic procedures involved in consolidating and deconsolidating cargo.
Identify the responsibilities of freight forwarders when handling consolidated shipments.
Recognize common shipping instruction errors and their potential consequences.
Explain the importance of accurate cargo descriptions, quantities, weights, and consignee information.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Shipping Instructions :: Information provided to a carrier or its representative for arranging shipment details and preparing transport documentation.
Cargo Consolidation :: The process of combining multiple smaller shipments into a larger transport unit or consignment.
Deconsolidation :: The process of separating consolidated cargo into its individual shipments for onward delivery or handling.
Full Container Load (FCL) :: A container shipment generally arranged for the use of one shipper or consignee, rather than being consolidated with unrelated shipments.
Less than Container Load (LCL) :: A shipment that occupies part of a container and is typically consolidated with other shipments.
Consolidator :: A logistics service provider that combines smaller cargo consignments into larger shipments for transportation.
Container Freight Station (CFS) :: A facility where cargo may be received, consolidated, deconsolidated, stored, and prepared for onward transportation.
House Bill of Lading (HBL) :: A bill of lading issued by a freight forwarder or non-vessel-operating common carrier to its customer for a shipment.
Master Bill of Lading (MBL) :: A bill of lading generally issued by an ocean carrier to the party contracting with it, which may be a freight forwarder or consolidator.
Cargo Description :: Information identifying the nature, type, and characteristics of goods being transported.
Verified Gross Mass (VGM) :: The verified total gross mass of a packed container, determined in accordance with applicable SOLAS requirements.
Shipping Cut-Off :: The deadline established by a carrier or terminal for completing a specified shipment requirement before departure.
