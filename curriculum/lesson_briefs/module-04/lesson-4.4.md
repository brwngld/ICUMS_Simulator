## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces the major payment methods used in international trade and explains how buyers and sellers manage payment obligations and commercial risks. Learners will examine advance payment, open account transactions, documentary collections, and letters of credit, including their advantages, limitations, and suitability for different trading relationships.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Explain the importance of payment arrangements in international trade.
Identify the major payment methods used in international commercial transactions.
Describe how advance payment and open account arrangements operate.
Explain the basic processes involved in documentary collections.
Describe the purpose and operation of documentary letters of credit.
Compare international payment methods based on buyer risk, seller risk, cost, and complexity.
Identify the roles of banks in facilitating international trade payments.
Recognise factors that influence the selection of an appropriate payment method.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
International Trade Payment :: The transfer of funds between trading parties to settle financial obligations arising from international commercial transactions.
Advance Payment :: A payment arrangement in which the buyer pays the seller before goods are shipped or delivered.
Open Account :: A payment arrangement in which the seller supplies goods before receiving payment, usually within an agreed credit period.
Documentary Collection :: A payment arrangement in which banks handle commercial or financial documents according to collection instructions, without generally guaranteeing payment.
Documents Against Payment (D/P) :: A documentary collection arrangement in which specified documents are released to the buyer against payment.
Documents Against Acceptance (D/A) :: A documentary collection arrangement in which specified documents are released to the buyer against acceptance of a time draft.
Letter of Credit (L/C) :: A bank's undertaking to honour a complying presentation of documents under the terms and conditions of the credit.
Issuing Bank :: The bank that issues a letter of credit at the request of an applicant or on its own behalf.
Advising Bank :: The bank that advises a letter of credit to the beneficiary, normally after checking its apparent authenticity.
Beneficiary :: The party in whose favour a letter of credit is issued, usually the seller or exporter.
Applicant :: The party requesting the issuance of a letter of credit, usually the buyer or importer.
Bill of Exchange :: A written, unconditional order directing one party to pay a specified sum of money to another party, either on demand or at a determinable future time.
Trade Finance :: Financial products and services used to facilitate domestic and international trade transactions.
Payment Risk :: The possibility of financial loss arising from delayed, incomplete, or failed payment.
