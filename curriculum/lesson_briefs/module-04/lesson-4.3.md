## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces Incoterms as internationally recognised trade rules used to clarify the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in the delivery of goods. Learners will examine how Incoterms allocate transportation obligations, costs, and risks, and understand the importance of selecting appropriate terms for different modes of transportation and commercial arrangements.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define Incoterms and explain their purpose in international trade.
Identify the organisation responsible for publishing Incoterms rules.
Explain how Incoterms allocate delivery responsibilities, costs, and risks between buyers and sellers.
Distinguish between Incoterms rules suitable for any mode of transport and those intended for sea and inland waterway transport.
Describe the basic responsibilities associated with commonly used Incoterms rules.
Explain the difference between the transfer of risk and the payment of transportation costs.
Recognise matters that Incoterms do not determine, including transfer of ownership and payment terms.
Apply basic Incoterms principles to simple international shipping scenarios.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Incoterms :: Internationally recognised trade rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce that define specified delivery responsibilities, costs, and risks between buyers and sellers.
International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) :: An international business organisation responsible for publishing and maintaining the Incoterms rules.
Incoterms 2020 :: The edition of Incoterms rules that entered into effect on 1 January 2020.
EXW (Ex Works) :: An Incoterms rule under which the seller makes goods available at a named place, with the buyer assuming most transportation responsibilities and risks from that point.
FCA (Free Carrier) :: An Incoterms rule under which the seller delivers goods to the carrier or another person nominated by the buyer at the named place.
FOB (Free on Board) :: An Incoterms rule for sea and inland waterway transport under which the seller delivers goods on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment.
CFR (Cost and Freight) :: An Incoterms rule under which the seller pays the cost and freight necessary to bring goods to the named destination port, while risk transfers when the goods are on board the vessel at the port of shipment.
CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) :: An Incoterms rule similar to CFR, but requiring the seller to obtain the specified minimum level of cargo insurance cover for the buyer's risk.
DAP (Delivered at Place) :: An Incoterms rule under which the seller delivers goods when they are placed at the buyer's disposal on the arriving means of transport, ready for unloading at the named destination.
DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) :: An Incoterms rule under which the seller assumes extensive responsibilities, including import clearance and applicable import duties, to deliver goods at the named destination ready for unloading.
Transfer of Risk :: The point at which the risk of loss or damage to goods passes from the seller to the buyer under the applicable contractual arrangement.
Named Place :: The specific location or port identified in an Incoterms clause to establish the relevant delivery or cost obligations.
