## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the importance of environmental responsibility, professional ethics, and continuous improvement in modern logistics management. Learners will explore sustainable transportation, energy efficiency, waste reduction, ethical procurement, regulatory compliance, and responsible business practices. The lesson also introduces structured improvement methods and explains how organisations can reduce environmental impacts while maintaining operational efficiency, accountability, and long-term competitiveness.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define sustainable logistics and explain its importance.



Identify major environmental impacts associated with transportation and warehousing.



Describe measures for improving energy efficiency and reducing logistics emissions.



Explain the importance of responsible waste management and resource utilisation.



Identify ethical issues affecting procurement, transportation, and logistics services.



Explain the importance of transparency, integrity, and regulatory compliance.



Describe how continuous improvement supports operational performance.



Identify common approaches used to analyse and improve logistics processes.



Explain how sustainability performance can be measured.



Recommend practical improvements that balance cost, service quality, environmental responsibility, and ethical considerations.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Sustainable Logistics :: The planning and management of logistics activities with consideration for environmental, social, and economic impacts.

Green Logistics :: Logistics practices intended to reduce environmental harm associated with transportation, storage, packaging, and distribution.

Carbon Footprint :: The total greenhouse gas emissions associated with an activity, organisation, product, or service, expressed in carbon dioxide equivalents.

Greenhouse Gas Emissions :: The release of gases that contribute to the greenhouse effect, including carbon dioxide and methane.

Energy Efficiency :: The achievement of a specified output or service using less energy.

Reverse Logistics :: The movement of goods from downstream locations back through the supply chain for returns, repair, reuse, recycling, or disposal.

Circular Economy :: An economic approach that seeks to retain the value of products and materials through reuse, repair, refurbishment, recycling, and other resource-efficient practices.

Ethical Procurement :: The acquisition of goods and services in accordance with principles of fairness, integrity, transparency, and responsible conduct.

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) :: An organisation's approach to managing its social, environmental, and ethical responsibilities.

Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) :: Categories used to assess environmental impacts, social responsibilities, and organisational governance practices.

Continuous Improvement :: An ongoing process of identifying and implementing changes to improve operational performance.

Lean Management :: A management approach focused on delivering value while identifying and reducing waste in processes.

Kaizen :: A continuous improvement philosophy emphasising ongoing, incremental improvements.

Root Cause Analysis :: A structured method for identifying the underlying causes of problems.

Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) :: A four-stage improvement cycle involving planning, implementation, evaluation, and corrective action.

Sustainability Indicator :: A measurable value used to assess environmental, social, or economic sustainability performance.
