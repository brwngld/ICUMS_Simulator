## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces the procedures used to examine and verify logistics and international trade documents before shipment processing. Learners will explore how to compare information across commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, certificates, and permits to identify inconsistencies, omissions, and errors. The lesson also explains the importance of document verification in reducing delays, preventing financial losses, and supporting compliance with contractual and regulatory requirements.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define document verification and explain its importance in logistics operations.
Identify the main documents that should be reviewed during shipment preparation.
Describe the procedures involved in checking commercial and transportation documents.
Explain how to compare shipment details across multiple documents.
Identify common discrepancies involving names, addresses, quantities, weights, values, and cargo descriptions.
Recognize the operational and regulatory consequences of inaccurate documentation.
Explain the procedures for reporting and correcting documentation discrepancies.
Describe the importance of document authenticity, validity, and completeness.
Apply a basic document verification checklist to a sample shipment.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Document Verification :: The process of examining documents to confirm their accuracy, completeness, consistency, authenticity, and compliance with applicable requirements.
Document Discrepancy :: An inconsistency, omission, or error found within a document or between related documents.
Document Authentication :: The process of confirming that a document is genuine or has been issued or endorsed by an authorised party.
Data Consistency :: The agreement of corresponding information across related documents and records.
Document Validation :: The process of checking whether a document satisfies specified rules, formats, or acceptance requirements.
Cargo Description Mismatch :: A discrepancy arising when the description of goods differs between documents or does not accurately reflect the cargo.
Quantity Discrepancy :: A difference between the quantity of goods stated in documents and the quantity recorded elsewhere or physically verified.
Weight Discrepancy :: A difference between declared and recorded or measured cargo weights.
Document Amendment :: An authorised correction or modification made to a previously issued document.
Document Checklist :: A structured list used to verify the availability, completeness, and accuracy of required documents.
Compliance Review :: An examination of documents or procedures to determine whether applicable legal, regulatory, or contractual requirements have been satisfied.
Document Control :: The systematic management of document preparation, review, approval, storage, access, and revision.
Audit Trail :: A chronological record of activities or changes that allows transactions and document modifications to be traced.
