## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the bill of lading and air waybill as major transportation documents used in international cargo movement. Learners will explore their purposes, contents, legal and operational functions, and the differences between maritime and air transport documentation. The lesson also introduces different types of bills of lading and explains how transport documents support cargo identification, shipment tracking, and delivery procedures.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define a bill of lading and an air waybill.
Explain the principal functions of a bill of lading in maritime transportation.
Describe the purpose and characteristics of an air waybill in air cargo operations.
Identify the essential information contained in bills of lading and air waybills.
Distinguish between negotiable and non-negotiable transport documents.
Identify common types of bills of lading and their uses.
Explain the differences between master and house transport documents.
Describe the importance of accurate transport documentation for cargo release and delivery.
Recognize common documentation errors that may affect shipment processing.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Bill of Lading (B/L) :: A maritime transport document issued by or on behalf of a carrier that acknowledges receipt of goods and provides evidence of the contract of carriage; certain forms also function as documents of title.
Air Waybill (AWB) :: A non-negotiable air transport document that provides evidence of the contract of carriage and acknowledges receipt of cargo for air transportation.
Master Bill of Lading (MBL) :: A bill of lading generally issued by an ocean carrier to the party contracting with it for transportation.
House Bill of Lading (HBL) :: A bill of lading issued by a freight forwarder or non-vessel-operating common carrier to its customer.
Master Air Waybill (MAWB) :: An air waybill issued by an airline or its authorised agent covering cargo accepted under the airline's contract of carriage.
House Air Waybill (HAWB) :: An air transport document issued by a freight forwarder to a shipper for an individual consignment.
Negotiable Bill of Lading :: A bill of lading issued in a form that allows the rights represented by the document to be transferred through the appropriate endorsement and delivery procedures.
Straight Bill of Lading :: A non-negotiable bill of lading naming a specific consignee.
Sea Waybill :: A non-negotiable maritime transport document that provides evidence of the contract of carriage and receipt of goods without functioning as a document of title.
Shipped on Board :: A notation confirming that goods have been loaded on board the specified vessel.
Port of Loading (POL) :: The port where cargo is loaded onto a vessel for maritime transportation.
Port of Discharge (POD) :: The port where cargo is unloaded from a vessel.
Notify Party :: The person or organisation identified in a transport document to receive notification concerning the shipment's arrival or related events.
