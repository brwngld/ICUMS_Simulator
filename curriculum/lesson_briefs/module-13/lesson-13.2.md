## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines how transportation routes and logistics networks are designed and improved to achieve efficient cargo movement. Learners will explore route selection, distribution networks, vehicle capacity, delivery scheduling, transportation costs, and service requirements. The lesson also introduces network optimisation methods and explains how organisations balance cost, transit time, reliability, and operational constraints when planning transportation activities.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define transportation route optimisation and logistics network optimisation.



Explain the importance of efficient route planning in logistics operations.



Identify factors influencing transportation route selection.



Describe the components of a logistics distribution network.



Distinguish between direct delivery and hub-and-spoke distribution arrangements.



Explain how vehicle capacity and delivery schedules affect route planning.



Identify common transportation constraints and operational trade-offs.



Describe how route optimisation can reduce transportation costs and delivery delays.



Explain the role of digital mapping and optimisation software in transportation planning.



Evaluate basic transportation alternatives using cost, distance, time, and service requirements.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Route Optimisation :: The process of identifying transportation routes that best satisfy specified cost, time, distance, and operational requirements.

Logistics Network :: A system of facilities, transportation links, and operational relationships used to move and distribute goods.

Distribution Network :: The arrangement of warehouses, distribution centres, transport routes, and delivery points used to supply customers.

Direct Delivery :: A distribution arrangement in which goods move directly from an origin to a destination without passing through an intermediate distribution facility.

Hub-and-Spoke Network :: A distribution structure in which shipments move through central hubs connecting multiple origins and destinations.

Vehicle Routing :: The process of assigning delivery or collection locations to vehicles and determining their travel sequences.

Vehicle Capacity :: The maximum permitted or practical quantity, weight, or volume of cargo that a vehicle can transport.

Load Factor :: A measure of the proportion of available transport capacity being utilised.

Delivery Window :: A specified period during which goods are expected or permitted to be delivered.

Route Constraint :: A restriction affecting route selection, such as vehicle capacity, road access, time limits, or delivery requirements.

Network Optimisation :: The improvement of facility locations, transportation links, and operational flows to achieve defined performance objectives.

Transportation Planning :: The process of organising transport resources, routes, schedules, and services to meet shipment requirements.

Backhaul :: A return or subsequent transport movement that may be used to carry additional cargo rather than operating empty.

Last-Mile Delivery :: The final transportation stage in which goods reach their intended recipient.
