## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson explores how organisations determine appropriate inventory quantities while balancing product availability, customer service, and operating costs. Learners will examine inventory classification, reorder points, safety stock, economic order quantity, and service-level targets. The lesson also explains how demand variability and replenishment lead times influence inventory decisions and how excessive or insufficient stock affects supply chain performance.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define inventory optimisation and explain its purpose.



Explain the relationship between inventory availability and customer service.



Identify the major costs associated with holding and replenishing inventory.



Describe the purpose of safety stock and reorder points.



Explain the principles of economic order quantity.

Describe how ABC inventory classification supports stock management.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Inventory Optimisation :: The process of determining inventory policies and quantities that balance cost, availability, and service requirements.

Service Level :: A defined measure of the ability to satisfy customer demand or meet specified service expectations.

Safety Stock :: Additional inventory maintained to reduce the risk of shortages caused by uncertainty in demand or replenishment.

Cycle Stock :: Inventory held to satisfy expected demand between replenishment orders.

Reorder Point :: The inventory position at which a replenishment order should be initiated under a defined policy.

Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) :: An inventory model used to estimate an order quantity that minimises specified ordering and holding costs under its assumptions.

ABC Analysis :: An inventory classification technique that groups items according to their relative importance, commonly based on annual consumption value.

Inventory Holding Cost :: The cost associated with maintaining stock over time.

Ordering Cost :: The expense incurred in placing and processing replenishment orders.

Stockout :: A situation in which an inventory item is unavailable when required.

Fill Rate :: The proportion of demand fulfilled immediately from available inventory under a defined measurement method.

Inventory Turnover :: A measure of how frequently inventory is sold or consumed and replaced during a specified period.

Replenishment Lead Time :: The period between initiating a replenishment order and receiving the goods.

Inventory Position :: A measure of stock availability that commonly includes on-hand inventory and outstanding orders, adjusted for backorders.
