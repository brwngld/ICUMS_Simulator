## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces inventory control principles and the methods used to manage stock within warehouses and distribution facilities. Learners will examine how inventory records are maintained, how stock movements are monitored, and how inventory rotation methods such as First In, First Out (FIFO) and First Expired, First Out (FEFO) help minimize waste and maintain product quality. The lesson also introduces stock levels, reorder points, stocktaking, and common inventory management challenges.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define inventory management and inventory control.
Explain the importance of maintaining accurate inventory records.
Identify common categories of inventory held by businesses.
Describe how stock receipts, issues, and transfers affect inventory balances.
Explain the First In, First Out (FIFO) inventory rotation method.
Explain the First Expired, First Out (FEFO) inventory rotation method.
Distinguish between FIFO and FEFO and identify suitable applications for each.
Define safety stock and reorder points and explain their purposes.
Recognize the operational consequences of stockouts, overstocking, and inventory discrepancies.
Explain how inventory control supports warehouse efficiency and customer service.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Inventory :: Goods and materials held by an organisation for production, operational use, distribution, or sale.
Inventory Management :: The planning, monitoring, and control of inventory to maintain appropriate stock availability and minimise unnecessary costs.
Inventory Control :: The procedures and systems used to track, safeguard, and maintain accurate records of stock.
First In, First Out (FIFO) :: An inventory rotation method in which goods received earlier are issued or used before goods received later.
First Expired, First Out (FEFO) :: An inventory rotation method in which goods with the earliest expiry dates are issued or used first.
Stock Level :: The quantity of a particular inventory item available or recorded at a given time.
Safety Stock :: Additional inventory maintained to reduce the risk of shortages caused by unexpected demand or supply delays.
Reorder Point :: The inventory level at which replenishment should be initiated under a defined stock control policy.
Lead Time :: The time between initiating a replenishment order and receiving the required goods.
Stockout :: A situation in which an inventory item is unavailable when required.
Overstocking :: Holding inventory quantities that exceed reasonable operational requirements.
Inventory Turnover :: A measure of how frequently inventory is sold or used and replaced during a specified period.
Batch Number :: An identifier assigned to a group of goods produced or processed under specified conditions.
Expiry Date :: The date after which a product should no longer be used or distributed under its applicable requirements.
