## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the documents, records, and verification procedures used to maintain accurate warehouse inventory information. Learners will explore goods received notes, stock records, inventory movement documents, dispatch records, and stocktaking reports. The lesson also explains how physical inventory is compared with recorded balances, how discrepancies are investigated, and how stock reconciliation supports accountability, loss prevention, and reliable warehouse operations.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Explain the importance of documentation and recordkeeping in warehouse operations.
Identify common documents used during goods receiving, storage, transfer, and dispatch.
Describe how inventory transactions are recorded and maintained.
Define stocktaking and explain its purpose.
Distinguish between periodic stocktaking and cycle counting.
Explain the procedures involved in comparing physical stock with recorded inventory balances.
Identify common causes of inventory discrepancies.
Describe how damaged, missing, or excess goods should be documented and investigated.
Explain the importance of authorized stock adjustments and audit trails.
Apply basic stock reconciliation principles to a simple warehouse inventory scenario.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Warehouse Documentation :: The documents and electronic records used to support and verify warehouse activities.
Goods Received Note (GRN) :: A document or electronic record confirming the receipt of specified goods into a warehouse or organisation.
Stock Card :: A manual or electronic record showing receipts, issues, and balances for a particular inventory item.
Inventory Ledger :: A record of inventory transactions and balances maintained for stock management and accounting purposes.
Stock Movement :: Any recorded change in the quantity or location of inventory, including receipts, issues, returns, and transfers.
Stocktaking :: The physical counting and verification of goods held in inventory.
Cycle Counting :: An inventory verification method in which selected items are counted regularly rather than counting all inventory at once.
Periodic Stocktaking :: The physical counting of inventory at predetermined intervals.
Stock Reconciliation :: The process of comparing physical inventory quantities with recorded balances and investigating differences.
Inventory Discrepancy :: A difference between recorded inventory information and actual stock quantities or conditions.
Stock Adjustment :: An authorised correction made to inventory records to reflect verified changes or discrepancies.
Damage Report :: A document recording goods identified as damaged, including relevant quantities, conditions, and circumstances.
Inventory Shrinkage :: The reduction in recorded inventory caused by factors such as theft, damage, loss, or recording errors.
Audit Trail :: A chronological record of inventory transactions and changes that supports verification and accountability.
