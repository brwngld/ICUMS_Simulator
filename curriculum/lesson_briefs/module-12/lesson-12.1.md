## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson introduces international transport corridors and their role in facilitating regional and global trade. Learners will examine how road, rail, maritime, and inland transport networks connect ports, industrial centres, border crossings, and inland markets. The lesson also explores corridor infrastructure, trade facilitation, operational bottlenecks, and the importance of regional cooperation in improving cargo movement.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define international transport corridors and explain their importance.



Identify the major infrastructure components of a transport corridor.



Explain how transport corridors connect ports, borders, and inland markets.



Describe the economic importance of regional transportation networks.



Identify common operational challenges affecting transport corridors.



Explain how infrastructure quality influences transit time and transportation costs.



Recognise the importance of regional cooperation and trade facilitation.



Identify examples of major West African transport corridors.



Evaluate basic factors influencing the efficiency of a transport corridor.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Transport Corridor :: A defined network of transportation routes and facilities connecting economic centres, ports, border crossings, and markets.

Trade Corridor :: A transportation and commercial route used to facilitate the movement of goods between trading regions.

Transit Country :: A country through which goods pass while travelling between their origin and final destination.

Landlocked Country :: A country without direct access to an ocean coastline.

Hinterland :: An inland region economically connected to a port or other transportation hub.

Border Crossing :: An authorised location where people, vehicles, or goods move between countries.

Transport Infrastructure :: Physical facilities such as roads, railways, bridges, ports, and terminals supporting transportation.

Corridor Performance :: The measured efficiency and reliability of cargo movement along a transport corridor.

Transit Time :: The duration required for goods to travel between specified points.

Trade Facilitation :: Measures intended to simplify, harmonise, and improve procedures governing international trade.

Bottleneck :: A point or process that restricts the capacity or efficiency of cargo movement.

Intermodal Connectivity :: The ability of different transportation modes and facilities to operate together efficiently.

Regional Integration :: Cooperation among countries to improve economic, commercial, and institutional connections.
