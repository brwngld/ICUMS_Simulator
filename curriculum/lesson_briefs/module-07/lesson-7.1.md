## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the fundamental operations involved in transporting cargo by sea. Learners will explore how ocean freight services operate, the roles of shipping lines and vessel operators, and the procedures involved in planning shipments around vessel schedules. The lesson also introduces shipping routes, port rotations, cargo booking, vessel departure and arrival information, and the operational factors that may affect maritime transportation.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define ocean freight and explain its importance in international trade.
Identify the major participants involved in ocean freight operations.
Distinguish between liner shipping and tramp shipping services.
Explain how vessel schedules and port rotations influence shipment planning.
Describe the basic procedures involved in booking cargo for ocean transportation.
Interpret estimated and actual vessel departure and arrival information.
Identify common factors that cause vessel delays and schedule changes.
Explain the role of transshipment in international maritime transportation.
Describe how shipping lines, freight forwarders, and port operators coordinate ocean freight shipments.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Ocean Freight :: The transportation of commercial goods by sea or other navigable waterways.
Liner Shipping :: A maritime transportation service operating on established routes with published or planned schedules.
Tramp Shipping :: Maritime transportation in which vessels are employed according to cargo availability and commercial arrangements rather than fixed scheduled routes.
Shipping Line :: A company that operates or commercially manages maritime cargo transportation services.
Vessel Schedule :: A planned timetable showing a vessel's expected port calls, departures, and arrivals.
Port Rotation :: The planned sequence of ports visited by a vessel during a voyage or service.
Port of Loading (POL) :: The port where cargo is loaded onto a vessel.
Port of Discharge (POD) :: The port where cargo is unloaded from a vessel.
Estimated Time of Departure (ETD) :: The anticipated date and time when a vessel is expected to depart.
Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA) :: The anticipated date and time when a vessel is expected to arrive.
Actual Time of Departure (ATD) :: The recorded date and time when a vessel actually departs.
Actual Time of Arrival (ATA) :: The recorded date and time when a vessel actually arrives.
Transshipment :: The transfer of cargo from one vessel or transport service to another during its journey to the final destination.
Feeder Vessel :: A vessel used to transport cargo between smaller ports and larger hub ports or terminals.
Shipping Route :: A maritime transportation path connecting ports or trading regions.
