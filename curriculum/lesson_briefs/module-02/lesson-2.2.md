## Summary (from curriculum)
This lesson examines the roles of logistics intermediaries and transportation service providers involved in coordinating and moving cargo. Learners will understand how freight forwarders, carriers, shipping lines, and different types of agents facilitate domestic and international shipments, including their responsibilities and relationships with importers, exporters, and other stakeholders.

## Learning Objectives (from curriculum)
Define freight forwarders, carriers, shipping lines, and shipping agents.
Explain the responsibilities of freight forwarders in arranging cargo transportation.
Distinguish between freight forwarders and carriers.
Describe the role of shipping lines in international maritime transportation.
Identify the functions of shipping agents and other logistics intermediaries.
Explain how logistics service providers coordinate to facilitate cargo movement.
Recognize the importance of communication and documentation between logistics stakeholders.

## Key Terms (from curriculum)
Freight Forwarder :: An individual or company that organises and coordinates the transportation of goods on behalf of shippers.
Carrier :: A person or company responsible for transporting goods or passengers under a transport agreement.
Shipping Line :: A company that operates or commercially manages vessels providing maritime cargo transportation services.
Shipping Agent :: A person or company appointed to represent a shipping line or vessel operator at a port or within a designated area.
Shipper :: The party that tenders goods for transportation, often identified as the consignor in shipping documents.
Consignee :: The person or organisation designated to receive goods under a transport document.
Logistics Intermediary :: A service provider that facilitates logistics transactions between cargo owners, transport operators, and other participants.
Freight :: Goods transported commercially by road, rail, sea, or air.
