# Ghana Legal Anchors — Verified Fact Sheet

Last verified: 2026-11-19

Purpose: a source-checked reference for curriculum writers on the legal anchors the
curriculum cites — Act 891's key provisions quoted, the unapplied amendments that
motivate the spec's C4.4 amounts ban, the Public Health Act 851 provisions behind
the FDA/GSA controls, and the subsidiary regulations the decks name. All Act 891
quotes are from the supplied text (`Customs Act 2015 (2).pdf`, a By Laws.Africa
consolidation, CC-BY licensed) — see section 1 for what that source is and is not.
Items that could not be confirmed are in the final **DO NOT CLAIM** section.

---

## 1. The Act 891 source and its consolidation status

Verified facts:

- The supplied Act is a consolidation "with improved formatting and with minor
  typographical errors corrected", whose front matter states: "There are
  outstanding amendments that have not yet been applied: Act 923 of 2016, Act 949
  of 2017, Act 957 of 2017, Act 1014 of 2020, Act 1083 of 2022." (source: ACT front
  matter, quoted)
- The consolidation is published by "By Laws.Africa and contributors. Licensed
  under CC-BY. Share widely and freely." (page footers), and notes that "A scan of
  the original gazette of each piece of legislation (including amendments) is
  available" (front matter).
- Consequence for the curriculum (spec C4.4): the text is Act 891 as at 18 May 2015
  plus formatting corrections; the five outstanding amendments are exactly the
  enactments that move monetary penalties — so state the offence and the mechanism,
  never a fine amount, unless the figure is verified against the amending Act or
  the current schedule with the citation.
- The Act has 152 sections; s.151 carries the Interpretation definitions (65 of
  them, extracted in `tmp/act_reference.md`); s.152 carries the repeal list.
  (source: ACT structure — verified programmatically during the brief wave)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Act 891's consolidated text carries a warning the curriculum must respect: five
  amending Acts (923/2016, 949/2017, 957/2017, 1014/2020, 1083/2022) are
  outstanding and not applied — so the penalties the Act prints may not be the
  penalties in force."

## 2. The record-keeping duty — section 9

Verified facts (quoted from ACT):

- s.9(1): "An owner, importer, consignee, exporter, entry filer or any other person
  required to keep records under this Act, shall maintain the records in their
  original form, which includes the electronic form, unless the
  Commissioner-General approves an alternative method of storage."
- s.9(2): the persons listed must produce the records for examination, inspection
  and audit by the Authority — including the person who "is an agent of a person
  described in paragraph (a) or (b), including a customs house agent" (s.9(2)(c))
  and the person "whose activity requires the filing of a declaration" (s.9(2)(d)).
- s.9(3): records to be maintained include records required in connection with the
  importation or exportation of goods; transportation under bond or storage;
  the collection or payment of duty; drawback and refund claims; "a certificate of
  origin required for a claim for preferential tariff treatment" (s.9(3)(f)); and
  "any other records required in connection with the conduct of customs business".
- s.9(4): the person shall comply with the requirements for making the records
  available and answering questions.
- s.9(5): "A person who is required to keep and render a record for examination,
  inspection or audit by the Authority shall keep the record for six years from the
  date of the activity that required creation of that record."
- s.9(6): where a signature or record is to be notarized, acknowledged, verified,
  sealed or made under oath, the requirement is satisfied if the electronic
  signature is appended.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Section 9 keeps the records whole: original form including electronic, six
  years' retention from the activity's date, production for examination, inspection
  and audit — and the customs house agent is squarely inside the duty (s.9(2)(c))."

## 3. The advance ruling — section 12

Verified facts (quoted from ACT):

- s.12(1): "The Commissioner-General may issue a written ruling applying the
  customs law to a particular set of facts submitted by an interested party."
- s.12(2): an interested party includes a person or authorised agent with "a
  demonstrable interest in the questions presented in the request", and an
  importer or exporter.
- s.12(3): "The ruling may relate to the tariff classification, customs value,
  country of origin of the goods or to any other activity to which the customs law
  applies."
- s.12(5): the request shall be in writing and include a statement of all relevant
  facts; the names and addresses of interested parties; the name of the port where
  the goods are expected to arrive or depart; and a description of the transaction
  in sufficient detail to allow the application of the customs laws.
- s.12(6): a classification request adds "a complete description of the
  merchandise including" — the packing weight, the chemical analysis, the
  description of the goods, the production and expiration date, the name and brand,
  the physical description, the function, the composition, the characteristics of
  components — plus "(b) the commercial or technical designation of the goods; and
  (c) where the goods consist of more than one material, the composition of the
  goods by weight, volume and value of each component." (The Act's text prints
  "(ix)" after "(vii)" with no "(viii)" and annotates: "[Please note: Numbering as
  in original.]" — quote the list, not the numbering.)
- s.12(7) begins the valuation-request additions (the drafts cite the subsection's
  existence; the valuation lesson works the classification list).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Section 12's advance ruling covers classification, value and origin; the
  classification request's description list is the curriculum's model of
  completeness (packing weight, chemical analysis, function, composition …)."

## 4. Objection — section 13

Verified facts (quoted from ACT):

- s.13(1): a person may object to the Commissioner-General's decisions:
  "(a) the assessment of duty, including underlying findings and determinations as
  to (i) the customs value of the goods; (ii) the tariff classification of the
  goods; (iii) the country of origin of the goods; (iv) the rate and amount of duty
  assessed; and (v) the person liable to pay the amount charged or the amount of
  that person's liability; (b) the exclusion of goods from the country as
  prohibited goods; (c) the refusal to pay a claim for drawback; (d) the refusal to
  refund or remit duty; or (e) a customs advance ruling which is binding."
- s.13(2): who may object — the importer or exporter; a person whose liability or
  refund right is affected; a person filing a drawback or refund claim; or their
  authorised agent.
- s.13(3): an objection shall include the name and address of the filer; the number
  and date of the entry; the date and nature of the decision; and a description of
  the merchandise.
- s.13(4): "The procedure for filing an objection shall be in accordance with the
  law governing revenue administration in the country." — the objections procedure
  lives in the revenue-administration law, not in Act 891.

Not verified in this run: the objection's time limit (it lives in the
revenue-administration law the subsection defers to — do not state a number of
days).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Section 13's objection reaches the assessment's underlying findings — value,
  classification, origin, rate, liability — and the prohibited-goods exclusion; the
  procedure itself is deferred to the revenue-administration law (s.13(4))."

## 5. The fourteen-day and warehouse clocks

Verified facts (quoted from ACT):

- s.4(3) (customs-controlled areas): "Where goods remain in a customs-controlled
  area for more than fourteen days after being (a) entered under another customs
  procedure, (b) entered for export, or (c) sold by public auction under this Act,
  the goods are liable to forfeiture to the State unless the failure to move the
  goods is explained to the satisfaction of the Commissioner-General."
- s.51 (missing or incomplete documents): a provisional declaration is perfected by
  a supplementary declaration "within four working days" (s.51(2)); the deposit
  made under s.51(1) is forfeited where the person "does not produce the documents
  or declarations required by the Commissioner-General within fourteen days of the
  time of deposit, or a further period as the Commissioner-General may allow"
  (s.51(3)).
- s.52 (uncleared goods): goods "not entered within seven days after being
  unloaded or within a further period as the proper officer may in special
  circumstances allow" may be deposited in a State warehouse.
- s.53(1): goods not delivered from a customs-controlled area within seven days
  after final discharge (or a further allowed period) may be deposited in a State
  warehouse "for a period of not more than fourteen days"; s.53(2): State
  warehouse goods "attract rent and other charges as prescribed"; s.53(3):
  perishables may be auctioned immediately, non-perishables per the prescribed
  directives.
- s.81(3) (goods not duly removed — private bonded warehouse): "Where the goods
  remain in a private bonded warehouse for more than fourteen days after being
  (a) entered under another customs procedure, (b) entered for export, or (c) sold
  by public auction under this Act, the goods are liable to forfeiture to the
  State unless the failure to move the goods is explained to the satisfaction of
  the Commissioner-General." s.81(2): the person in charge who fails to produce
  goods on request "incurs a penalty of three hundred percent of the duty payable
  on the goods not so produced, in addition to the duty due" — statutory text,
  quoteable with the section.
- s.126(4) (disposal of goods): non-perishable goods in the Commissioner-General's
  custody may be sold "after fourteen days notice published in the Gazette or at
  least two daily newspapers of national circulation".

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The Act's clocks: fourteen days in a customs-controlled area or a private
  bonded warehouse and the goods are liable to forfeiture (ss.4(3), 81(3));
  seven days uncleared and the goods go to the State warehouse (ss.52-53);
  fourteen days' notice before the auction (s.126(4))."

## 6. The repealed enactments and the CHA licensing line

Verified facts (quoted from ACT):

- s.152's repealed list ends with: "Customs House Agents (Licensing) Act, 1978
  (S.M.C.D.188)" — the CHA licensing function now lives in Act 891's own s.43.
  (see the institutions sheet's section 6 for the s.43 quote set)
- The repeal list also includes the CEPS amendment Acts (Act 614 of 2002, Act 634
  of 2002, Act 636 of 2003, Act 758 of 2008) among others.
- s.151's "customs house agent": "a person who is granted a licence to carry out
  customs business" (the Act's text prints "customs businesss" — normalised in the
  curriculum, documented in the briefs' fact-check cautions).

## 7. Public Health Act, 2012 (Act 851) — the FDA's gates

Verified facts (quoted from the supplied PHA text
`Public-Health-Act-851 (1).pdf`):

- s.80(1): "There is hereby established a body corporate to be known as the Food
  and Drugs Authority." s.81: the object — "to provide and enforce standards for
  the sale of food, herbal medicinal products, cosmetics, drugs, medical devices
  and household chemical substances." s.82: the functions — ensure adequate and
  effective standards for food, drugs, cosmetics, household chemicals and medical
  devices; monitor through the District Assemblies and any other agency of State;
  advise the Minister; approve the initiation and conduct of clinical trials; and
  perform ancillary functions. (The FDA deck summarises these as its "Mandate and
  Functions" from "Parts 6, 7 and 8" of the Act — the deck's framing.)
- s.97(1) (registration of food): "A person shall not manufacture, import, export,
  distribute, sell or supply food or expose food for sale unless the Authority has
  registered the food." s.97(2) preserves sample importation for registration
  purposes; s.97(3)-(6) set the application and registration machinery.
- s.118(1) (registration of drugs, herbal medicinal products, medical devices or
  household chemical substances): "A person shall not manufacture, prepare, import,
  export, distribute, sell, supply or exhibit for sale a drug, herbal medicinal
  product, cosmetic, medical device or household chemical substance unless the
  article has been registered by the Authority." s.118(2)-(4): the personal-use
  import-permit carve-out, the registration-sample carve-out, and the conditional
  registration power.
- Part structure in the supplied text: Part Six — Tobacco Control Measures; Part
  Seven — Food and Drugs (ss.80 onward); Part Eight — Clinical Trials.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The FDA's import gates are registration gates: no food without registration
  (PHA s.97(1)), no drug, herbal medicinal product, cosmetic, medical device or
  household chemical substance without registration (PHA s.118(1))."

## 8. The subsidiary regulations the decks name

Verified facts (all as the decks state them — the regulations' full texts were not
read; quote the deck's statement of what each does):

- LI 1541:1992 — "the General Labelling Rules" (GSA deck: "STANDARDS BOARD GENERAL
  LABELLING RULES, 1992 (L.I.1541)"; GSA_NOTES: "AUTHORITY GENERAL LABELING RULES,
  1992 (L.I.1541)"). The minimum label information the decks list: name of product;
  instructions or directions for use / warnings; name and address of manufacturer,
  producer, distributor; country of origin; date of manufacture (the GSA deck's
  slide adds expiry date and batch/lot number to the list); batch/lot number.
  Language: "Label information must be in English. If not, the client pays a
  penalty; the consignment is detained in the warehouse and must be relabelled with
  English translations." (GSA deck slide 35; GSA_NOTES pages 27-28)
- LI 1586:1994 — second-hand items prohibition: the GSA deck's list — handkerchiefs,
  underpants, mattresses, sanitary wares. (GSA deck slide 36; GSA_NOTES page 29)
- LI 1932:2008 — prohibited products: the GSA deck's list — incandescent lamps,
  used refrigerators & freezers, used air-conditioners. (GSA deck slide 36;
  GSA_NOTES page 29)
- LI 1693:2001 — prohibited cylinders: used LPG cylinders. (GSA deck slide 36 — a
  fourth instrument beyond the three the PLAN names)
- NRCD 173 — the Standards Decree, 1973: the GSA deck's timeline names it as the
  instrument that reconstituted the National Standards Board as the Ghana
  Standards Board in 1973. (GSA deck slide 4)
- NRCD 326 — the Weights & Measures Decree, 1975: the deck's timeline names it as
  the legal-metrology instrument the Board became custodian of in 1975. (GSA deck
  slide 4)
- Ghana Standards Authority Act, 2022 (Act 1078) s.54(1)(c) — pattern approval:
  "A person shall not use for trade, industry, health, safety/environment a
  weight, measure or instrument which has not been Pattern approved" (GSA deck
  slide 38's quotation; not verified against the Act 1078 full text).

Not verified: the full text of any LI or NRCD; whether the decks' lists are the
prohibitions' complete scope; the penalty the LI 1541 language breach attracts
(the deck says "a penalty" without an amount — state the mechanism, not an
amount).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The labelling rule (LI 1541:1992) sets the minimum label fields and requires
  English; the prohibition instruments (LI 1586:1994, LI 1932:2008, LI 1693:2001)
  keep second-hand clothing articles, used refrigerators and air-conditioners and
  used LPG cylinders out of Ghana; the pattern-approval gate (Act 1078, s.54(1)(c))
  covers weighing and measuring instruments."

## DO NOT CLAIM

The following were NOT verified and must not appear in learner-facing material
without fresh sourcing:

- Any fine, penalty or fee amount other than figures that appear in Act 891's own
  text quoted with the section (s.81(2)'s three hundred percent is the current
  example) — the five outstanding amendments (Act 923/2016, 949/2017, 957/2017,
  1014/2020, 1083/2022) are exactly the instruments that move these figures.
- The objection's time limit in days (s.13(4) defers it to the
  revenue-administration law, which was not supplied).
- The full text of LI 1541, LI 1586, LI 1932, LI 1693, NRCD 173 or NRCD 326 — the
  decks' statements of what each does are the anchor; the regulations' own words
  were not read.
- The prohibitions' complete scope beyond the decks' lists (LI 1586 and LI 1932
  list more items than the decks show).
- The penalty amount for the LI 1541 English-language breach (the deck says
  "a penalty" — no amount).
- Act 1078's provisions beyond s.54(1)(c) as the deck quotes it; Act 1078's
  establishment machinery.
- Any claim that the consolidated Act 891 text reflects the amending Acts — the
  front matter says they are not applied.
- "Ghana was the first nation to sign and ratify the AfCFTA" — recorded in the
  instruments sheet as a claimed, not asserted, fact.
