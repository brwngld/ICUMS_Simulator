# Ghana Customs Institutions — Verified Fact Sheet

Last verified: 2026-11-19

Purpose: a source-checked reference for curriculum writers on **which Ghanaian body
does what** in the customs chain. Two kinds of source are used, and each fact below
says which: (a) the training decks supplied for the course (quoted with the deck
name), and (b) pages verified online (with the URL and the verification date).
Institutional facts are governed by spec C4.5: they come only from an anchor, this
sheet, or a verified authoritative source — never from memory. Items that could not
be confirmed are listed in the final **DO NOT CLAIM** section and must not appear in
learner-facing material.

---

## 1. The Ghana Revenue Authority and its Customs Division

Verified facts:

- The Customs Division of the GRA has a three-fold responsibility: mobilisation of
  revenue from imports and exports, facilitation of legitimate trade, and provision
  of public security and safety. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority,
  https://gra.gov.gh/customs/ — verified 2026-10, carried forward unchanged
  2026-11-19)
- Customs operates as a "first line of defence" protecting Ghana's territorial
  integrity through preventive operations at approved and unapproved borders, and
  operates at harbours, airports, land borders and parcel posts. (source: Ghana
  Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/)
- Act 891 assigns the customs functions to the Commissioner-General: declarations
  are made to the Commissioner-General (ss.34-40), licences are granted by the
  Commissioner-General (s.43), advance rulings issue from the Commissioner-General
  (s.12), and objections lie against decisions of the Commissioner-General (s.13).
  (source: ACT — Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891), throughout)
- The Act's repealed-enactments list ends with the Customs House Agents (Licensing)
  Act, 1978 (S.M.C.D.188) — the CHA licensing function now lives in Act 891 itself,
  section 43. (source: ACT, s.152)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "GRA Customs Division collects revenue on imports and exports, facilitates
  legitimate trade, and protects Ghana's security at harbours, airports, land
  borders and parcel posts."
- "Act 891 places the customs decisions — entry, licence, ruling, objection — with
  the Commissioner-General."

## 2. Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA)

Verified facts:

- "Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA) is a statutory corporation established
  under Ghana's Provisional National Defence Council Law (PNDCL 160) of 1986 to
  build, plan, develop, manage, maintain, operate and control all ports in Ghana."
  (source: CPC2026 Role of Port Operators (GPHA) — Sea deck, slide 5, quoted)
- "The main responsibility of the Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA) is to
  build, plan, develop, manage, maintain, operate, and control all designated public
  ports and harbours in Ghana." (source: GPHA deck, slide 6, quoted)
- The Authority manages and operates Ghana's sea ports and various business units
  "in collaboration with a number of private service providers in the areas of
  vessel handling, stevedoring, transfer, storage, receipt and delivery of
  containerized and general cargo", plus safety, security and conservancy services.
  (source: GPHA deck, slide 5)
- "The Port of Tema and the Port of Takoradi facilitate the import of essential
  goods like petroleum, machinery, and vehicles, alongside the export of major
  commodities including cocoa, gold, manganese, and bauxite" and "Seaports in Ghana
  handle over 80% of the country's international trade". (source: GPHA deck,
  slide 3, quoted)
- GPHA manages the Port of Tema and the Port of Takoradi, plus a fishing harbour at
  Tema. (source: Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority, https://ghanaports.gov.gh/ —
  verified 2026-10, carried forward unchanged 2026-11-19)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority — a statutory corporation established
  under PNDCL 160 of 1986 — builds, develops, manages and controls Ghana's public
  ports and harbours."
- "Ghana's two seaports — Tema and Takoradi — are managed by GPHA, and seaports
  handle over 80% of Ghana's international trade (the training deck's figure)."

## 3. Port operators, terminal operators and the port's stakeholders

Verified facts (the deck's teaching frame — these are roles, not named companies):

- "A port operator is a company or entity responsible for managing the day-to-day
  activities at a port terminal. This includes handling cargo loading and
  unloading, operating cranes and other equipment, managing storage facilities, and
  coordinating logistics with shipping lines and transportation providers." Port
  operators "often lease terminal space from port authorities". (source: GPHA deck,
  slide 12, quoted)
- The deck's stakeholders list at a port: port authorities (governance,
  infrastructure, regulatory compliance), shipping lines (operate vessels),
  terminal operators (handle and store cargo at terminals), freight forwarders
  (coordinate movement across modes), customs agencies (enforce import/export
  regulations), logistics providers, importers/exporters (own the cargo),
  stevedores (physically load and unload). (source: GPHA deck, slide 15)
- Public versus private operators: public operators are government-owned and focus
  on public interest and trade facilitation; private operators are commercial
  enterprises that fund projects with private capital and lease terminal space.
  (source: GPHA deck, slides 12-14)

Not verified: the names of any specific terminal operator at Tema or Takoradi. The
deck teaches the roles; no company name is attached to any role.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Terminal operators run the day-to-day cargo handling at the berths, leasing
  terminal space from the port authority; customs clears the goods, the port and
  terminal operators move and store them."

## 4. Food and Drugs Authority (FDA)

Verified facts:

- The FDA deck states: "The Food and Drugs Authority (FDA) draws its mandate from
  Parts 6, 7 and 8 of the Public Health Act, 2012 (Act 851)." (source: CPC2026 MDA —
  FDA deck, slide 3)
- PHA s.80 establishes "a body corporate to be known as the Food and Drugs
  Authority"; s.81 sets its object ("to provide and enforce standards for the sale
  of food, herbal medicinal products, cosmetics, drugs, medical devices and
  household chemical substances"); s.82 lists the functions (ensure adequate and
  effective standards; monitor compliance through the District Assemblies; advise
  the Minister). (source: Public Health Act, 2012 (Act 851), ss.80-82 — verified
  word-for-word against the supplied Act text)
- PHA s.97(1): "A person shall not manufacture, import, export, distribute, sell or
  supply food or expose food for sale unless the Authority has registered the
  food." (source: PHA 851 s.97(1), verified word-for-word; the FDA deck quotes the
  same gate in its slide 5)
- PHA s.118(1): "A person shall not manufacture, prepare, import, export,
  distribute, sell, supply or exhibit for sale a drug, herbal medicinal product,
  cosmetic, medical device or household chemical substance unless the article has
  been registered by the Authority." (source: PHA 851 s.118(1), verified
  word-for-word; the FDA deck quotes the same gate)
- The FDA's import/export work sits with its Centre for Import and Export Control
  (CIEC), per the deck. (source: CPC2026 MDA — FDA deck, slide 5)
- Note the deck's s.97(1) paraphrase omits "sell or" in the verb list; the Act text
  above governs.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The FDA's mandate comes from the Public Health Act, 2012 (Act 851); no food, drug,
  cosmetic, medical device or household chemical substance may be imported unless it
  is registered by the Authority (ss.97 and 118)."

## 5. Ghana Standards Authority (GSA)

Verified facts:

- The GSA's timeline, from the deck: established 1967 as the National Standards
  Board; reconstituted 1973 as the Ghana Standards Board by the Standards Decree,
  NRCD 173; made custodian in 1975 of the Weights & Measures Decree, 1975 (NRCD
  326) — legal metrology; renamed the Ghana Standards Authority (GSA) in 2011;
  governed since 2022 by the Ghana Standards Authority Act, 2022 (Act 1078).
  (source: CPC2026 MDA — GSA deck, slide 4 timeline)
- The deck's GSA mandate: Ghana's national body for standards and "a critical
  pillar of the NQI" (national quality infrastructure), with a mandate at the ports
  and borders for import inspection, conformity assessment, certificates of
  conformity and analysis, labelling, and weighing-and-measuring instruments.
  (source: GSA deck, slides 2-3 objectives)
- Act 1078 s.54(1)(c): "A person shall not use for trade, industry, health,
  safety/environment a weight, measure or instrument which has not been Pattern
  approved" — and the deck states it is a statutory offence to import any weighing
  or measuring instrument into Ghana without a valid Pattern Approval Certificate.
  (source: GSA deck, slide 38, quoting Act 1078 s.54(1)(c))
- The labelling gate: "Every regulated product must carry a compliant label. The
  General Labelling Rule (LI 1541) sets out the minimum information a label must
  display." (source: GSA deck, slide 35 — see the legal-anchors sheet for the six
  fields)

Not verified against the Act 1078 full text: the exact wording of s.54(1)(c) beyond
the deck's quotation; Act 1078's establishment provisions. The deck's quote is the
anchor.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The GSA — Ghana's national standards body, now under the Ghana Standards
  Authority Act, 2022 (Act 1078) — inspects regulated imports, enforces the
  labelling rule, and pattern-approves weighing and measuring instruments used in
  trade."

## 6. The customs house agent licensing route

Verified facts (all from ACT — Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891)):

- Definition: "'customs house agent' means a person who is granted a licence to
  carry out customs business" (s.151; the Act's text prints "customs businesss"
  with three s's — normalised in the curriculum).
- The licence gate (s.43(1)): "A company or partnership shall not engage in the
  business of customs house agent unless that company or partnership is wholly
  owned by an indigenous Ghanaian and has been granted a licence by the
  Commissioner-General."
- The grant conditions (s.43(2)): the company's regulations must authorise the
  business and at least one director or employee must hold "a certificate of
  proficiency in customs business"; the same test for a partnership's agreement and
  a partner or employee. A statutory body or State institution may be licensed
  where an employee holds the certificate (s.43(3)).
- Fees and validity: the licensee pays the prescribed fees (s.43(4)); "A customs
  house agent licence is valid for one year and may be renewed on payment of the
  prescribed fees" (s.43(5)).
- Suspension or revocation: the Commissioner-General may suspend or revoke the
  licence where the licensee breaches the licence's conditions or the Act's
  provisions (s.43(6)).
- The authority rule (s.44(1)): where a person applies on behalf of another to
  transact customs business, "the Commissioner-General may require the applicant to
  produce a written authority from the person on whose behalf" they act.
- The Act's repealed list ends with the Customs House Agents (Licensing) Act, 1978
  (S.M.C.D.188) — replaced by Act 891's own licensing scheme (s.152).
- In the declaration: "Where a customs house agent acts on behalf of a person, the
  agent shall indicate in the declaration, the person on whose behalf the
  declaration is made and the agent shall be taken as the declarant." (source: ACT,
  s.49(4), quoted)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "A customs house agent is a company wholly owned by an indigenous Ghanaian,
  licensed by the Commissioner-General, with at least one director or employee
  holding a certificate of proficiency in customs business; the licence runs one
  year and is renewed on payment of the prescribed fees (Act 891, s.43)."

## 7. The Ghana Shippers' Authority and GIFF

Verified facts (carried from the verified Logistics sheet, 2026-10):

- The Ghana Shippers' Authority is an active trade-facilitation stakeholder; its
  site links to regulatory agencies and reports collaboration with GSA and the
  Environmental Protection Authority on permit matters. (source:
  https://shippers.org.gh/)
- The Ghana Institute of Freight Forwarders (GIFF) appears on the Shippers'
  Authority "Useful Links" list (ghanafreightforwarders.org), confirming it exists
  as a recognised Ghanaian freight-forwarding institution. (source:
  https://shippers.org.gh/)

Not verified: GIFF's status as "the professional body" (its own site unfetched);
the Shippers' Authority's statutory basis.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The Ghana Institute of Freight Forwarders (GIFF) is a recognised Ghanaian
  institution for the freight-forwarding profession."

## 8. The Ministry of Trade and the import-permit frame

Verified facts (carried from the verified Logistics sheet, 2026-10):

- Ghana's import procedure requires permits "from any of the regulatory agencies,
  dependent on the type of goods", with FDA and GSA explicitly named on the GRA
  import-procedures page. (source: https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- "The Import Declaration Form (IDF) is obtained from the Ministry of Trade."
  (source: https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- The Shippers' Authority news item refers to the Environmental Protection
  Authority (EPA) working with GSA on permit delays — EPA exists as a permit-
  relevant body, but no specific import function is attributed to it beyond the
  simulator's modelling. (source: https://shippers.org.gh/)

Not verified individually: the Plant Protection and Regulatory Services
Directorate (MoFA), Veterinary Services Directorate, National Petroleum Authority,
Energy Commission, Fisheries Commission, and Ghana Export Promotion Authority as
permit agencies. The general "regulatory agencies" statement covers the concept;
do not attribute specific functions to these named bodies.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Depending on the goods, an importer may need permits from regulatory agencies
  such as the FDA for food and medicines, or the GSA for standards conformity."
- "An Import Declaration Form (IDF) is obtained from the Ministry of Trade."

## 9. Who does what at clearance (the deck's division of labour)

Verified facts:

- The port deck's clearance frame: clearance begins with submission of shipping
  documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, permits) to customs;
  customs reviews documents to verify nature, value and classification; physical
  inspection where necessary; duties, taxes and fees calculated and paid before
  release; then customs grants clearance. (source: GPHA deck, slides 16-17)
- The GRA import-procedures frame: the declaration is submitted in ICUMS by the
  clearing agent; ICUMS generates the tax bill; the importer pays at participating
  banks. (source: https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- The Act's frame: the declarant makes the declaration (s.34's "person" the Act
  defines), the agent lodges on written authority (ss.43-44), the proper officer
  examines and releases, the Commissioner-General decides (s.151's definitions and
  the procedures Part).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Customs decides, the port moves: documents are lodged with customs by the agent,
  duties are assessed and paid, and the goods are released for delivery — while the
  port authority and terminal operators handle the vessel, the cargo and the yard."

## DO NOT CLAIM

The following were NOT verified and must not appear in learner-facing material
without fresh sourcing:

- The acronyms "GPA", "MPA" or "GTA" for Ghanaian port or trade bodies — they
  appear in no supplied source. Ghana's port body is GPHA; do not invent variants.
- Meridian Port Services (MPS) as Tema's container-terminal operator; Terminal 3
  as a deep-water terminal opened in 2019; Tema as "Ghana's main container
  gateway"; Takoradi as "one of Ghana's oldest ports" or opened in 1928 (carried
  from the Logistics sheet's DO NOT CLAIM).
- Any named terminal operator at Tema or Takoradi.
- GIFF as "the professional body" for freight forwarders.
- Specific import functions for: Plant Protection and Regulatory Services
  Directorate (MoFA), Veterinary Services Directorate, National Petroleum
  Authority, Energy Commission, Fisheries Commission, Ghana Export Promotion
  Authority.
- The Shippers' Authority's statutory basis; the Single Window's institutional
  history (2015 launch, 2022 transfer — see the electronic-systems sheet).
- Any duty, levy or fee rate, any penalty amount, any free-time period. The 300
  percent figure at Act s.81(2) is statutory text and may be quoted WITH the
  section; no other figure without the same treatment.
- The FDA's "Parts 6, 7 and 8" claim extended beyond what the deck states: Part
  Six of PHA 851 is Tobacco Control, Part Seven is Food and Drugs, Part Eight is
  Clinical Trials — teach the registration gates (ss.97, 118) rather than the
  part-level mandate without the deck's framing.
- Any licence fee amount under s.43(4) (the Act says "prescribed fees"; the
  prescription lives in Regulations not supplied).
