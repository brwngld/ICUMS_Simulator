# Customs Instruments and Standards — Verified Fact Sheet

Last verified: 2026-11-19

Purpose: a source-checked reference for curriculum writers on the international
instruments the customs curriculum names — the Harmonised System and its
Convention, the GRI, the Revised Kyoto Convention, the WTO Trade Facilitation
Agreement, Incoterms 2020, the ECOWAS Common External Tariff and Ghana's TSN
layer, the TIR and ATA conventions, and the AfCFTA. Two kinds of source, each fact
says which: (a) the supplied anchors (ACT, the TARIFF workbook, the training
decks), and (b) pages verified online (with URL and verification date; the 2026-11-16
and 2026-11-17 verification runs were done during the M1/M9/M7 draft waves and are
carried forward). Governed by spec C4.7: instrument wording is matched exactly,
never paraphrased from memory. Items that could not be confirmed are in the final
**DO NOT CLAIM** section.

---

## 1. The Harmonised System and its Convention

Verified facts:

- Act 891's definition (s.151, quoted): "'Harmonised Commodity Description and
  Coding System' means the nomenclature comprising the headings, sub-headings and
  their related numerical codes, the section, chapter and sub-heading notes and the
  general rules for the interpretation of the Harmonised System contained in the
  text of the International Convention on the Harmonised Commodity Description and
  Coding System approved by the Customs Co-operation Council on 14th June, 1983 and
  the relevant Schedules attached to it" — the Act's own text carries the
  Convention date inside the definition (the Act spells "Harmonised"; the Act's
  s.151 definitions vary straight and curly quotes between entries).
- The Convention: "The International Convention on the Harmonized Commodity
  Description and Coding System (HS Convention) entered into force on 1 January
  1988", concluded at Brussels on 14 June 1983; "the Harmonized System it
  establishes is administered by the World Customs Organization". (source: WCO,
  https://www.wcoomd.org/en/topics/nomenclature/instrument-and-tools/hs_convention.aspx
  — verified 2026-11-19)
- The HS comprises more than 5,000 commodity groups, each identified by a six-digit
  code, arranged in a legal and logical structure. (source: WCO, "What is the
  Harmonized System?" — verified 2026-11-19)
- The HS is revised roughly every five years by the WCO's Harmonized System
  Committee. (source: WCO/CSL Globe summary — verified 2026-11-19; the ~5-year
  cadence is the claim, not a dated schedule)
- Duty link in Ghana: duty is imposed at the rates "prescribed under the
  Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System" as s.1(1) provides, with the
  nomenclature incorporated by s.151's definition. (source: ACT, ss.1(1), 151)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana's tariff descends from the Harmonised System — the WCO's six-digit
  nomenclature, established by the 1983 Convention and in force since 1988 — which
  Act 891 incorporates by definition (s.151) and s.1(1) makes the duty's basis."

## 2. The General Rules for the Interpretation (GRI)

Verified facts:

- GRI 1, as quoted from the WCO's published text during the M7 wave (verified
  2026-11-17): the titles of sections, chapters and sub-chapters are provided for
  ease of reference only; for legal purposes, classification shall be determined
  according to the terms of the headings and any relative section or chapter notes
  and, provided such headings or notes do not otherwise require, according to the
  following provisions. (source: WCO published GRI text — verified 2026-11-17,
  wcoomd.org tariff teaching text)
- The GRI are part of the incorporated nomenclature — s.151's definition names "the
  general rules for the interpretation of the Harmonised System" inside the
  Harmonised System's meaning. (source: ACT, s.151)
- The rules' method as taught from the Act's incorporation: GRI 1 (headings and
  notes decide; titles are signposts); GRI 2 (the incomplete, the unassembled, the
  mixed — limbs (a) and (b), with 3(b) the bridge); GRI 3 (the ordered steps —
  specificity, essential character, last-in-order); GRI 6 (same-level comparison
  in the subheading descent). (source: the drafts' M7 teaching, anchored on the
  verified GRI 1 text and the Act's incorporation)

Not verified verbatim in this run: the full official wording of GRI 2-6 beyond the
characterisations above. Quote GRI 1 verbatim; state GRI 2-6 at the characterised
level, or re-verify against the WCO text before quoting.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "GRI 1 makes the headings and the section and chapter notes the legal test — the
  titles are signposts only (WCO text; incorporated by Act 891, s.151)."

## 3. The Revised Kyoto Convention (RKC)

Verified facts:

- Ghana acceded to the Revised Kyoto Convention on 29 June 2019, as recorded on
  the WCO's official list of contracting parties to the RKC. (source: WCO
  contracting-parties list — WEB-verified 2026-11-16 during the M1 wave)
- The RKC is the World Customs Organization's modernised framework for simplified
  and effective customs procedures. (source: WCO — WEB-verified 2026-11-16)
- Route of application: the RKC applies through Ghana's own accession — Act 891's
  text does not incorporate it the way it incorporates the Harmonised System.
  (source: ACT vs. WCO record — the drafts' M1 teaching)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana acceded to the Revised Kyoto Convention on 29 June 2019 (WCO
  contracting-parties list) — joined outside the Act, not incorporated by it."

## 4. The WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA)

Verified facts:

- Ghana accepted the WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement on 4 January 2017; the TFA
  entered into force generally on 22 February 2017. (source: WTO TFA facility —
  WEB-verified 2026-11-16 during the M1 wave)
- Route of application: like the RKC, the TFA binds through its own acceptance,
  not through Act 891's text. (source: ACT vs. WTO record)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana accepted the WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement on 4 January 2017, and the
  TFA entered into force on 22 February 2017 (WTO TFA facility)."

## 5. The ECOWAS Common External Tariff and Ghana's TSN layer

Verified facts:

- Ghana began implementing the ECOWAS Common External Tariff on 1 February 2016,
  moving the tariff from four bands to five: 0 percent for essential social goods,
  5 percent, 10 percent, 20 percent, and 35 percent for specific goods for economic
  development. (source: GRA/ECOWAS CET reporting — WEB-verified 2026-11-16 during
  the M1 wave)
- The supplied national schedule is the TARIFF workbook (`HS FINAL
  2017-15-506.xlsx`): 377 sheets, 5,886 TSN rows, columns Heading / TSN / Commodity
  Description / SU / CET ID / VAT / IE. The TSN is Ghana's eight-digit Tariff
  Subheading layer under the CET; the CET ID column ties the national row to the
  regional rate. (source: TARIFF workbook — validated programmatically 2026-11-02;
  see PLAN's source inventory)
- The worked example row verified in the M8 wave: Table 230, TSN 6907.21.00.00
  (ceramic tiles), CET 20, VAT 15 — used in the full-duty worked example with
  every figure labelled illustrative per C4.2-C4.3. (source: TARIFF workbook,
  Table 230)
- Rate caution (C4.2): the supplied schedule is the 2024 refresh of a 2017 base;
  rates stated from it must be labelled illustrative of the structure, verify the
  rate in force.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana implemented the ECOWAS Common External Tariff from 1 February 2016 with
  five bands (0, 5, 10, 20, 35 percent); the national TSN layer adds the
  eight-digit lines the customs declaration uses."

## 6. Incoterms 2020

Verified facts:

- "Incoterms® 2020" is published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC)
  and entered into force on 1 January 2020; it is the most recent edition of the
  rules. (source: ICC, https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/
  and /incoterms-2020 — verified 2026-11-19)
- Incoterms 2020 contains eleven terms, organised as seven rules for any mode of
  transport (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP) and four for sea and inland
  waterway transport (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF). (source: ICC — verified 2026-11-19;
  the deck's slide 16 shows the same split)
- The deck's definitions (CPC2026 Shipping and INCOTERMS deck, slides 17-37, each
  introduced with "officially means"): FAS — seller delivers when the goods are
  placed alongside the ship at the named port of shipment, risk transfers then;
  FOB — seller delivers on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named
  port of shipment, risk transfers on board; CFR — seller delivers on board,
  risk transfers on board, seller contracts and pays carriage to destination but
  owes no obligation to purchase insurance; CIF — as CFR plus the seller must
  contract for insurance; DDP — seller delivers cleared for import at the named
  place of destination, bearing all risks; DAP — seller delivers on the arriving
  means of transport ready for unloading at the named place; DPU — seller
  delivers after unloading at the named place — "the only Incoterms® rule that
  requires the seller to unload goods at destination"; EXW — seller places the
  goods at the buyer's disposal at a named place, with no obligation to load or
  clear for export; FCA — seller delivers on the buyer's means of transport at the
  seller's premises, or having reached the named other place ready for unloading;
  CPT — seller hands the goods to the carrier it contracted and must contract for
  carriage to destination; CIP — as CPT plus the insurance obligation.
  (source: CPC2026 Shipping and INCOTERMS deck, quoted)
- The deck's framing: "Incoterms are a set of internationally recognised and
  accepted rules covering the conditions of sale" (slide 15 region).
- The ICC's key 2020 change: DAT was renamed DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded).
  (source: ICC — verified 2026-11-19)

Not verified: whether the deck's definitions correspond to the ICC's exact
published wording (the deck paraphrases with "officially means"); the CIF
insurance-obligation nuance is flagged in the curriculum for the fact-checker (the
deck's CIF slide states the seller delivers on board and risk transfers on board,
and the drafts carry the insurance leg from the deck's own framing).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Incoterms 2020 — the ICC's rules in force since 1 January 2020 — set eleven
  terms: seven for any mode (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP) and four for sea
  (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF)."

## 7. The TIR and ATA conventions

Verified facts:

- Act 891 names the TIR Carnets convention and the ATA carnet and
  temporary-admission conventions in section 95(1)(b) and (c) for transit
  movements — the Act's own incorporation, distinct from the RKC/TFA route.
  (source: ACT, s.95(1)(b)-(c); the M1 drafts teach the two routes)

Not verified: Ghana's status under the TIR or ATA conventions beyond the Act's
naming of them; the conventions' full titles and administering bodies.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Act 891 itself names the TIR Carnets convention and the ATA carnet and
  temporary-admission conventions for transit movements (s.95(1)) — the Act's own
  incorporation, unlike the RKC and the TFA."

## 8. The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA)

Verified facts:

- The Agreement Establishing the AfCFTA was signed on 16 May 2018 in Kigali,
  Rwanda, together with its three protocols — on Trade in Goods, on Trade in
  Services, and on the Rules and Procedures on the Settlement of Disputes.
  (source: tralac AfCFTA legal texts — verified 2026-11-19,
  https://www.tralac.org/resources/our-resources/6730-continental-free-trade-area-cfta.html)
- Ghana signed and ratified in 2018, depositing its ratification with the AU in
  December 2018; Ghana's ratification was pivotal to reaching the entry-into-force
  threshold (May 2019). (source: trade.gov Ghana AfCFTA update and academic
  summary — verified 2026-11-19; "Ghana was the first nation to sign and ratify"
  appears in the academic source — treat "first" as claimed, not asserted)
- Trading under the AfCFTA officially commenced on 1 January 2021. (source:
  carried from the verified Logistics sheet, 2026-10; re-confirmed 2026-11-19)
- The AfCFTA's negotiations and implementation are overseen by a permanent
  secretariat based in Accra, Ghana. (source: carried from the verified Logistics
  sheet, 2026-10)
- As of the December 2025 update, 25 tariff schedules had been finalised under the
  AfCFTA, with early trade flows emerging. (source: trade.gov Ghana AfCFTA update,
  December 2025 — verified 2026-11-19; a time-bound figure, re-check before reuse)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The AfCFTA was signed at Kigali on 16 May 2018 with protocols on trade in
  goods, trade in services and dispute settlement; Ghana ratified in December
  2018; trading commenced on 1 January 2021, and the AfCFTA Secretariat sits in
  Accra."

## DO NOT CLAIM

The following were NOT verified and must not appear in learner-facing material
without fresh sourcing:

- The verbatim text of GRI 2-6 beyond the characterisations in section 2 — quote
  GRI 1 only, or re-verify against the WCO's published text first.
- The HS 2022 edition's specific changes, the current edition number in force for
  Ghana's schedule, or the next revision's date.
- WCO membership counts; the RKC's number of contracting parties; the RKC's body
  content (chapters, annexes, transitional standards) beyond the framework
  characterisation.
- Any CET rate for a specific good beyond the verified worked-example row (Table
  230, TSN 6907.21.00.00) — and that row is always labelled illustrative per
  C4.2-C4.3.
- The five CET bands mapped onto specific goods (which goods sit at 35 percent,
  for example) — the band structure is verified; the mapping is not.
- The ICC's exact published Incoterms definitions word-for-word (the deck
  paraphrases; quote the deck as the deck).
- Ghana's TIR or ATA convention membership status; the conventions' administering
  bodies.
- AfCFTA protocol details beyond the three signed protocols: ratification counts,
  the specific tariff-schedule lines Ghana offered, the Guided Trade Initiative's
  participants.
- Any date for the Ghana National Single Window's launch or transfer (see the
  electronic-systems sheet) — the instruments sheet makes no GNSW claims.
