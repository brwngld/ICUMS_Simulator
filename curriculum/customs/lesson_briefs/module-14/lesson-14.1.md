# LESSON BRIEF 14.1 — Records, audit trails and post-clearance audit

Course 5: Advanced Border Controls, Compliance and Professional Practice · difficulty `advanced`
Module 14: Advanced Compliance and Case Management · lesson 1 of 4 · module 14 of 15
Proposed slug: `records-audit-trails-and-post-clearance-audit`

## Summary

The declaration ends; the trail must not. Act 891 requires records to be kept (s.9),
lets the Authority be informed (s.11) and audits the clearance after release (s.7) —
so a mistake can be found, and corrected, after the goods are gone. This lesson
teaches the record-keeping duty, what an audit trail must contain, and how a
post-clearance audit runs.

## Learning Objectives

State the record-keeping duty (s.9): who keeps records, and the Act's definition of a record.
Explain the provision of information (s.11) as the authority's information right.
Describe the post-clearance audit (s.7): its purpose, its scope, and the records it consumes.
Build an audit trail from a clearance: declaration, documents, records — and what each proves later.

## Key Terms

record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data
electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means
electronic signature :: the electronic method of authentication prescribed by the Commissioner-General
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export

## Source anchors
