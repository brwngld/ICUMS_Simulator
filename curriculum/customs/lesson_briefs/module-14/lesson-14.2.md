# LESSON BRIEF 14.2 — Amendments, cancellations, offences and administrative penalties

Course 5: Advanced Border Controls, Compliance and Professional Practice · difficulty `advanced`
Module 14: Advanced Compliance and Case Management · lesson 2 of 4 · module 14 of 15
Proposed slug: `amendments-cancellations-offences-and-administrative-penalties`

## Summary

Declarations can be corrected — and punished. The Act's offence architecture spans
ss.121–143: penalties for false statements and misdeclarations, goods used contrary
to their authorised purpose, falsification, seizures and the offences that follow.
This lesson teaches the offence machinery and the correction routes — with the
spec's hardest rule enforced: penalty and fine amounts are never stated, because
Act 891 as supplied carries five unapplied amendments that move exactly those figures.

## Learning Objectives

Identify the declaration-correction routes the Act provides, and the difference between amending and cancelling.
Map the offence architecture: s.121 penalties, s.122 goods used contrary to authorised purpose, s.123 falsification/alteration/forgery, and the offences ss.127–138 carry.
Explain the false-statement definition (s.151) as the foundation of misdeclaration offences.
State the enforcement chain — seizure (s.124), claims (s.125), disposal (s.126) — without pricing any outcome.

## Key Terms

false statement :: a statement made with respect to goods subject to customs control that is false or misleading in a material respect or a material omission from a required statement or document with respect to goods subject to customs control
uncustomed goods :: goods liable to duty on which the full duties due have not been paid, and goods, whether liable to duty or not, that are imported or exported or in any way dealt with contrary to the provisions of this Act in relation to customs
prohibited goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` ss.121–143 — the offence and procedure machinery, each claim cited to its
  section: s.121 penalties; s.122 authorised purpose; s.123 falsification; s.124–126
  seizure/claims/disposal; s.127 prohibitions; ss.128–138 the border offences
  (including obstruction s.131, impersonation s.133, running goods s.134, concealment
  s.138); s.139 bribes; s.140 civil proceedings; s.142 procedure in small value
  cases; s.143 criminal proceedings.
- `ACT` front matter — the outstanding amendments (Act 923/2016, Act 949/2017,
  Act 957/2017, Act 1014/2020, Act 1083/2022), the reason penalty amounts are banned.

**Fact-check caution.** The C4.4 ban is absolute here: state the offence and the
mechanism; do not state a fine, penalty unit value or imprisonment term unless
verified against the amending Act or the current schedule, with the citation. Where
the draft needs the unapplied-amendments point, quote the front-matter note itself.
