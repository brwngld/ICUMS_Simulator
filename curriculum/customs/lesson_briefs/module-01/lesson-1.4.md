# LESSON BRIEF 1.4 — The import, export and transit clearance lifecycle

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 1: Customs Foundations and the Legal Mandate · lesson 4 of 4 · module 1 of 15
Proposed slug: `the-import-export-and-transit-clearance-lifecycle`

## Summary

Every consignment moves through the same legal sequence: the conveyance arrives and
reports; the goods are unloaded under supervision; somebody declares them; customs
examines or exempts them from examination; the Commissioner-General releases them; and
the goods are delivered. Exports have their own trigger — goods placed on board for
consignment outside the country — and goods can also move under transit or
transhipment without duty being paid, under bond, or by bonded carrier. This lesson
walks the learner through that sequence as Act 891 orders it, and ends where the
lifecycle ends when it breaks down: goods becoming uncleared and going to the State
warehouse.

## Learning Objectives

Sequence the import lifecycle as Act 891 provides it: arrival and report, unloading, entry and declaration, examination or exemption from examination, release, delivery.
State when importation legally begins ("time of import") and when an export legally occurs ("time of export", s.97), and why the timing matters for liability.
Explain what a declaration and a pre-entry are, who may make them (the declarant), and what documents the Act requires to be submitted.
Describe transit under section 95 and transhipment under section 96: how goods move without duty payment and what the responsible person must do.
Describe what happens to goods that are never cleared: uncleared goods, and the deposit of goods in the State warehouse under sections 52 and 53.

## Key Terms

arrival :: the point in time when a conveyance crosses the borders into the country
time of import :: the time of arrival of a conveyance within the limits of Ghana from outside the country with the intent to unlade merchandise
time of export :: the time when goods within Ghana are placed on board a conveyance for consignment to a place outside Ghana and the international voyage to that place commences
import :: to bring or cause goods to be brought into the country
export :: the act of taking out or causing to be taken out any goods from the country
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export
declarant :: a person who makes a declaration for customs purposes, and who is the owner of the goods, the importer, exporter, or the agent
pre-entry :: the entry of goods prior to their importation into Ghana
uncustomed goods :: goods liable to duty on which the full duties due have not been paid, and goods, whether liable to duty or not, that are imported or exported or in any way dealt with contrary to the provisions of this Act in relation to customs
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.14 — documents to be submitted; s.16 — arrival of conveyance; s.17 — report to
  be made on arrival of conveyance; s.21 — mooring and unloading. Together with s.6
  these carry the import sequence's opening half. (Sections 15, 18–20, 22–29 lie inside
  the plan's anchor range and may be cited where the lesson needs them — masters, bulk
  breaking, departure — but the named five are the spine.)
- `ACT` s.6(1) — the Commissioner-General may release declared goods as soon as they
  have been examined or exempted from examination, where no offence has been found,
  the required documents have been acquired, and the stated conditions are met.
- `ACT` s.48 — time of entry; s.49 — declaration; s.50 — pre-entry; s.51 — missing or
  incomplete documents. The entry half of the sequence.
- `ACT` s.52 — uncleared goods; s.53 — deposit of goods in the State warehouse. The
  lifecycle's failure mode.
- `ACT` s.95(1) — transit: goods may move from one port of entry to another (a) under a
  bond or by a bonded carrier or both without payment of duty; (b) under the TIR
  Carnets convention where the movement began or ends outside Ghana; (c) under the ATA
  carnet and temporary-admission conventions. s.95 is also 1.2's anchor for
  instruments the Act incorporates.
- `ACT` s.96(1)-(2) — transhipment: goods transfer from an importing conveyance to an
  exporting conveyance within the same port of entry without payment of duty, and the
  person declaring them is responsible for unloading, securing them in an approved
  customs-controlled area, identification, and loading.
- `ACT` s.97(1)-(3) — exports occur where goods are placed on board for consignment to
  a place outside the country; overland, where the declaration is passed and the goods
  cross the boundary; exports are subject to customs supervision and controls as
  prescribed by Regulations.

**Fact-check caution.** The Act's sequence is the anchor — do not import procedural
claims from practice (forms, timings, "you must do X within Y days") that the sections
cited here do not state. Electronic declarations and workflow statuses belong to
Module 5; the word "declaration" here is the Act's defined term, not a screen. Duty
liability and calculation (ss.71–72) are named in the objectives only as the reason
timing matters; their content is Module 8.
