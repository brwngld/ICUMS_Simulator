# LESSON BRIEF 1.2 — Customs laws, regulations and international standards

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 1: Customs Foundations and the Legal Mandate · lesson 2 of 4 · module 1 of 15
Proposed slug: `customs-laws-regulations-and-international-standards`

## Summary

Customs powers in Ghana are not one document but a stack: the 1992 Constitution at the
top, then the Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891) and its amendment Acts, the Revenue
Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915), the Customs Regulations (L.I. 2248) made under the
Act, the Commissioner-General's Orders and Customs Codes, and finally the international
instruments Ghana has adopted — the Harmonised System convention the Act itself
incorporates, and the facilitation conventions and agreements Ghana has acceded to.
This lesson teaches the learner to name each layer, say what it does, and understand
why an unapplied amendment changes what can safely be stated as fact.

## Learning Objectives

List the sources of customs law in Ghana in order of authority, from the Constitution to the Commissioner-General's Orders.
State what the Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891) and the Customs Regulations (L.I. 2248) each do, and how subordinate legislation relates to the parent Act.
Explain the practical consequence of Act 891's unapplied amendments for figures and procedures quoted from the Act.
Identify the international instruments the Act itself incorporates — the Harmonised System convention, the TIR Carnets convention and the ATA carnet conventions — and what each is for.
Name the facilitation instruments Ghana has acceded to outside the Act — the Revised Kyoto Convention and the WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement — and the regional tariff instrument, the ECOWAS Common External Tariff.

## Key Terms

Regulations :: the Regulations made under this Act by legislative Instrument
Minister :: the Minister responsible for Finance
Board :: the governing body of the Ghana Revenue Authority
tariff :: the Tariff Schedule of Ghana
Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System :: the nomenclature comprising the headings, sub-headings and their related numerical codes, the section, chapter and sub-heading notes and the general rules for the interpretation of the Harmonised System contained in the text of the International Convention on the Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System approved by the Customs Co-operation Council on 14th June, 1983 and the relevant Schedules attached to it

## Source anchors

- `ACT` front matter — the Act as supplied is "Legislation as at 18 May 2015" and its
  opening note lists the outstanding amendments not yet applied: Act 923 of 2016,
  Act 949 of 2017, Act 957 of 2017, Act 1014 of 2020, Act 1083 of 2022. Quote the note
  itself; it is the anchor for C4.4's penalty caution.
- `ACT` s.1(1) — duties and taxes are imposed at the rate specified in the Harmonised
  System: the point where the Act incorporates an international instrument into
  domestic law.
- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section
  (the HS definition names the International Convention on the Harmonised Commodity
  Description and Coding System and its approval by the Customs Co-operation Council
  on 14 June 1983).
- `CPC-LAW` — the GRA International Tax Office deck's legal-framework slide lists the
  stack verbatim: Constitution of the Republic of Ghana, 1992; Customs Act, 2015
  (Act 891); Customs (Amendment) Act, 2015 (Act 905); Revenue Administration Act, 2016
  (Act 915); Customs Regulations (L.I. 2248); Commissioner-General's Orders, Customs
  Codes; International Instruments Regarding Customs Operations. Its purpose slide
  grounds the "what the laws do" content: the framework to classify and value goods,
  impose duties and taxes, and empower the Customs Division to collect and account for
  them.
- WEB-verified (confirmed 2026-11-16; cite the source in `FACTS & SOURCES` if used):
  Ghana acceded to the Revised Kyoto Convention on 29 June 2019 (WCO contracting-parties
  list); Ghana's WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement acceptance was deposited 4 January 2017
  and the TFA entered into force 22 February 2017 (WTO TFA database); Ghana began
  implementing the ECOWAS Common External Tariff on 1 February 2016, moving from four
  bands to five (0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 35%) (GRA/ECOWAS CET reporting).

**Fact-check caution.** The `CPC-LAW` slide names "International Instruments Regarding
Customs Operations" but does not enumerate them, and the instruments research sheet is
not yet written. Do not state which instruments the deck means, and do not state any
instrument content, membership list or date beyond the three WEB-verified items above
and what the Act's own text grounds (the HS convention definition, s.95's TIR and ATA
convention references). Act 905 of 2015 is an applied amendment — it is not on the
front matter's outstanding list — so do not describe it as unapplied. When the
instruments sheet lands, it replaces the WEB-verified block as the primary anchor.

## Do not teach in this lesson

- The content of any instrument beyond what this brief grounds — GRI wording, RKC
  general standards, TFA measures, CET band assignments to specific goods. That is
  Module 7 and the instruments fact sheet's territory.
- Penalty amounts under Act 891 or its amendments. That is C4.4, and this lesson's
  unapplied-amendments point is the reason the ban exists.
- The clearance lifecycle itself. That is lesson 1.4.
- ICUMS, the Single Window, or any named electronic system. That is Module 5.
