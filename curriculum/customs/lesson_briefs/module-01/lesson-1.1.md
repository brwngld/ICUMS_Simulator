# LESSON BRIEF 1.1 — Purpose, functions and mandate of Customs

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 1: Customs Foundations and the Legal Mandate · lesson 1 of 4 · module 1 of 15
Proposed slug: `purpose-functions-and-mandate-of-customs`

## Summary

Customs is the branch of government that controls what crosses a national border: it
collects duty and tax on imported and exported goods, examines and releases consignments
against declared particulars, stops prohibited and restricted goods, and keeps records
that let it revisit a clearance after the goods have gone. This lesson establishes who
holds those powers in Ghana under the Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891), why the law makes
controls risk-based instead of examining every consignment, and what "under customs
control" actually means for the person clearing goods at Tema.

## Learning Objectives

State the main functions a customs administration performs under the Customs Act, 2015 (Act 891).
Identify the persons on whom Act 891 confers power: the Authority, the Commissioner-General, an officer and a proper officer.
Explain why customs controls are conducted primarily on the basis of risk management rather than uniform examination.
Distinguish duty as defined in Act 891 from tax collected on domestic transactions.
Describe what it means for goods to be under customs control from arrival to release.

## Key Terms

Authority :: the Ghana Revenue Authority established under the Ghana Revenue Authority Act, 2009 (Act 791)
Commissioner-General :: the person appointed under section 13 of the Ghana Revenue Authority Act, 2009 (Act 791)
officer :: a person employed by the Ghana Revenue Authority and assigned to perform customs duties
proper officer :: an officer whose right or duty it is to exact the performance of, or to perform, the act referred to
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
customs business :: an activity that has to do with the importation or exportation of goods
customs-controlled area :: a place in the country, designated by the Commissioner-General, where the Authority has control over goods, persons and conveyances
arrival :: the point in time when a conveyance crosses the borders into the country

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.1(1) — duty and tax are imposed on goods imported into or exported from the country at the rate of duty and tax specified in the Harmonised System; s.1(2)(a) allows any other tax, duty or levy prescribed under another enactment.
- `ACT` s.2 — exempt goods specified in the Harmonised System; persons, organisations and institutions in the Schedules are exempt; a removal article is exempt from duty.
- `ACT` s.3 — the Authority shall conduct customs controls including random checks; a control shall primarily be based on risk management, with the objective of identifying and evaluating risks and developing counter-measures from international, national and local level data; joint control and targeting with cooperating foreign customs administrations is permitted; risk register, risk profile and risk assessment data are confidential.
- `ACT` s.4 — the Commissioner-General may designate a customs-controlled area; s.4(3) fourteen-day forfeiture rule; s.4(6)-(7) limits on actions against the Authority.
- `ACT` s.5(1) — the activities that may be conducted in a customs-controlled area: loading, unloading, inspection, assessment and clearing; temporary storage of goods not yet released, including undeclared and seized goods; embarkation, disembarkation or control of persons; storage and processing of imported goods under bond.
- `ACT` s.6 — release of declared goods once examined or exempted from examination and the stated conditions are met.
- `ACT` s.7, s.8, s.9, s.10, s.12 — post-clearance audit, Authorised Economic Operator, record keeping, the Customs Laboratory, and customs advance rulings, as the wider mandate.
- `ACT` s.151 — the definitions quoted in Key Terms are taken from the Interpretation section.
- `CPC-LAW` — background and purpose of customs laws and regulations, as presented by the GRA International Tax Office.

## Do not teach in this lesson

  the fourteen-day rule as an operational deadline. That is lesson 1.3.

  lesson 1.2; here, mention only that powers come from an Act and its Regulations.

  That is lesson 1.4.

  practical track's territory.
