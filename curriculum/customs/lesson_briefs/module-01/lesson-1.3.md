# LESSON BRIEF 1.3 — Customs controlled areas, ports, borders and approved routes

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 1: Customs Foundations and the Legal Mandate · lesson 3 of 4 · module 1 of 15
Proposed slug: `customs-controlled-areas-ports-borders-and-approved-routes`

## Summary

Customs control is tied to physical places, and Act 891 names who designates each one:
the Minister designates ports, approved places of loading and unloading, and boarding
stations by Gazette notice; the Commissioner-General designates customs-controlled
areas and transit sheds in writing; and a private operator can be licensed to run an
area as a customs-controlled area. This lesson teaches the learner which place is
which, who designates it, what may lawfully happen inside a customs-controlled area,
what the fourteen-day rule does to goods left there, and how a bonded warehouse licence
differs from an area licence.

## Learning Objectives

Identify who designates each kind of customs place — port, approved place of loading or unloading, boarding station, transit-shed, customs-controlled area — and how the designation is published.
State the activities that may lawfully be conducted inside a customs-controlled area under section 5(1).
Explain the licensing route for a private area to operate as a customs-controlled area under section 46, and what a licence obliges.
Describe the fourteen-day rule in section 4(3): what goods it catches, what consequence follows, and the exception.
Distinguish the licensing of a private bonded warehouse under section 47 from the licensing of a customs-controlled area under section 46.

## Key Terms

port :: a place, whether on the coast or elsewhere, designated by the Minister by notice published in the Gazette and at least two daily newspapers of national circulation, subject to any conditions or limitations specified in such notice, to be a port for the purposes of this Act
approved place of unloading :: includes a quay, jetty, wharf or a part of an airport, designated by the Minister by notice in the Gazette and at least two daily newspapers of national circulation, to be a place where coastwise or imported goods or goods about to be carried coastwise or exported may be unloaded or loaded
boarding station :: a station or place appointed by the Minister by notice in the Gazette and at least two daily newspapers of national circulation to be a station or place for conveyances arriving at or departing from a port or place to bring to for the boarding or setting down of officers
transit-shed :: a building in a customs-controlled area, designated by the Commissioner-General by notice in writing, for the deposit of un-entered, un-declared goods
customs-controlled area :: a place in the country, designated by the Commissioner-General, where the Authority has control over goods, persons and conveyances
approved :: approved by the Commissioner-General or the proper officer
sufferance wharf :: a place other than an approved place of loading or unloading at which the Commissioner-General may and under such conditions and in such manner direct, either generally, or in a particular case, allows goods to be loaded or unloaded

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section. Note
  that s.151 defines "approved place of unloading" and "approved place of loading" as
  one inclusive pair. The designating-authority split is visible in the definitions
  themselves: the Minister by Gazette and two national newspapers for ports, approved
  places and boarding stations; the Commissioner-General in writing for transit sheds;
  the Commissioner-General for customs-controlled areas.
- `ACT` s.4(1) — the Commissioner-General may designate an area within the country as a
  customs-controlled area, and prescribe by directive the measures for its
  establishment, the protection of the Government's interests, and the withdrawal and
  accounting for goods deposited there.
- `ACT` s.4(3) — the fourteen-day rule: goods remaining in a customs-controlled area
  more than fourteen days after being entered under another customs procedure, entered
  for export, or sold by public auction are liable to forfeiture to the State unless
  the failure to move them is explained to the satisfaction of the Commissioner-General.
  Quote the three triggering events and the exception; none may be dropped.
- `ACT` s.5(1) — the permitted activities, verbatim in the brief: (a) loading, unloading,
  inspection, assessment and clearing of imported or exported goods; (b) temporary
  storage of goods imported at a port of entry and not yet released, including goods
  not yet declared and goods seized and liable to forfeiture; (c) embarkation,
  disembarkation or control of persons arriving in or departing from the country;
  (d) storage and processing of imported goods under bond.
- `ACT` s.46(1) — a person in charge of an area for processing a conveyance, for the
  temporary storage of unreleased goods, or for the embarkation, disembarkation or
  processing of persons may apply to the Commissioner-General for a licence to operate
  it as a customs-controlled area.
- `ACT` s.47(1)-(3) — a building or place may only be used as a private bonded warehouse
  if licensed by the Commissioner-General, the keeper deposits the required bond and
  pays the prescribed fees; the licence is in an approved form and may be revoked at
  any time.
- `CPC-PORT` — the port operators deck, as the operational background for how these
  places function day to day.

**Fact-check caution.** The institutions research sheet is not yet written, so do not
name port authorities, terminal operators or specific Ghanaian ports beyond what
`CPC-PORT` states, and do not attach operational claims to a named institution. The
places in this lesson come from the Act's definitions; keep every designation claim
tied to the defining provision. Two Key Terms above smooth the Act's typography:
the Act's text of the "port" definition reads "two daily newspaper", and "officer"
(lesson 1.1) reads "custom duties" — both normalised here as evident printing errors.
