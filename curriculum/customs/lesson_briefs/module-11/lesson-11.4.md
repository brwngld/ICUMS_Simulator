# LESSON BRIEF 11.4 — Movements between free zones, the customs territory and foreign markets

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`
Module 11: Transit, Transhipment and Free Zones · lesson 4 of 4 · module 11 of 15
Proposed slug: `movements-between-free-zones-the-customs-territory-and-foreign-markets`

## Summary

Zone goods move in three directions: deeper into the zone, out to a foreign market, or
into Ghana's customs territory — and each crossing has its own duty consequence. Act
891's free-zone provision sits alongside the transit and export provisions that carry
the goods. This lesson closes Module 11 by teaching the three crossings and the
documentation each needs.

## Learning Objectives

Map the three crossings — zone to zone, zone to foreign market, zone to the customs territory — and the duty treatment of each as the Act provides.
State which provisions carry each crossing: s.94 for the zone frame, s.95–96 for controlled movements, s.97 for exports.
Explain what changes when zone goods enter home use: the declaration, the duty, and the origin question.
Identify the documentation chain for each crossing, from the sources' own terms.

## Key Terms

Free Zone :: a place designated as a free zone under the Free Zones Act, 1995 (Act 504)
export :: the act of taking out or causing to be taken out any goods from the country
import :: to bring or cause goods to be brought into the country
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` ss.94–96 — the free-zone provision and the controlled-movement procedures.
- `ACT` s.97 — exports, as the zone-to-foreign-market crossing's trigger.
- `CPC-FZ` — the scan deck, read visually if used, pages cited (spec C3).

**Fact-check caution.** Duty treatment at each crossing must trace to the cited
sections; where the Act defers to the Free Zones Act or Regulations, say so. Do not
state incentive or exemption terms for zone operators — the source coverage does not
support it (see lesson 11.3's caution).
