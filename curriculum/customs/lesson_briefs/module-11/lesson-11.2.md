# LESSON BRIEF 11.2 — Guarantees, tracking, sealing, diversion risk and acquittal

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`
Module 11: Transit, Transhipment and Free Zones · lesson 2 of 4 · module 11 of 15
Proposed slug: `guarantees-tracking-sealing-diversion-risk-and-acquittal`

## Summary

Transit works because the revenue is guaranteed while the goods are on the road: a
bond or guarantee secures the duty, the goods are sealed and identified, and the
procedure ends with an acquittal proving the goods arrived. Act 891's security
machinery (ss.108–111) backstops s.95's bond requirement. This lesson teaches how the
guarantee, the seal and the acquittal hold a moving consignment legally together —
and what happens on diversion.

## Learning Objectives

State the security machinery: forms of security (s.108), surety (s.109), release and cancellation of the guarantee (s.110), and the bond's validity (s.111).
Explain the identification measures a transit movement carries — seals, marking, accompanying documents — as s.95 and the responsible-person rule require.
Describe acquittal: what closes a transit movement, and what an unacquitted movement leaves open.
Identify diversion risk and the Act's response: forfeiture of unlawfully loaded or unloaded goods (s.41) and the seizure machinery (s.124).

## Key Terms

bonded carrier :: a carrier licensed in accordance with this Act to transport bonded goods within the country
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` ss.108–111 — forms of security, surety, release and cancellation of guarantee,
  validity of bond.
- `ACT` s.95 — transit, including the bond/bonded-carrier requirement and the
  responsible-person content of s.96(2) where the contrast helps.
- `ACT` s.41 — forfeiture of goods unlawfully loaded or unloaded; s.124 — seizure and
  forfeiture.
- `CPC-TRANSIT` — the scan deck, read visually if used, with pages cited (spec C3).

**Fact-check caution.** Guarantee amounts, sealing technologies and tracking systems
are practice facts: state only what the sources state. The named-systems fact sheet
will carry any electronic tracking claim when written — until then, teach the rule
(the goods must remain identified and the movement must be acquitted), not the tool.
