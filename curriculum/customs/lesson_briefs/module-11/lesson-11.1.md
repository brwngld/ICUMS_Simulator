# LESSON BRIEF 11.1 — Transit and transhipment concepts, routes and documentation

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`
Module 11: Transit, Transhipment and Free Zones · lesson 1 of 4 · module 11 of 15
Proposed slug: `transit-and-transhipment-concepts-routes-and-documentation`

## Summary

Some goods never enter Ghana's commerce at all: they cross it, under customs control,
from one entry point to another (transit) or from one vessel to another in the same
port (transhipment). Act 891 provides both procedures — transit with its bond, bonded
carrier and convention routes; transhipment with its in-port transfer rules — and the
definitions fix the vocabulary. This lesson teaches the two procedures and how they
differ from import.

## Learning Objectives

Define transit (s.95) and transhipment (s.96) and the duty-deferral each provides.
State transit's three lawful movement modes under s.95(1): bond or bonded carrier or both, the TIR carnet convention, and the ATA/temporary-admission conventions.
Explain transhipment's in-port rule: transfer between importing and exporting conveyances within the same port of entry, and the declarant's responsibilities (s.96(2)).
Contrast both procedures with home-use import: what "under customs control" means for goods that never enter free circulation.

## Key Terms

transit-shed :: a building in a customs-controlled area, designated by the Commissioner-General by notice in writing, for the deposit of un-entered, un-declared goods
bonded carrier :: a carrier licensed in accordance with this Act to transport bonded goods within the country
customs-controlled area :: a place in the country, designated by the Commissioner-General, where the Authority has control over goods, persons and conveyances
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
