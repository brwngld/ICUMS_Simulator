# LESSON BRIEF 11.3 — Free-zone legal framework, territoriality and approved operations

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`
Module 11: Transit, Transhipment and Free Zones · lesson 3 of 4 · module 11 of 15
Proposed slug: `free-zone-legal-framework-territoriality-and-approved-operations`

## Summary

A free zone is a place outside the customs tariff's ordinary reach, though inside the
country: goods may enter, be processed and leave without home-use duty, under the
Free Zones Act's designation. Act 891 defines the free zone by reference to that Act
and provides its own free-zone provision (s.94). This lesson teaches the legal
framework, what territoriality means for duty, and what operations are approved
inside a zone.

## Learning Objectives

State the Act's free-zone definition — a place designated under the Free Zones Act,
1995 (Act 504) — and what that designation means for customs control.
Explain s.94's provision for goods and operations in a free zone.
Describe territoriality: goods in a zone are not in free circulation, and what that implies for duty, for movement into the customs territory, and for movement out.
Identify the approved operations a zone may host, as the sources present them.

## Key Terms

Free Zone :: a place designated as a free zone under the Free Zones Act, 1995 (Act 504)
import :: to bring or cause goods to be brought into the country
export :: the act of taking out or causing to be taken out any goods from the country
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
