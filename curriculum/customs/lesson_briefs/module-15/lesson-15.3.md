# LESSON BRIEF 15.3 — Compliance programmes, intelligence use and continuous improvement

Course 5: Advanced Border Controls, Compliance and Professional Practice · difficulty `advanced`
Module 15: Professional Customs Practice and Capstone · lesson 3 of 4 · module 15 of 15
Proposed slug: `compliance-programmes-intelligence-use-and-continuous-improvement`

## Summary

Mature practice manages compliance the way customs manages risk: systematically,
with records, and improving. The Authorised Economic Operator idea — trusted trader
status earned by compliance — sits in the Act (s.8); the risk provisions (s.3) show
how both sides of the border use data. This lesson teaches a practice's compliance
programme: what to build, what to measure, and how to improve it.

## Learning Objectives

State the Authorised Economic Operator provision (s.8): who registers, and what the registration presumes.
Build a compliance programme's parts for a practice: declaration accuracy, record discipline, treatment of errors, and the feedback loop.
Explain intelligence use by the trader: the public sources a professional monitors (the Act, the tariff, the agencies' rules) — cited, never from memory.
Connect the risk-management provisions (s.3) to the practice's own improvement cycle.

## Key Terms

Authorised Economic Operator :: an internationally certified person whose role in the international supply chain is secure and is in compliance with customs controls and other procedures
Authority :: the Ghana Revenue Authority established under the Ghana Revenue Authority Act, 2009 (Act 791)
record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data
customs business :: an activity that has to do with the importation or exportation of goods
