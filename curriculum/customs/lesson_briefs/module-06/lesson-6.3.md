# LESSON BRIEF 6.3 — Food and Drugs Authority permits, inspection and release

Course 2: Customs Procedures, Electronic Declarations and Import Controls · difficulty `beginner-intermediate`
Module 6: MDA Requirements and Import Controls · lesson 3 of 4 · module 6 of 15
Proposed slug: `food-and-drugs-authority-permits-inspection-and-release`

## Summary

Food, drugs, cosmetics and medical devices cannot enter Ghana's market without the
Food and Drugs Authority's approval. The FDA deck sets out the authority's mandate and
functions through its Centre for Import and Export Control, and the Public Health Act
supplies the statute behind it. This lesson teaches what FDA controls at the border,
how its permits attach to a clearance, and what release looks like when the agency is
satisfied.

## Learning Objectives

State FDA's mandate and functions as the deck presents them, through the Centre for Import and Export Control.
Identify the product categories FDA controls at the border from the deck's own list.
Describe the permit-to-release chain: FDA approval evidenced in the clearance before customs releases.
Connect the Public Health Act (Act 851) as the statutory anchor behind FDA's border controls.

## Key Terms

prohibited goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law
restricted goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law
electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `CPC-FDA` — the FDA deck (Centre for Import and Export Control, 2026): mandate and
  functions, MDA requirements, and the permit process as the deck states it. Quote its
  lists with slide numbers.
- `PHA` — Public Health Act, 2009 (Act 851): the statutory anchor for FDA's controls;
  cite the parts the FDA deck itself relies on, and no others.

**Fact-check caution.** Cite Act 851 only where the FDA deck ties a control to it; do
not range over the Act's other parts. The legal-anchors fact sheet, when written,
will index the Act 851 provisions the agencies use. Do not describe FDA's online
portal or screens — C7's rule applies.
