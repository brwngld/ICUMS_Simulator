# LESSON BRIEF 6.1 — Regulatory coordination and electronic permit applications

Course 2: Customs Procedures, Electronic Declarations and Import Controls · difficulty `beginner-intermediate`
Module 6: MDA Requirements and Import Controls · lesson 1 of 4 · module 6 of 15
Proposed slug: `regulatory-coordination-and-electronic-permit-applications`

## Summary

Customs does not clear alone: restricted goods need the controlling agency's approval
before they move, and that approval now arrives as an electronic permit the declaration
references. The ministries, departments and agencies — GSA, FDA and the rest — each
hold a piece of the border under their own statutes. This lesson teaches how the
multi-agency picture fits together and what an electronic permit does in a clearance.

## Learning Objectives

Explain what an MDA is in the Ghana clearance context and why customs alone cannot release restricted goods.
Identify the agencies a typical import may touch — standards, food and drugs, and the sector regulators named by the goods.
Describe the electronic permit's place in a declaration: evidence that a restricted-goods allowance exists.
State the prohibited/restricted distinction and how it decides whether a permit is even possible.

## Key Terms

prohibited goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law
restricted goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law
electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means

**Fact-check caution.** The institutions fact sheet is not yet written. Name only the
agencies the decks name (Ghana Standards Authority; Food and Drugs Authority); describe
every other agency generically ("the sector regulator for the goods"). Do not state
which agency controls which goods beyond the decks' own lists, and do not describe
permit screens or portals — C7's rule applies.
