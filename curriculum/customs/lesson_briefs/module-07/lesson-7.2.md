# LESSON BRIEF 7.2 — General Rules for Interpretation and legal notes

Course 3: Classification, Valuation, Origin and Duty · difficulty `intermediate`
Module 7: Harmonised System Classification · lesson 2 of 4 · module 7 of 15
Proposed slug: `general-rules-for-interpretation-and-legal-notes`

## Summary

Classification is decided by law, not intuition: the General Rules for Interpretation
are the six (plus subdivision) rules every tariff decision must walk, and the section
and chapter notes legally redefine the words of the headings they govern. Act 891's
HS definition incorporates those rules and notes into Ghanaian law. This lesson
teaches how a classifier moves through GRI 1 to GRI 6 and how the notes bind the text.

## Learning Objectives

State the role of the General Rules for Interpretation as the legal method of classification.
Walk the GRI sequence — classification by heading text and section/chapter notes (GRI 1), incomplete or unfinished goods (GRI 2), mixed and composite goods (GRI 3), and subheading comparison (GRI 6) — at rule-statement level.
Explain how section and chapter notes override or extend heading text, using the workbook's own notes.
Apply the rules' order: earlier rules decide only where later ones cannot.

## Key Terms

Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System :: the nomenclature comprising the headings, sub-headings and their related numerical codes, the section, chapter and sub-heading notes and the general rules for the interpretation of the Harmonised System contained in the text of the International Convention on the Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System approved by the Customs Co-operation Council on 14th June, 1983 and the relevant Schedules attached to it
tariff :: the Tariff Schedule of Ghana

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim; the HS definition is the legal route by
  which the GRI and the notes bind in Ghana.
- `TARIFF` — the workbook's schedule sheets carry the section and chapter notes
  (Table 1 opens with the First Schedule's Section I notes) — quote notes from the
  workbook's own text with their table and section reference.
- The instruments fact sheet (`customs-instruments-and-standards.md`, planned): when
  written, it will carry the verbatim GRI text and the HS Convention's structure.

**Fact-check caution.** The GRI's authoritative wording is not yet in a written fact
sheet, and the workbook's sheets may not carry the GRI text itself. Do not paraphrase
a GRI from memory: quote a rule verbatim only if you locate its text in the supplied
sources or verify it against the WCO's published HS text (cite the source), otherwise
state the rule's function without its number-by-number wording. This is exactly the
claim type learners repeat.
