# LESSON BRIEF 13.4 — Duty-free shops, ship stores and controlled consumption

Course 5: Advanced Border Controls, Compliance and Professional Practice · difficulty `advanced`
Module 13: Special Goods and Advanced Border Controls · lesson 4 of 4 · module 13 of 15
Proposed slug: `duty-free-shops-ship-stores-and-controlled-consumption`

## Summary

Some goods cross the border without ever entering home use: stores on a ship or
aircraft, and goods sold in duty-free shops under control. Act 891 defines cargo so
stores fall outside it, provides for aircraft and ship stores (s.24) and the delivery
of stores (s.90), and the duty-free deck covers the shop regime. This lesson teaches
the controlled-consumption family — what "stores" means legally and how duty-free
retail stays inside the law.

## Learning Objectives

Explain the cargo/stores split: the Act's cargo definition excludes stores and passenger or crew baggage, and s.24 governs the stores.
State the delivery-of-stores provision (s.90) and the duty treatment it provides.
Describe the duty-free shop regime as the deck presents it, and the control that keeps shop goods from leaking into home use.
Identify the clearance consequences: what a learner checks when stores and duty-free goods appear on a declaration.

## Key Terms

cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
ship :: floating craft of every description, but does not include aircraft
aircraft :: an apparatus, whether or not mechanically propelled, which is used for the transport by air of human beings or goods
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section (the
  cargo definition's stores carve-out is the lesson's legal spine).
- `ACT` s.24 — aircraft and ship stores.
- `ACT` s.90 — delivery of stores; s.91 — duty to be paid, as the boundary provision.
- `CPC-DFS` — `DUTY-FREE SHOPS AND SHIP STORES.pdf` is a scan with no usable text
  layer: read visually or treat as unavailable, and the draft says which it did
  (spec C3).

**Fact-check caution.** The duty-free deck is a scan — deck-sourced claims must be
read visually with pages cited, or dropped. Shop licensing, quotas and product lists
beyond the Act's provisions wait for the sources; teach the control concept, not an
inventory.
