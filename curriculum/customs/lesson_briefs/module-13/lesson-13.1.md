# LESSON BRIEF 13.1 — Prohibited, restricted and conditionally permitted goods

Course 5: Advanced Border Controls, Compliance and Professional Practice · difficulty `advanced`
Module 13: Special Goods and Advanced Border Controls · lesson 1 of 4 · module 13 of 15
Proposed slug: `prohibited-restricted-and-conditionally-permitted-goods`

## Summary

Three legal states decide whether goods cross the border at all: prohibited — no
import or export; restricted — only as the law allows; and conditionally permitted —
allowed once a requirement is met. Act 891's definitions fix the vocabulary and its
provisions fix the machinery (ss.37, 98–100). This lesson teaches the three states,
how a learner tells them apart, and what each demands of a declaration.

## Learning Objectives

Define prohibited goods and restricted goods from the Act's Interpretation section.
State the machinery: prohibition and restriction powers (s.37), prohibited sale (s.98), prohibited imports (s.99) and the penalty provision (s.100).
Explain "conditionally permitted" as the practical case of restricted goods: allowed against a condition (permit, licence, standard).
Apply the states to a declaration: what the officer checks before release for each.

## Key Terms

prohibited goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law
restricted goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law
uncustomed goods :: goods liable to duty on which the full duties due have not been paid, and goods, whether liable to duty or not, that are imported or exported or in any way dealt with contrary to the provisions of this Act in relation to customs
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export
