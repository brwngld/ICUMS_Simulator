# LESSON BRIEF 12.4 — Treatment decisions, physical examination and post-clearance follow-up

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`
Module 12: Customs Risk Management and Examination · lesson 4 of 4 · module 12 of 15
Proposed slug: `treatment-decisions-physical-examination-and-post-clearance-follow-up`

## Summary

A risk decision ends in one of three treatments: release without examination,
examination of the goods, or a documentary challenge — and the follow-up can also
happen after release, through audit. Act 891 gives officers police-style powers for
the physical side (ss.112–120), the invoice-and-package power (s.101), and the
post-clearance audit (s.7). This lesson teaches how a risk decision becomes action,
and the limits the law places on each power.

## Learning Objectives

Map risk decisions to treatments: no examination, documentary check, physical examination, post-clearance follow-up.
State the officer powers relevant at the border: police powers (s.112), search of persons with its restriction (ss.113–114), premises search (s.117), stop and board of conveyances (ss.118–119), and seizure of abandoned conveyances (s.120) — each with its limit.
Explain the power to demand invoices and inspect packages (s.101) as the documentary treatment's legal basis.
Describe post-clearance audit (s.7) as the treatment that follows release, and the records it consumes (s.9).

## Key Terms

officer :: a person employed by the Ghana Revenue Authority and assigned to perform customs duties
proper officer :: an officer whose right or duty it is to exact the performance of or to perform the act referred to
record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` ss.112–120 — the officer powers, each cited to its section: s.112 police
  powers; s.113 search of persons; s.114 restriction on search of persons; s.117
  search of premises; s.118 stop conveyance; s.119 board conveyance; s.120 seize
  abandoned conveyance.
- `ACT` s.101 — power to demand invoices and inspect packages.
- `ACT` s.7 — post-clearance audit; s.9 — record keeping.
- `CPC-RISK` — the deck's treatment/examination sections; cite slides.

**Fact-check caution.** The powers' limits are law: state each power with its
provision and its restriction (s.114 especially). Do not add operational practice
(where examinations happen, how long they take) that the sources do not state.
Arrest and detention content (s.115) belongs to Module 14's enforcement lesson; cite
it only as part of the powers map without teaching it.
