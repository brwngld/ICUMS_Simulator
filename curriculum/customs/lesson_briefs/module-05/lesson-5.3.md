# LESSON BRIEF 5.3 — Manifests, attachments, permits and electronic supporting documents

Course 2: Customs Procedures, Electronic Declarations and Import Controls · difficulty `beginner-intermediate`
Module 5: Electronic Customs Systems and Declarations · lesson 3 of 4 · module 5 of 15
Proposed slug: `manifests-attachments-permits-and-electronic-supporting-documents`

## Summary

A declaration travels with its evidence: the manifest that reports what the conveyance
carries, the attachments that prove the particulars, and the permits other agencies
require before certain goods may move. Act 891 sets the arrival report that starts the
manifest chain and the electronic record rules that let that evidence exist digitally.
This lesson teaches the supporting-document chain as the system sees it — what exists,
what it must match, and what happens when it is missing.

## Learning Objectives

State the report the master makes on arrival (s.17) as the manifest chain's legal starting point.
Explain what an attachment proves on a declaration, and the electronic record rules that let it exist digitally.
Identify the permit requirement's legal shape: prohibited and restricted goods move only as the law allows, and agency permits evidence that allowance.
Describe the consequence of missing or incomplete documents under section 51.

## Key Terms

electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means
electronic signature :: the electronic method of authentication prescribed by the Commissioner-General
prohibited goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law
restricted goods :: goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.16–17 — arrival and the report to be made on arrival, as the manifest
  chain's start.
- `ACT` s.14 — documents to be submitted.
- `ACT` s.51 — missing or incomplete documents.
- `CPC-GSA` and `CPC-FDA` — the agency decks' permit requirements, as the evidence
  that a restricted-goods allowance exists (the agencies themselves are Module 6's
  subject; cite here only as permit sources).

**Fact-check caution.** C7 applies: permits and attachments are taught as rules —
what the system must see and why — never as screens or upload buttons. Do not state
ICUMS attachment formats or permit workflows; the named-systems fact sheet will carry
those when written. Agency requirements are cited only to the decks' own slides.
