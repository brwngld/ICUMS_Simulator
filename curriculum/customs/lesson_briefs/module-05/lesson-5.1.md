# LESSON BRIEF 5.1 — Access, roles and navigation in the customs electronic system

Course 2: Customs Procedures, Electronic Declarations and Import Controls · difficulty `beginner-intermediate`

Proposed slug: `access-roles-and-navigation-in-the-customs-electronic-system`

## Summary

Declarations are made electronically, and the electronic environment assigns every
user a role: who may lodge, who may amend, who may release. Act 891 gives the electronic
declaration legal force, defines the electronic record and signature, and requires
agents to act on written authority. This lesson teaches the rules of the electronic
environment — what the system requires from each role and why — as rules, never as
screens.

## Learning Objectives

State what makes a declaration an "electronic declaration" under the Act and who may make one.
Explain the legal force of the electronic record and the electronic signature as the Act defines them.
Describe how the agent's written-authority duty (ss.43–44) carries into electronic lodging.
Distinguish the roles in a clearance — declarant, agent, proper officer, Commissioner-General — by what each is legally entitled to do.

## Key Terms

electronic declaration :: a declaration made electronically to the Commissioner-General, whether personally or on behalf of any person as agent of the particulars of a consignment of goods imported or exported or any other declaration authorised by the Commissioner-General in connection with the import or export of goods under this Act
electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means
electronic signature :: the electronic method of authentication prescribed by the Commissioner-General
agent :: a person performing customs business on behalf of another person
proper officer :: an officer whose right or duty it is to exact the performance of or to perform the act referred to
