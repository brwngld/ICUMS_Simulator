# LESSON BRIEF 2.2 — Commercial invoice, packing list and supporting records

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`

Proposed slug: `commercial-invoice-packing-list-and-supporting-records`

## Summary

The declaration is only as good as the documents behind it. Act 891 empowers customs to
demand the paperwork that proves what the goods are, what they cost and who owns them,
and it obliges businesses to keep records of every customs activity long after the
goods have gone. This lesson teaches the commercial invoice, the packing list and the
supporting records a clearance stands on, what customs can demand and inspect, and the
record-keeping duty that outlives the transaction.

## Learning Objectives

Identify the commercial invoice and the packing list as the core supporting documents of a clearance, and state what each shows.
State the power customs has to demand invoices and inspect packages under section 101.
Describe what counts as a "record" under the Act and who must keep customs records under section 9.
Explain why a false or misleading statement on a supporting document is a "false statement" with consequences, not a typo.

## Key Terms

record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data
false statement :: a statement made with respect to goods subject to customs control that is false or misleading in a material respect or a material omission from a required statement or document with respect to goods subject to customs control
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export
electronic record :: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.14 — documents to be submitted: the anchor for what accompanies a
  declaration.
- `ACT` s.101 — power to demand invoices and inspect packages: quote the provision's
  own framing of the power.
- `ACT` s.9 — record keeping: who must keep records and the obligation's shape (the
  retention period is a statutory deadline under C4.1 — state it with its provision).
- `CPC-TT` — the shipping deck's document slides, as the commercial-side description
  of the invoice and packing list.
