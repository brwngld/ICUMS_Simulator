# LESSON BRIEF 2.3 — Bill of lading, air waybill and certificate of origin

## Summary

Three transport documents decide who may claim the goods and what tariff treatment they
deserve: the bill of lading for sea cargo, the air waybill for air cargo, and the
certificate of origin that carries a preference claim. The Act ties each to the craft
that carries the goods and to the country-of-origin rule that valuation and preference
turn on. This lesson teaches what each document does, who issues it, and where it fits
in the clearance.

## Learning Objectives

State the function of the bill of lading and the air waybill, and the transport mode each belongs to.
Explain what a certificate of origin evidences and why preferential treatment depends on it.
Identify the master as the person in charge of the conveyance that carries the goods.
Connect the country-of-origin provision to the origin claims these documents support.

## Key Terms

ship :: floating craft of every description, but does not include aircraft
aircraft :: an apparatus, whether or not mechanically propelled, which is used for the transport by air of human beings or goods
master :: a person who is in charge of a conveyance
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — ship, aircraft and cargo, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.15 — master, verbatim ("a person who is in charge of a conveyance", for the
  purposes of that Part).
- `ACT` s.70 — country of origin: the provision the certificate of origin feeds; state
  its role without importing origin-rules content (that is Module 9).
- `CPC-TT` — the shipping deck's transport-document slides as the operational
  description of the B/L and air waybill.
