# LESSON BRIEF 2.1 — Modes of transport, vessel operations and cargo types

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 2: Trade, Shipping and Clearance Documents · lesson 1 of 4 · module 2 of 15
Proposed slug: `modes-of-transport-vessel-operations-and-cargo-types`

## Summary

Goods arrive at Ghana's borders by sea, air, road, rail, inland waterway and pipeline,
and the law names the craft and the people that carry them: ships and aircraft as
defined in Act 891, the master in charge of a conveyance, and cargo distinguished from
stores and passenger baggage. This lesson teaches the modes of transport, the vessel
types the learner will actually see at Tema and Kotoka, and the legal vocabulary the
Act fixes for them.

## Learning Objectives

List the modes of transport goods move by, and name the two craft types the Act defines.
Identify the main vessel types and the cargo each is built to carry.
State what the Act means by cargo, and how stores and passenger baggage differ from it.
Explain who the master of a conveyance is and why the law attaches duties to that person.

## Key Terms

ship :: floating craft of every description, but does not include aircraft
aircraft :: an apparatus, whether or not mechanically propelled, which is used for the transport by air of human beings or goods
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
container :: an article of transport equipment (a) of a permanent character and strong enough to be suitable for repeated use; (b) specially designed to facilitate the carriage of goods by one or more modes of transport without intermediate reloading; (c) fitted with a device that permits ready handling, particularly its transfer from one mode of transport to another; and (d) designed as to be easy to fill and empty
vehicle :: a conveyance for the transport by land of goods or persons
master :: a person who is in charge of a conveyance

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section, except
  "master", which the Act defines at s.15 ("a person who is in charge of a conveyance",
  for the purposes of that Part).
- `ACT` ss.16–21 — arrival, report, mooring and unloading: the legal frame a vessel
  operates inside once it reaches Ghana (the lifecycle detail is lesson 1.4's; here they
  ground who does what on the craft).
- `ACT` s.24 — aircraft and ship stores: the provision behind the cargo/stores split.
- `CPC-TT` — the shipping deck's modes of transport (ocean, air, rail, road, inland
  waterway, pipeline) and its vessel-type slides (container ships, bulk carriers,
  tanker ships, passenger ships, RORO/multi-purpose and pure-car carriers), as the
  operational content for vessel operations and cargo types.
- `CPC-TT` — the container slides, grounding the Act's "container" definition in the
  equipment the learner sees stacked at Tema.
