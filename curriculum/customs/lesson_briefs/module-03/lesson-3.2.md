# LESSON BRIEF 3.2 — Importers, exporters, customs house agents and freight forwarders

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 3: The Port and Border Clearance Ecosystem · lesson 2 of 4 · module 3 of 15
Proposed slug: `importers-exporters-customs-house-agents-and-freight-forwarders`

## Summary

A clearance is lodged by someone: the importer or exporter themselves, or a licensed
customs house agent acting for them. Act 891 defines the agent's role, requires the
agent to produce written authority, and licenses the bonded carriers that move goods
under bond. This lesson teaches who may lodge a declaration, what an agent must be
able to show, and where the freight forwarder sits relative to the licensed agent.

## Learning Objectives

Identify who may act in a customs transaction: the owner, importer, exporter or agent.
State what a customs house agent is licensed to do and what the agent must produce on demand under section 44.
Explain the difference between the agent performing customs business and the freight forwarder's wider logistics role.
Describe the bonded carrier's place in moving goods under customs control.

## Key Terms

agent :: a person performing customs business on behalf of another person
customs house agent :: a person who is granted a licence to carry out customs business
customs business :: an activity that has to do with the importation or exportation of goods
declarant :: a person who makes a declaration for customs purposes, and who is the owner of the goods, the importer, exporter, or the agent
bonded carrier :: a carrier licensed in accordance with this Act to transport bonded goods within the country

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.43 — customs house agent: the licensing provision; quote its framing of who
  may be licensed and what the licence is for.
- `ACT` s.44 — agent to produce authority: the written-authority requirement.
- `ACT` s.45 — bonded carrier: the licence that lets a carrier move bonded goods.
- `CPC-LAW` — the GRA deck's account of training and certifying persons to perform
  agency duties for and on behalf of persons doing business with the Customs Division,
  as the practice-side description of the agent's role.

**Fact-check caution.** The institutions fact sheet is not yet written, so the CHA
licensing route's administrative detail (course, certification body, renewal) waits
for that sheet; state the Act's licensing requirement and the deck's training
description only. Freight forwarders are not defined in Act 891 — describe the role
generically, without importing a statutory claim.
