# LESSON BRIEF 3.3 — Cargo arrival, manifesting, discharge and terminal handling

## Summary

The port learns what a ship carries before the ship arrives: the cargo report and the
manifest. Act 891 sets the arrival rules a conveyance must follow, the report the
master must make, the account of cargo due before departure, and the consequences of
goods loaded or unloaded unlawfully. This lesson walks the physical side of the
clearance — arrival, reporting, manifesting, discharge and terminal handling — as the
Act sequences it.

## Learning Objectives

State the arrival obligations of a conveyance under section 16: the port, the direct routing, and the place of mooring.
Identify the report the master must make on arrival (s.17) and the account of cargo due before departure (s.39).
Explain what breaking bulk and mooring/unloading (ss.20–21) mean on the quay, and under whose supervision they happen.
Describe the transire (s.40) and the forfeiture rule for goods unlawfully loaded or unloaded (s.41).

## Key Terms

arrival :: the point in time when a conveyance crosses the borders into the country
master :: a person who is in charge of a conveyance
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
package :: a parcel, container, bundle, box, cask or other receptacle
ship :: floating craft of every description, but does not include aircraft

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.15 — master, verbatim ("a person who is in charge of a conveyance").
- `ACT` s.16 — arrival of conveyance: quote the three arrival obligations in s.16(1)
  (the port or a Commissioner-General-approved place, direct routing without touching
  elsewhere, and proceeding quickly to the proper place of mooring or unloading).
- `ACT` s.17 — report to be made on arrival of conveyance; s.18 — failure to make
  report or account for package reported.
- `ACT` ss.19–21 — duties of a master, breaking of bulk, mooring and unloading.
- `ACT` ss.39–41 — account of cargo before departure, transire on arrival, and
  forfeiture of goods unlawfully loaded or unloaded.
- `CPC-PORT` — the deck's terminal-handling description, as the operational picture.
