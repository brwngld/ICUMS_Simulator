# LESSON BRIEF 3.4 — Release, delivery, storage, demurrage and detention

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 3: The Port and Border Clearance Ecosystem · lesson 4 of 4 · module 3 of 15
Proposed slug: `release-delivery-storage-demurrage-and-detention`

## Summary

The clearing officer's goal is release; the port's clock keeps running until delivery.
Act 891 sets the conditions under which the Commissioner-General releases declared
goods, and it provides for what happens to goods nobody clears: uncleared goods and
the deposit of goods in the State warehouse. This lesson separates the legal moment
(release) from the physical one (delivery), and teaches where goods sit — and at whose
risk — while they wait.

## Learning Objectives

State the release conditions of section 6(1): examination or exemption from examination, no offence found, required documents acquired, and the stated conditions met.
Distinguish release (a customs decision) from delivery (a physical handover after release).
Explain what makes goods "uncleared" under section 52 and what the Commissioner-General may do with them.
Describe the State warehouse under section 53: what goes there, who keeps it, and at whose expense goods wait.

## Key Terms

uncustomed goods :: goods liable to duty on which the full duties due have not been paid, and goods, whether liable to duty or not, that are imported or exported or in any way dealt with contrary to the provisions of this Act in relation to customs
State warehouse :: a place approved by the Commissioner-General for the deposit of uncustomed, un-entered, un-examined, abandoned, detained or seized goods for the security of the goods or the payment of duties that may be due
warehouse keeper :: the person licensed to operate a private bonded warehouse
cargo :: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage
