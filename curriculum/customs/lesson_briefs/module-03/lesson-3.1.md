# LESSON BRIEF 3.1 — Customs, port authorities, terminal operators and shipping lines

Course 1: Customs Foundations, Documents and the Clearance Ecosystem · difficulty `beginner`
Module 3: The Port and Border Clearance Ecosystem · lesson 1 of 4 · module 3 of 15
Proposed slug: `customs-port-authorities-terminal-operators-and-shipping-lines`

## Summary

A consignment at Tema passes through many hands: the customs division that controls it,
the port authority that owns the harbour, the terminal operator that stacks its
containers, and the shipping line that carried it. Each body holds a different legal
position, and the clearance stalls when the learner cannot tell them apart. This
lesson maps the ecosystem — who regulates, who operates, who carries — using the port
operators deck's picture of Ghana's seaports.

## Learning Objectives

Identify the four kinds of actor at a seaport clearance — customs, port authority, terminal operator, shipping line — and each one's role.
State what the Act's place designations (port, approved place, customs-controlled area) mean for who may do what on the quay.
Describe the port operators deck's account of Ghana's seaports and their share of the country's trade.
Distinguish a regulatory decision (customs) from an operational service (terminal) in a clearance sequence.

## Key Terms

port :: a place, whether on the coast or elsewhere, designated by the Minister by notice published in the Gazette and at least two daily newspapers of national circulation, subject to any conditions or limitations specified in such notice, to be a port for the purposes of this Act
approved place of unloading :: includes a quay, jetty, wharf or a part of an airport, designated by the Minister by notice in the Gazette and at least two daily newspapers of national circulation, to be a place where coastwise or imported goods or goods about to be carried coastwise or exported may be unloaded or loaded
customs-controlled area :: a place in the country, designated by the Commissioner-General, where the Authority has control over goods, persons and conveyances
ship :: floating craft of every description, but does not include aircraft

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` s.4–5 — designation of, and permitted activities in, a customs-controlled area
  (taught in lesson 1.3; cited here as the legal frame the operator works inside).
- `CPC-PORT` — the port operators deck: seaports as the link in the global supply
  chain handling up to 90 percent of world freight, Ghana's seaports handling over
  80% of the country's international trade, and the Port of Tema and Port of Takoradi
  as the gateways — quote these from the deck with its slide numbers.
- `CPC-PORT` — the deck's description of the seaport's functions, as the operational
  picture of terminal handling.

**Fact-check caution.** The institutions fact sheet is not yet written. Name only the
institutions the deck names (Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority as the port
authority; the Port of Tema and the Port of Takoradi), keep terminal operators and
shipping lines generic unless the deck names them, and attach no operational claim to
a named private company.
