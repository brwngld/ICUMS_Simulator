# LESSON BRIEF 8.4 — Worked customs value and full-duty calculations

Course 3: Classification, Valuation, Origin and Duty · difficulty `intermediate`
Module 8: Customs Valuation and Duty Calculation · lesson 4 of 4 · module 8 of 15
Proposed slug: `worked-customs-value-and-full-duty-calculations`

## Summary

Everything in the module now runs as arithmetic: a real consignment's invoice, its
Incoterm, the additions the law allows, the exchange conversion, the tariff row, and
the charge stack computed line by line. Act 891 supplies the method and the workbook
supplies the rows. This lesson is the module's worked capstone — every figure shown,
every figure sourced, every figure labelled illustrative.

## Learning Objectives

Build a customs value from an invoice under a stated Incoterm, showing each addition and the exchange conversion.
Compute the full charge stack — import duty, import VAT, processing fee, ECOWAS levy — on the correct bases, line by line.
Verify each computed figure against the tariff workbook row it came from.
State where the computation's uncertainties sit (the edition caveat) and how a practitioner resolves them before relying on a number.

## Key Terms

customs value of imported goods :: the value of goods, determined in accordance with this Act
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
tariff :: the Tariff Schedule of Ghana

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `ACT` ss.67–69 — valuation method, fallbacks, exchange rate.
- `ACT` ss.71–72 — liability and calculation.
- `TARIFF` — the workbook: the worked example must cite its table, heading, TSN and
  the columns used.
- `FULL DUTY CALCULATIONS - AKYA` — the scan's tax-element table (Import Duty on CIF;
  Import VAT at the stated rate on CIF + Import Duty; Processing Fee; ECOWAS Levy),
  as the structure the worked example mirrors; cite the page. The rest of the scan is
  read visually or treated as unavailable, and the draft says which (spec C3).

**Fact-check caution.** All money in the worked example is illustrative and the
example says so (spec C4.3); every rate carries the "illustrative of the structure —
verify the rate in force" label (spec C4.2). The example's goods should be a Ghanaian
trader's consignment (spec C5) — e.g. a containerized FMCG line through Tema — with an
international counterpoint (Rotterdam or Shenzhen) in the second worked example.
