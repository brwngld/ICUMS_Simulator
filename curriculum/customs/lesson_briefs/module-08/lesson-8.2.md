# LESSON BRIEF 8.2 — CIF, FOB, assists, additions, deductions and exchange rates

## Summary

The invoice price is rarely the customs value: freight, insurance, commissions,
packing, assists and royalties move the number, and the Incoterm on the invoice says
which side of the line each cost sits on. Act 891 fixes the exchange-rate provision
that converts foreign currency, and the delivery terms from Module 2 now earn their
keep. This lesson teaches what goes into the customs value and what stays out.

## Learning Objectives

Map each Incoterm's cost split (FOB, CFR, CIF) onto the customs-value build: what the term adds or removes.
Identify the additions the Act allows — commissions, packing, assists — and the deductions it refuses.
State the exchange-rate provision (s.69) and why the conversion moment matters.
Distinguish buying commission from selling commission using the Act's own definitions, and place each on the correct side.

## Key Terms

customs value of imported goods :: the value of goods, determined in accordance with this Act
buying commission :: a fee paid by an importer to the agent of the importer for the service of representing the importer in the purchase of the goods being valued
selling commission :: fees paid to the agent of seller for services performed in connection with selling imported goods
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods

**Fact-check caution.** Which additions and deductions the Act allows must be cited to
s.67–69's own text; where the Act defers to Regulations, say so rather than
inventing a list. The exchange rate is a statutory figure — never quote a current
rate; point to the provision and the schedule's currency columns instead.
