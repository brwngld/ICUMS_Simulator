# LESSON BRIEF 4.1 — Imports for home consumption and export procedures

Course 2: Customs Procedures, Electronic Declarations and Import Controls · difficulty `beginner-intermediate`
Module 4: Customs Regimes and Core Procedures · lesson 1 of 4 · module 4 of 15
Proposed slug: `imports-for-home-consumption-and-export-procedures`

## Summary

Home consumption is the default import: goods enter the country's free circulation,
duty paid. Export is its mirror — goods placed on board for consignment outside the
country. Act 891 names the home-use procedure and the export procedure's trigger, and
the timing provisions fix when each legally begins. This lesson teaches both
procedures as the Act defines them and the timing rules that make liability dates
unambiguous.

## Learning Objectives

State what placing goods under the home-use procedure achieves: free circulation in Ghana after duty and requirements are satisfied.
Identify when an export legally occurs under section 97, for both ship/aircraft consignment and overland exportation.
Distinguish the legal moments — time of import, time of entry, time of export — that the Act fixes and why liability hangs on them.
Connect the procedures to the sequence learned in Module 1: arrival, declaration, examination, release.

## Key Terms

import :: to bring or cause goods to be brought into the country
export :: the act of taking out or causing to be taken out any goods from the country
time of import :: the time of arrival of a conveyance within the limits of Ghana from outside the country with the intent to unlade merchandise
time of export :: the time when goods within Ghana are placed on board a conveyance for consignment to a place outside Ghana and the international voyage to that place commences
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
declaration :: a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export
