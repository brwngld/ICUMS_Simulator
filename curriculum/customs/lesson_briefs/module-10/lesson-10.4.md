# LESSON BRIEF 10.4 — Reconciliation, acquittal and control of duty relief

Course 4: Warehousing, Special Movements, Risk and Examination · difficulty `intermediate-advanced`

Proposed slug: `reconciliation-acquittal-and-control-of-duty-relief`

## Summary

Every relief is a promise: the goods will be used as authorised, the goods will leave,
or the duty will be paid. The Act polices those promises through records, securities
and audits — the bond's validity (ss.108–111), the warehouse records (s.80), the
audit (s.7) and the reconciliation of what was declared against what happened. This
lesson closes the module by teaching how the system checks that relief was deserved.

## Learning Objectives

Explain the promise structure of relief: authorised purpose, export outcome, or eventual payment.
State the security machinery's parts — forms of security (s.108), surety (s.109), release and cancellation of guarantee (s.110), validity of bond (s.111) — and how a bond is discharged.
Describe the warehouse keeper's record duty (s.80) and the audit's use of records (s.7).
Walk a reconciliation: declared relief, actual outcome, and the adjustment when they differ.

## Key Terms

record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data
private bonded warehouse :: a building or place appointed by the Commissioner-General and licensed to be a place where goods to be warehoused are secured
warehouse keeper :: the person licensed to operate a private bonded warehouse
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
