# LESSON BRIEF 9.2 — Preferential and non-preferential rules of origin

Course 3: Classification, Valuation, Origin and Duty · difficulty `intermediate`
Module 9: Rules of Origin and Trade Preferences · lesson 2 of 4 · module 9 of 15
Proposed slug: `preferential-and-non-preferential-rules-of-origin`

## Summary

The same goods can have two origins: the non-preferential origin that names them on
the declaration, and the preferential origin a trade agreement grants. The two follow
different rules and produce different duties. The continental agreement's annexes in
the supplied sources show how a preference regime writes its rules. This lesson
teaches the difference and how each rule family is tested.

## Learning Objectives

Distinguish preferential origin (agreement-based, tied to a claim) from non-preferential origin (declaration-based, tied to measures).
Explain what a preference rule typically tests: wholly obtained, change of classification, or a process rule — as the annexes present them.
State how a preferential claim changes the duty outcome on an otherwise identical consignment.
Identify the instruments Ghana trades under, from the annexes and the instruments fact sheet when written.

## Key Terms

produced :: grown, manufactured or mined
manufacture :: to make, produce, or cause to be made or produced, goods
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
tariff :: the Tariff Schedule of Ghana

## Source anchors

- `ACT` s.151 — the Key Terms above, verbatim from the Interpretation section.
- `AFU` — `body_gen-annex-and-specific-annexes.pdf` (African Union / continental
  agreement annexes): the rule-writing pattern for a preference regime; quote the
  annex text you use with its annex reference.
- `ACT` s.70 — the non-preferential side's statutory anchor.
- The instruments fact sheet (planned): when written, it grounds instrument names,
  membership and dates. The WEB-verified facts in the PLAN's calibration note (RKC
  accession, TFA acceptance, ECOWAS CET implementation) are available to cite with
  their sources.

**Fact-check caution.** Do not state any agreement's rule thresholds (value-add
percentages, change-of-heading rules) unless the supplied annex states them or the
instruments fact sheet has been written. ECOWAS CET content is regime context, not a
rule source: cite the CET's implementation date only, as verified.
