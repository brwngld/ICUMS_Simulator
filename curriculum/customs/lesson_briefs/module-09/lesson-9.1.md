# LESSON BRIEF 9.1 — Origin concepts, wholly obtained goods and substantial transformation

Course 3: Classification, Valuation, Origin and Duty · difficulty `intermediate`
Module 9: Rules of Origin and Trade Preferences · lesson 1 of 4 · module 9 of 15
Proposed slug: `origin-concepts-wholly-obtained-goods-and-substantial-transformation`

## Summary

Origin decides which tariff row a good deserves and whether a preference applies. The
two founding concepts are wholly obtained — grown, produced or mined entirely in one
country — and substantial transformation, where inputs from several countries become
something else. Act 891 fixes the country-of-origin provision, and its definitions of
"produced" and "manufacture" carry the concepts. This lesson teaches the founding
ideas every origin rule builds on.

## Learning Objectives

State the country-of-origin provision's role (s.70) in the tariff treatment of goods.
Define "wholly obtained" using the Act's own definitions of produced and manufacture.
Explain substantial transformation: the change that gives multi-country goods an origin.
Distinguish origin from nationality of the trader: the goods' origin, not the seller's country.

## Key Terms

produced :: grown, manufactured or mined
manufacture :: to make, produce, or cause to be made or produced, goods
raw material :: goods from which other goods are capable of being manufactured and any residue from any process of manufacture
import :: to bring or cause goods to be brought into the country
export :: the act of taking out or causing to be taken out any goods from the country
