# LESSON BRIEF 9.4 — Verification, denial of preference and origin disputes

Course 3: Classification, Valuation, Origin and Duty · difficulty `intermediate`
Module 9: Rules of Origin and Trade Preferences · lesson 4 of 4 · module 9 of 15
Proposed slug: `verification-denial-of-preference-and-origin-disputes`

## Summary

A preference claim can be checked, challenged and taken away. Customs verifies origin
claims against the records the trader must keep, and a failed verification denies the
preference and re-assesses the duty. This lesson teaches how verification works, what
records prove, and where the trader's remedies — objection and advance ruling — fit.

## Learning Objectives

Describe a verification: what customs asks for, and what records answer it (s.9's record-keeping duty as the evidence base).
State the consequences of a failed claim: denial of preference, duty re-assessment.
Explain the trader's remedies — objection (s.13) and the advance ruling (s.12) — as dispute-prevention and dispute-resolution.
Connect post-clearance audit (s.7) to origin claims discovered late.

## Key Terms

record :: any information kept in the ordinary course of business that pertains to any customs activity, in the nature of a statement, declaration, document, data, book, paper, correspondence, accounts, and financial accounting data
customs advance ruling :: a binding written statement issued by customs that interprets and applies customs laws and regulations to a specific set of facts upon application by a trade about a prospective transaction
duty :: customs duty, excise duty, tax or other charges collectible on imported or exported goods
