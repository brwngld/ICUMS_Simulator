=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The deferral family, at a glance
BODY:
Module 4's first lesson taught the default: home use, duty paid, free circulation. This lesson walks the family of procedures that defer the default — goods that enter Ghana, but whose duty question is postponed because of what the goods will do next.

The family's members, each defined by its own section: temporary admission (section 75) — goods enter, stay temporarily, and leave; inward processing (section 76) — goods enter to be processed and re-exported; re-importation in the same state (section 77) — Ghanaian goods returning unchanged; re-importation after outward processing (section 78) — Ghanaian goods returning from processing abroad; re-exportation of commercial goods (section 89) — the exit route when deferred goods leave.

The family's shared logic: the duty that home use would pay is deferred — secured by the procedure's conditions — because the goods' intended future (departure, processing-and-departure, or return) changes what the border should collect now. The learner reads each member by asking two questions: what is deferred, and what condition secures the deferral. The answers are the sections' own terms.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Temporary admission: goods in, goods out
BODY:
Section 75 provides temporary admission: the procedure under which goods may enter Ghana for a limited purpose — use, exhibition, work — without paying the duty home use would carry, because the goods are admitted on the understanding that they will leave.

The procedure's logic runs on the departure: the admission is temporary by definition, so the conditions that govern it exist to ensure the departure happens — the goods remain identifiable, the stay runs within the admitted limits, and the exit is documented. Section 97's export provisions are the machinery the departure eventually uses.

The learner's reading of the section: temporary admission is home use with the duty deferred and the clock running. The goods are under customs procedure for their whole stay — used, exhibited or worked as admitted, but not entered into free circulation — and the deferral holds only while the procedure's conditions hold.

Where the section defers detail to the Regulations, the lesson says so: the admission's limits and forms live where the Act puts them, and the learner states "as prescribed" rather than inventing a period.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Inward processing: goods in, goods transformed, goods out
BODY:
Section 76 provides inward processing: goods imported to be processed — manufactured, worked, transformed — and then re-exported, with the duty treatment following the goods' actual destiny.

The Act's definitions carry the block. "Manufacture" means to make, produce, or cause to be made or produced, goods; "raw material" means goods from which other goods are capable of being manufactured, and any residue from any process of manufacture. Inward processing is the raw-material definition in motion: goods enter as inputs, the manufacture happens in Ghana, and the outputs leave.

The procedure's logic: duty follows destiny. If the imported goods will leave as part of exported products, charging home-use duty on their entry would tax exports — so the procedure defers or relieves the duty under its conditions, and the re-export settles the account.

The learner's reading: inward processing is the deferral family's industrial member — its conditions exist to prove the processing happened and the outputs left. Where the section defers detail to the Regulations, the discipline stands: "as prescribed", not invented.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Re-importation: same state and after outward processing
BODY:
Two provisions cover Ghanaian goods returning, and the difference between them is the condition of the goods at return.

Re-importation in the same state — section 77: goods that left Ghana and return unchanged. The classic case is the returned export: goods sent out, rejected or undelivered, coming back as they went. The duty question is simple because the goods are simple — nothing happened to them abroad.

Re-importation after outward processing — section 78: goods that left Ghana for processing abroad and return processed. The mirror of inward processing: the Ghanaian goods were the inputs, the foreign factory was the processor, and the returning goods are the outputs. The duty question is harder, because the goods have changed — and the procedure's conditions govern what is owed on the return.

The learner's discrimination test is the goods' state at return: unchanged points to section 77; transformed points to section 78. The two provisions exist precisely because the difference matters to the duty — and the file that proves which happened is the file the claim rests on.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Re-exportation: the deferral family's exit
BODY:
Section 89 provides the re-exportation of commercial goods — the exit route for goods that entered under a deferral and are now leaving.

The deferral family's procedures all presume a departure: temporary admission's goods leave, warehoused goods leave or enter home use, inward processing's outputs leave. The re-exportation provision is where the leaving is formalised — the goods exit under a procedure that accounts for what they were when they entered and what the deferral secured.

The learner's reading of the flow: enter under the deferral (section 75 or 76 or the warehouse), stay under its conditions, and leave under the re-exportation — with the papers proving the chain. The deferral's account closes when the goods leave, because the deferred duty's question was always "will they stay or will they go" — and re-exportation is the "go" answer.

What the provision does not do is forgive: a deferral whose goods never leave and never enter home use has not closed its account. The exit — or the home-use entry with duty paid — is what the procedures' conditions require, and the learner's advice tracks which closing the client is heading for.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Deferral is not exemption
BODY:
The family's most important discipline is the distinction between deferral and exemption, because learners conflate them constantly.

An exemption (section 2's exempt goods, the Schedules' exempt persons) removes the duty question: the goods are exempt by law, and no duty arises. A deferral postpones it: the duty would arise — the goods are not exempt — but the procedure suspends the question because the goods' intended future makes the collection premature.

The distinction's teeth: a deferred duty is still owed if the deferral's conditions fail. Goods admitted temporarily that never leave; warehoused goods that never move; processing outputs that stay in the market — each failed condition revives the question the procedure postponed. An exempt good's question never arises, so there is nothing to revive.

The learner's advice discipline: for a deferral, the client is told the duty is postponed, not cancelled — and the conditions that keep it postponed are the client's continuing obligations. The family's procedures are promises, and the promise is what the conditions enforce.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Construction equipment admitted for a project, then re-exported
BODY:
Step 1. The intention. A construction company wins a two-year road project in Ghana and imports six excavators to execute it. The machines will leave when the project ends. The intention is a temporary stay — the regime is temporary admission under section 75.

Step 2. The admission. The machines enter under the procedure's conditions: identifiable, admitted for the stated purpose, the duty deferred because the goods will leave. The company's file holds the admission's papers — the particulars, the conditions, the machines' identities.

Step 3. The stay. For two years the machines work the project — used as admitted, not sold, not entered into free circulation. The deferral holds while the conditions hold; the machines' file stays live, because the procedure's obligations are continuing ones.

Step 4. The departure. The project ends. The machines are loaded for their outbound voyage, and the exit is documented under the re-exportation machinery of section 89 — the deferral family's exit route. The account closes: goods admitted, goods departed, the deferred duty's question answered by the departure.

Step 5. The counterfactual. Had the company sold one excavator in Ghana instead of exporting it, the deferral's condition would have failed for that machine — the deferred duty's question revives, and the entry becomes home use with duty paid.

What does this example demonstrate? Temporary admission is a promise with a clock: the goods' departure is what the deferral secured, and the sale that breaks the promise revives the question.

Key lesson: the deferred duty is owed unless the departure happens — the conditions are the promise, and the re-exportation is the performance.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Ghanaian goods processed abroad and returned
BODY:
Step 1. The outward leg. A Ghanaian textile firm sends 5,000 metres of grey fabric abroad for dyeing and finishing — work its own plant cannot do. The goods leave under the export machinery, with the outward-processing intention documented.

Step 2. The processing. The foreign plant dyes and finishes the fabric. The goods that will return are not the goods that left — they are transformed, which is why section 78's re-importation after outward processing is the returning regime, and section 77's same-state provision is not.

Step 3. The return. The finished fabric returns to Ghana. The re-importation claim rests on the file: the outward shipment's papers, the processing evidence, the returning goods' particulars — the chain proving the goods left as inputs and return as their processed outputs.

Step 4. The duty question. The procedure's conditions govern what is owed on the return — the duty treatment of the transformation follows the section's terms and the Regulations where the Act defers to them. The firm's file is what makes the claim checkable.

Step 5. The discrimination test applied. Had the firm's goods returned undyed — rejected, unchanged — the returning regime would have been section 77: same state. The state at return picks the provision.

What does this example demonstrate? The re-importation provisions are discriminated by the goods' condition at return — unchanged or transformed — and the file proves which one applies.

Key lesson: the state at return picks the provision; the file proves the state.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: "As prescribed by Regulations" — the deferral family's recurring phrase
BODY:
The deferral family's sections defer their operational detail repeatedly: the admission's periods and conditions, the processing's controls, the re-importation's documentation. Where the Act writes "as prescribed" or points to the Regulations, this programme writes the same phrase — and the learner must not fill the gap from memory. A specific time limit, condition or form quoted without a source is a claim the border can falsify, and clients act on such claims at the border's mercy. The deferral family's conditions are exactly what the Regulations prescribe, and the legal-anchors fact sheet (ghana-legal-anchors.md, planned) will carry them with citations when written — until then, the phrase is the honest answer. The discipline: state the section's own terms, write "as prescribed by Regulations" for the deferred detail, and verify any specific figure before it reaches a client.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The failed deferral revives the question
BODY:
Every procedure in the deferral family presumes a future — departure, processing-and-departure, return — and the duty question is postponed on that presumption. When the presumption fails, the question comes back. Goods admitted temporarily that are sold in the market have entered free circulation without paying for it; processing outputs that stayed have become home-use goods retroactively in the law's eyes; the deferral's conditions are the promises whose keeping keeps the duty away. The learner's advice formula: the client is told the duty is postponed, the conditions that keep it postponed, and the moment the conditions break — because the revival is not a penalty invented by an officer; it is the deferred question coming due. The exemption never comes due; the deferral always can.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A company imports exhibition equipment under temporary admission. Halfway through the show, it sells two of the exhibited machines to Ghanaian buyers. Which option best states the duty position of the two sold machines?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. No duty arises, because the machines were admitted under a procedure and the admission covers their whole stay.
2. The machines were exempt on entry, so no duty can ever arise for them.
3. The deferral's condition has failed for those machines: temporary admission defers the duty on the understanding that the goods will leave — goods sold into the market have entered free circulation without paying for it, so the deferred duty's question revives for them.
4. The duty position is unchanged, because the sale was to Ghanaian buyers rather than foreigners.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's deferral-versus-exemption distinction carries the answer. Temporary admission under section 75 defers the duty home use would carry, on the condition that the goods leave — the admission is temporary by definition. Selling the machines into the Ghanaian market breaks the condition: the goods have entered free circulation, which is the event the deferral postponed the duty against. The deferred question revives for the sold machines, and the entry becomes home use with the duty paid.

Answer 1 mistakes the deferral for an exemption that covers the stay regardless of what happens to the goods. Answer 2 claims an exemption that never existed — the admission is a postponement, not a section 2 exemption. Answer 4 invents a buyer-nationality test that appears nowhere in the procedure; the event is the entry into free circulation, whoever the buyer is.

The takeaway: the deferred duty is owed unless the deferral's condition is performed — and a sale into the market is the condition's breach, machine by machine.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
A Ghanaian firm sends goods abroad twice. Shipment A returns unchanged after a buyer abroad rejected it. Shipment B returns dyed and finished after processing in a foreign plant. Which option best states the returning provisions that apply?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both shipments return under re-importation in the same state (s.77), because both left Ghana from the same exporter.
2. Both shipments return under re-importation after outward processing (s.78), because both crossed a border.
3. Shipment A returns under section 77 — re-importation in the same state — because the goods are unchanged; shipment B returns under section 78 — re-importation after outward processing — because the goods were transformed abroad; the state at return picks the provision.
4. Neither shipment needs a provision, because returning goods owe no duty.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's discrimination test is the goods' state at return. Shipment A's goods are unchanged — rejected and returned as they went — which is section 77's re-importation in the same state. Shipment B's goods were transformed abroad — dyed and finished — which is section 78's re-importation after outward processing: the mirror of inward processing, where Ghanaian goods were the inputs of a foreign manufacture. The two provisions exist precisely because the difference matters to the duty question on return.

Answer 1 collapses the distinction on exporter identity — the exporter did not change; the goods did. Answer 2 collapses it on the border crossing — both crossed, but only one came back transformed. Answer 4 assumes returning goods are duty-free as a category; the provisions exist because the return's duty question is real, and its answer depends on the goods' state.

The takeaway: unchanged at return, section 77; transformed at return, section 78 — and the file proving the state is what makes either claim checkable.
