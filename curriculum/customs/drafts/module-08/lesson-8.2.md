=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The additions section 67(3) makes to the price
BODY:
Section 67(3) adjusts the customs value to include three cost groups, and the adjustments are where the Incoterm meets the law.

(a) The cost of transport for importing the goods into the country — the freight that brought them. (b) Charges for loading, unloading and handling associated with that transport. (c) The rate of insurance, calculated as the relevant enactment provides, where the importer fails to declare the insurance paid.

The additions' logic is the customs value's base: the transaction value is the price — and the law adds to it the costs of getting the goods to Ghana that the price may not include. An FOB invoice prices the goods at the loading port; the freight and the insurance were the buyer's costs; section 67(3) adds them back, so the customs value reaches the base the charges are computed on. A CIF invoice already carries the freight and insurance — the additions have less to add, because the price includes them.

The learner's reading: the additions are not a penalty or a surcharge — they are the law bringing the value to the base, and the Incoterm tells the officer which lines the addition touches.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The additions of subsection (4): packing, commissions, containers, assists
BODY:
Section 67(4) lists the additions made to the price paid or payable where they are incurred by the buyer but are not already included in the price.

(a) The cost of packing, whether for labour or materials. (b) Commissions and brokerage — except the buying commission. (c) The cost of containers treated as being one with the goods for customs purposes. (d) The value, apportioned as appropriate, of goods and services supplied directly or indirectly by the buyer free of charge or at a reduced cost for use in the production or sale for export of the imported goods: the materials, components and parts incorporated in the goods; the tools, dies and moulds; the materials consumed in the production; and the engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans and sketches undertaken outside the country necessary for the production.

The list's principle: costs the buyer incurred for the goods' production and sale-for-export are part of what the goods cost, whether or not they appear on the invoice. The assists — the buyer's moulds, the buyer's designs — are value the buyer supplied, and the law adds them.

The learner's reading: the additions run "where they are incurred by the buyer but are not already included" — the condition is checked for each line, and the invoice's terms decide what is already in.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Buying commission and selling commission: the defined split
BODY:
Section 151 defines the two commissions, and the definitions place them on opposite sides of the value.

A "buying commission" is a fee paid by an importer to the agent of the importer for the service of representing the importer in the purchase of the goods being valued. A "selling commission" is fees paid to the agent of the seller for services performed in connection with selling the imported goods.

The placement follows the definitions: the buying commission is the buyer's own purchasing representation — a cost of buying, not a cost of the goods — and section 67(4)(b) expressly excepts it from the commissions added. The selling commission is paid for selling the goods — a cost of getting them sold, incurred for the seller's benefit — and it is inside the value the additions capture.

The learner's discrimination test: whose agent, and for what? The buyer's purchasing agent's fee is excepted; the seller's selling agent's fee is added. The two definitions are the test's text, and the file's evidence is whose agent the fee paid.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The assists: value the buyer supplied
BODY:
Section 67(4)(d)'s assists are the additions list's most technical lines, and the learner should hold them as four families.

The materials, components and parts incorporated in the imported goods — supplied by the buyer free of charge or at reduced cost. The tools, dies and moulds supplied for the production. The materials consumed in the production. The engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans and sketches undertaken outside the country, necessary for the production.

Each family is value the buyer contributed to the goods' making — value that would otherwise hide in the seller's lower price. The apportionment is the provision's own: "the value, apportioned as appropriate" — the assists' value is apportioned to the imported goods' share of the production.

The learner's practice: assists are found by asking the buyer what they supplied to the producer — materials, tooling, designs — and each answer is tested against the families, apportioned, and added where the condition (free of charge or reduced cost, for the production or sale for export) is met.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The exchange rate: the conversion provision
BODY:
Section 69 provides the conversion rule: where the conversion of currency is necessary for the determination of the customs value, the Commissioner-General shall use the current rate of exchange determined by the Bank of Ghana.

Two disciplines follow. The rate's source is fixed: the Bank of Ghana's determined rate — not the trader's bank's rate, not the invoice's rate, not a remembered rate. And the conversion's moment is the determination's moment: the customs value is determined in cedis at the prescribed rate, and the file states the rate used.

The learner's practice: an invoice in dollars is converted at the Bank of Ghana's determined rate — the rate stated in the file, with its source — and the illustrative figures in this module's worked examples carry the spec's C4.3 label: money is illustrative and the example says so. The rate is never quoted from memory; the provision points to the Bank of Ghana's determination, and the verification is that determination's record.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The Incoterm bridge: reading the invoice for the build
BODY:
Lesson 2.4 taught the delivery terms as allocations; this lesson uses them, because the additions' map is the term's map.

An FOB invoice prices the goods at the loading port: the freight and insurance to Ghana were the buyer's costs — and section 67(3)(a)-(c)'s additions bring them in. A CIF invoice prices the goods with cost, insurance and freight included — the additions have already been made by the price, and the officer's check confirms the term's coverage. The buyer's assists and commissions run across both terms: they are checked under section 67(4) whatever the delivery term is.

The bridge's lesson: the Incoterm tells the officer where the invoice stops; section 67 tells the officer where the value must reach. The difference between the two is the additions' work — and the agent's file states the term, the invoice's lines, and the additions applied, so the build is transparent.

The learner's deliverable: a value build that shows the invoice, the term, the additions, and the base — every line sourced to its provision.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Tiles on FOB: the additions built
BODY:
Step 1. The invoice. The Kumasi trader's tiles are invoiced at 40,000 dollars, FOB the foreign loading port — the term of lesson 2.4's example. The price covers the goods to the vessel's rail; the freight and insurance are the buyer's.

Step 2. The transport additions. The freight to Tema is 3,200 dollars, the marine insurance 200 dollars: section 67(3)(a) adds the transport cost, and 67(3)(c) adds the insurance — the importer declared it, so the declared figure enters.

Step 3. The buyer-cost additions. The buyer supplied the tile designs to the producer for the export production — section 67(4)(d)'s engineering and design family. The design work's apportioned value, 500 dollars, is added; the buyer's buying commission to its purchasing agent, 300 dollars, is excepted under 67(4)(b) and stays out.

Step 4. The conversion. The additions' total is converted at the Bank of Ghana's determined rate under section 69 — the rate stated in the file — and the customs value reaches its base in cedis.

Step 5. The transparency. The build shows every line: invoice 40,000; freight 3,200; insurance 200; designs 500; buying commission excepted; the conversion rate stated. The officer reads the build, and the term's map matches the additions.

What does this example demonstrate? The FOB term tells the build what the invoice omits, and section 67's additions bring the omitted costs in — with the excepted commission staying out.

Key lesson: the term maps the additions; the provisions justify them; and the build's every line is sourced.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A CIF invoice read at a foreign border
BODY:
Step 1. The invoice. A European importer's goods arrive on a CIF invoice: cost, insurance and freight included — the term's allocations from lesson 2.4.

Step 2. The check. The administration's valuation reads the term: the CIF price already carries the transport and insurance — the additions' work is confirming the invoice's coverage, not adding fresh lines. The buyer's assists and commissions are checked under the corresponding provisions, because the term does not remove that check.

Step 3. The distinction held. The administration's file shows the term, the invoice's lines, and the additions' state: nothing to add where the term included, additions applied where the buyer's costs ran outside.

Step 4. The transfer. The reading is the same reading Ghana's section 67(3) produces: the term tells the officer what is in; the provisions tell the officer what must be in; and the build reconciles the two. The buyer's assists and commissions are checked under the corresponding provisions in both jurisdictions — the delivery term does not remove that check, because the buyer's contributions run across every term.

What does this example demonstrate? The Incoterm's allocations and the valuation's additions are two readings of the same costs — the term for the parties, the provisions for the value — and the professional reads both before the base is built.

Key lesson: the term does not decide the value; it decides which lines the value build checks — and the checks run whatever the term says, because the assists and the commissions are the buyer's contributions, not the invoice's.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The additions are statutory — no invented lists
BODY:
Section 67(3) and (4) state the additions, and the lesson's lists are the section's lists: transport, loading, unloading, handling, insurance; packing, commissions except the buying commission, containers, the assists' families. A cost the section does not name is not added because a tariff practice adds it, or because another country's rules add it — the additions are the section's, and the file's build cites each added line to its paragraph. The spec's C4.1 discipline applies to the additions as to every statutory statement: the provision is named, and the named provision is the authority. An addition the file cannot cite is an addition the file cannot make — and the learner who invents a cost line has made the valuation's first fabrication.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: Storage costs within the country stay out
BODY:
Section 72(3) provides the exclusion from the other end: where costs for storage or usual forms of handling have been incurred within the country in respect of goods placed under a customs procedure, they are not taken into account for the calculation of the amount of duty. The provision's logic is the suspense regime's: the costs of the goods' controlled wait are the procedure's costs, not the goods' value. The learner's discrimination: the international transport and insurance that brought the goods in are additions (section 67(3)); the storage and handling after arrival under the procedure are excluded (section 72(3)). The two provisions are the same valuation's two ends — the journey added, the wait excluded — and the file that cites both is the file that builds and excludes correctly.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
An importer's buying commission of 300 dollars was paid to the importer's purchasing agent. Which option best states whether the commission is added to the customs value?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. It is added, because all commissions are part of the price.
2. It is not added: section 67(4)(b) adds commissions and brokerage "except the buying commission", and section 151 defines the buying commission as the fee paid by the importer to the importer's agent for representing the importer in the purchase — the buyer's own purchasing representation stays out.
3. It is added only if the purchasing agent is foreign.
4. It is not added because commissions are always excepted.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 67(4)(b) of Act 891 adds commissions and brokerage incurred by the buyer but not already included in the price — with the express exception of the buying commission — and section 151 defines the buying commission as the fee paid by the importer to the importer's agent for the service of representing the importer in the purchase of the goods being valued. The exception is the definition's own: the buyer's purchasing representation is a cost of buying, not a cost of the goods.

Answer 1 reads "commissions and brokerage" without the exception — the exception is in the provision's text. Answer 3 invents a nationality test for the agent; the test is whose agent and for what service. Answer 4 overcorrects into a general exception: the selling commission — defined as fees paid to the seller's agent for selling services — is added, and the definition's split is the placement.

The takeaway: the two commission definitions are the placement's text — the buyer's purchasing fee excepted, the seller's selling fee added — and the file's evidence is whose agent the fee paid.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
The buyer supplied the producer with moulds and design drawings for the export production of the goods, free of charge. Which option best states the treatment under section 67(4)(d)?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Nothing is added, because the buyer's generosity is not a payment.
2. The moulds and designs are assists — goods and services supplied by the buyer free of charge for use in the production or sale for export of the imported goods — and their value, apportioned as appropriate, is added: the tools, dies and moulds family, and the engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans family.
3. Only the moulds are added; design drawings are never value.
4. The assists are added only if the seller invoices them.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 67(4)(d) of Act 891 adds the value, apportioned as appropriate, of goods and services supplied directly or indirectly by the buyer free of charge or at a reduced cost for use in connection with the production or sale for export of the imported goods — and its families are exactly the scenario's: materials, components and parts incorporated in the goods; tools, dies and moulds; materials consumed in the production; and engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans and sketches undertaken outside the country necessary for the production. The moulds are the tools-and-dies family; the drawings are the design family.

Answer 1 mistakes the absence of an invoice for the absence of value — the assists' whole purpose is to capture value supplied without charge. Answer 3 splits the families against the provision's list, which names the design work expressly. Answer 4 invents a seller-invoice condition; the condition is the buyer's free-or-reduced-cost supply for the production.

The takeaway: the assists capture the buyer's contribution to the making — free of charge is the trigger, the families are the list, and the apportionment is the provision's own rule.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
An invoice is in dollars, and the customs value must be determined in cedis. Which option best states the conversion rule under Act 891?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The trader's bank's rate on the invoice date.
2. The invoice's stated rate.
3. Section 69: the Commissioner-General shall use the current rate of exchange determined by the Bank of Ghana — the source is fixed, the rate is stated in the file, and the worked examples' figures are labelled illustrative per spec C4.3.
4. The rate is whichever the declarant declares, because the declarant determines the duty.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 69 of Act 891 provides: where the conversion of currency is necessary for the determination of the customs value, the Commissioner-General shall use the current rate of exchange determined by the Bank of Ghana. The provision fixes the rate's source — the Bank of Ghana's determination — and the learner's file states the rate used, with its source, for the conversion the value build performed.

Answer 1 substitutes the trader's bank for the statutory source. Answer 2 lets the invoice fix the rate — the invoice states the commercial terms, and the conversion provision states the customs rate. Answer 4 confuses section 72(1)'s acceptance of the declarant's determined duty with a free choice of exchange rate — the declarant determines on the rules applicable, and section 69 is one of them.

The takeaway: the conversion's source is the Bank of Ghana's determined rate — stated in the file, never remembered, and the illustrative figures carry their label.
