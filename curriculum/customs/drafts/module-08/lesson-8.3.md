=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The charge stack, as the sources present it
BODY:
A home-use clearance settles more than one charge, and the sources present the stack's structure together. The scan's extracted tax-element table — the FULL DUTY CALCULATIONS source's first page — shows the structure with its bases: import duty, on the applicable HS CIF; import VAT, on the CIF plus the import duty; the processing fee, on the CIF; the ECOWAS levy, on the CIF.

The structure's teaching point is the bases: import duty sits on the CIF — the cost, insurance and freight build — while the import VAT sits on the duty-inclusive base, CIF plus the duty already charged. The other charges each sit on their own base. The learner's discipline: every charge is stated with its base, and the base is what the arithmetic multiplies — Module 8.4's worked example runs the stack line by line.

The stack's figures are the sources' — and every rate the learner quotes carries the spec's C4.2 label: illustrative of the structure, verify the rate in force. The structure is the lesson's; the rates are the schedule's.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Where each charge comes from in the Act
BODY:
The stack's provisions are the Act's own, and each charge traces to one.

The imposition: section 1(1) imposes duties and taxes on prescribed goods imported or exported at the Harmonised System rate; subsection (2)(a) adds that the imposed duties and taxes may include any other tax, duty or levy on goods prescribed under any other enactment — the provision that carries the levies. The exemptions: section 2 — duties and taxes are not chargeable on exempt goods specified in the Harmonised System; the persons, organisations and institutions in the respective Schedules are exempt; a removal article is exempt from duty.

The liability: section 71 provides when a person incurs a customs liability — the goods entered for consumption and the declaration accepted; the goods unlawfully introduced; the goods unlawfully removed; the failure to comply with a procedure's conditions; the free-zone goods used other than as permitted. The calculation: section 72 — the duty determined on the rules applicable at the time the liability was incurred, taking into account the customs value, quantity, nature and origin of the goods.

The learner's trace: every charge in the stack names its provision, and the trace is the file's first discipline.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Liability: when the obligation begins
BODY:
Section 71(1) states the moments a customs liability is incurred, and the list is the lifecycle's legal anchors.

(a) Imported goods subject to duties are entered for consumption by that person and the declaration is accepted — the lodgement-and-acceptance moment. (b) Goods are unlawfully introduced into the country by that person. (c) Goods are unlawfully removed from a customs-controlled area or a free zone to another part of the country. (d) That person fails to comply with the conditions governing the use of a customs procedure the goods were placed under. (e) Goods imported in a free zone or customs-controlled area are used, consumed or lost other than as permitted.

Subsection (2) fixes the payers: the importer or the importer's agent for the entered goods; the person who imported or aided the unlawful introduction; and so on through the list's cases.

The learner's reading: liability begins at the entry's acceptance — before payment, before release — and the unlawful cases incur it without any entry at all. The deferral procedures' goods incur liability on their own moments, which is why their security matters.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Calculation: the rules at the moment
BODY:
Section 72 provides the calculation's rules, and its disciplines are specific.

Subsection (2): a person determines the amount of duty on the basis of the rules for calculation applicable at the time the customs liability was incurred — the moment's rules, not the clearance date's. Subsection (3): where storage or usual-handling costs have been incurred within the country for goods under a customs procedure, they are not taken into account — the wait's costs stay out of the value. Subsection (4): a package or covering is considered a separate article where, in the Commissioner-General's opinion, it is not the usual or proper package for the goods, or is designed for a separate use. Subsection (5): the customs value, quantity, nature and origin of the goods are taken into account in the calculation.

The learner's reading: the calculation takes the four inputs — value, quantity, nature, origin — and excludes the domestic wait's costs. Each discipline is a provision, and the file cites them.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The exemptions: the stack's zero rows
BODY:
Section 2 provides the exemptions, and the learner should hold them as the stack's zero rows.

Subsection (1): duties and taxes are not chargeable on exempt goods specified in the Harmonised System. Subsection (2): the persons, organisations and institutions specified in the respective Schedules are exempt from the payment of duties and taxes. Subsection (3): a removal article is exempt from duty — and section 151's removal-article definition is long and precise, with its own exclusions: arms, ammunition, motor vehicles, spare parts and building materials are not removal articles, whatever their owner's intentions.

The exemptions' discipline: the exempt goods are specified in the schedule — looked up, not recited; the exempt persons are in the Schedules — verified, not assumed; and the removal article is a defined category with its own exclusions, quoted from the definition. The zero row is as much a row as any rate row, and the file's trace names it.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
On which base is the import VAT computed, as the sources' tax-element table shows?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. On the FOB value alone.
2. On the CIF plus the import duty — the duty-inclusive base.
3. On the CIF alone, the same as the import duty's base.
4. On the goods' selling price in the market.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The FULL DUTY CALCULATIONS source's extracted tax-element table states the stack's bases: import duty on the applicable HS CIF; import VAT on the CIF plus the import duty; the processing fee on the CIF; the ECOWAS levy on the CIF. The import VAT's base is duty-inclusive — the customs value plus the duty already charged — which is the structure's teaching point: the VAT's base includes the duty.

Answer 1 misstates the base as FOB; the table's base is the CIF — the cost, insurance and freight build. Answer 3 halves the structure: the VAT's base is not the VAT's own base — it is the duty-inclusive one, and the difference is the stack's teaching point. Answer 4 substitutes a market price that no customs base uses.

The takeaway: the bases are the stack's structure — duty on the CIF, VAT on the duty-inclusive base, the other charges on theirs — and every base is stated before the arithmetic runs.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
Which of the following is NOT a moment at which section 71(1) provides that a person incurs a customs liability?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Imported goods subject to duties are entered for consumption by that person and the declaration is accepted.
2. Imported goods are unlawfully introduced into the country by that person.
3. The person's goods are inspected and found conforming at the border.
4. That person fails to comply with the conditions governing the use of a customs procedure the goods were placed under.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 71(1) of Act 891 lists the liability moments: imported goods subject to duties entered for consumption and the declaration accepted; goods unlawfully introduced into the country; goods unlawfully removed from a customs-controlled area or free zone; the failure to comply with the conditions governing a customs procedure's use; and goods in a free zone or customs-controlled area used, consumed or lost other than as permitted. A conforming inspection is none of these — it is the control decision that precedes release.

Answers 1, 2 and 4 restate the provision's own moments: the entry-and-acceptance, the unlawful introduction, and the procedure-condition failure.

The takeaway: liability begins at the entry's acceptance and runs through the unlawful cases — the conforming inspection is the control, not a liability moment — and the learner reads the list as the provision states it.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
A client claims his goods are exempt from duty "because they are for my personal use". Which option best states the exemption position under section 2?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The client is right: personal use is an exemption category.
2. The client is right if the goods are electrical appliances.
3. The exemptions are defined states: duties and taxes are not chargeable on exempt goods specified in the Harmonised System (s.2(1)); the persons, organisations and institutions specified in the respective Schedules are exempt (s.2(2)); and a removal article is exempt from duty (s.2(3)) — with the removal article's definition excluding arms, ammunition, motor vehicles, spare parts and building materials. Personal use alone is none of these; the client's goods must be looked up in the schedule.
4. The client is right if the goods arrive by post.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 2 of Act 891 states the exemptions in its own terms: subsection (1) — duties and taxes are not chargeable on exempt goods specified in the Harmonised System; subsection (2) — the persons, organisations and institutions specified in the respective Schedules are exempt; subsection (3) — a removal article is exempt from duty. The removal article's definition in section 151 is precise, and its exclusions are the definition's teeth: arms, ammunition, motor vehicles, spare parts and building materials are not removal articles, whatever their use.

Answer 1 invents a personal-use category the section does not carry. Answer 2 invents a goods-category exemption; the exemptions live in the schedule, not in a goods type's name. Answer 4 invents a postal carve-out; the postal shipment provisions (section 64's territory) govern procedure, not exemption.

The takeaway: the exemptions are looked up — the schedule's exempt goods, the Schedules' exempt persons, the defined removal article — and "personal use" is none of them until the schedule says so.
