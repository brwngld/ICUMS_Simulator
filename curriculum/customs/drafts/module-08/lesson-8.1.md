=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Transaction value: the price actually paid or payable
BODY:
Section 67(1) of Act 891 states the valuation's starting point: for customs purposes, customs value is the transaction value — the price actually paid or payable for goods imported into the country — except in cases where provision is specifically made for another method of determining customs value.

Subsection (2) defines the price's reach: the total payment made or to be made by the buyer to the seller, or by the buyer to a third party for the benefit of the seller, for the imported goods — and it includes all payments made or to be made as a condition of sale of the imported goods. The definition's width is its point: payments routed to a third party for the seller's benefit, and payments that are the sale's condition, are all inside the price — the trader cannot move value out of the declaration by moving the payment's destination.

Section 151's definition carries the term: the customs value of imported goods is the value of goods, determined in accordance with the Act. The determination's method is section 67's transaction value — or, where that cannot be used, the hierarchy of section 68.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The hierarchy: the sequential fallback methods
BODY:
Section 68(1) states what happens where the transaction value cannot be determined: the customs value shall be determined by proceeding sequentially through the secondary methods.

(a) The transaction value of identical goods sold for export to this country and exported within three months of the date of export of the goods being valued. (b) The transaction value of similar goods sold for export to this country and exported within three months. (c) The deductive value — based on the unit price at which the imported goods, or identical or similar imported goods, are sold within the country in the greatest aggregate quantity to persons not related to the seller, at or about the date of importation, reduced by the deductions the subsection lists: commissions; additions for profit and general expenses; international transport and insurance costs; domestic transport and insurance; and duties payable on importation. (d) The computed value — the sum of the cost or value of materials and manufacture, an amount for profit and general expenses, and the associated costs.

The methods are identical goods, similar goods, deductive, computed — in that order, sequentially.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The order is mandatory
BODY:
Section 68(1)'s word "sequentially" is the hierarchy's law: the methods are tried in the stated order, and the professional does not shop for the cheapest.

The order's logic is evidentiary: identical goods' transaction values are the closest evidence of the goods' own value; similar goods' are the next; the deductive method works back from the domestic sale; the computed value builds from the costs. Each step's evidence is further from the goods' own transaction than the step before — which is why the sequence runs as it does, and why skipping a step is a legal error rather than a preference.

The discipline for the trader and the agent: a valuation claim states which method is used and why the earlier methods cannot be — the sequential requirement means the file shows the walk, not just the landing. The officer's review tests exactly that: a computed value claimed where identical goods' values exist is a valuation that failed to walk.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: When transaction value fails
BODY:
Section 67(1)'s exception is where the learner's harder cases begin: the transaction value cannot be used where the provision is specifically made for another method — and the practice's cases are the ones where the price is not a price.

The classic failures: the price is restricted because the sale's conditions limit the buyer's disposal of the goods; the price is conditional — uncertain amounts contingent on something untested; the price reflects arrangements the buyer and seller have built between them rather than the goods' worth; or there is no sale at all — goods imported free of charge, consignment goods, goods for a branch. In each case, the price paid is not the transaction value the section contemplates, and the sequential methods of section 68 take over.

The learner's discipline: the failure is not assumed — it is established. The file shows why the transaction value cannot be used: the condition, the contingency, the absence of sale. The hierarchy's walk begins only when that showing is made, and the method used is the first available in the order.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: identical goods and similar goods: the fallback's comparators
BODY:
The first two fallback methods rest on section 151's defined comparators, and the definitions are the test.

"Identical goods" are goods which are the same in all respects, including physical characteristics, quality, and reputation, but disregarding minor differences in appearance. "Similar goods" are goods which, although not alike in all respects, have like characteristics and like component materials which enable them to perform the same functions and to be commercially interchangeable.

The fallback's use of them: the transaction value of identical goods — same in all respects — sold for export to Ghana within the three-month window; and where no identical goods' values exist, similar goods' — like characteristics, same functions, commercially interchangeable. The three-month window is the provision's own: values outside it do not anchor the fallback, because the market the values speak for is the market at the goods' export date.

The learner's discrimination: identical is a defined state, tested by all-respects sameness; similar is its defined fallback — and the difference decides which method's evidence the valuation uses.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The computed value: the build from costs
BODY:
Section 68(1)(d)'s computed value is the hierarchy's furthest fallback, and its shape is the goods' cost structure rebuilt: the sum of the cost or value of materials and manufacture or other processing employed in the production of the goods for import, an amount for profit and general expenses equal to the amount the subsection frames, and the associated costs.

The computed value's evidence is the producer's side: the materials, the manufacture, the profit and general expenses that a producer in the exporting country would carry. Its use is the last resort's logic — the domestic sale's deductions (the deductive method) cannot be had, and the producer's costs are the remaining evidence of value.

The learner's discipline: the computed value is built from sourced figures — the producer's costs documented, the profit and general expenses per the subsection's frame — and every figure is cited. A computed value assembled from estimates is not the method's answer; it is the method's failure, dressed.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A transaction value accepted, and a fallback illustrated
BODY:
Step 1. The simple case. A consignment of tiles is declared at its invoice price: 43,200 dollars, CIF Tema, sold at arm's length, no conditions restricting the buyer's disposal. The transaction value is the price actually paid or payable — section 67(1)-(2) — and the customs value is built on it: the additions of section 67(3)-(4) applied (the next lesson's work), and the value established.

Step 2. The harder case, illustrated. A different consignment — goods supplied free of charge by a foreign parent to its Ghanaian branch. No sale exists: no price was paid or payable. The transaction value cannot be determined, and section 68's walk begins.

Step 3. The walk's first steps. The agent searches for identical goods — goods the same in all respects — sold for export to Ghana within three months of the export date. The search finds none. Similar goods — like characteristics, same functions, commercially interchangeable — sold for export within the window: none found.

Step 4. The deductive method. The identical or similar goods' domestic sale prices — in the greatest aggregate quantity, to unrelated buyers, at about the importation date — are gathered, and the subsection's deductions applied: commissions, profit and general expenses, the international transport and insurance, the domestic transport, the duties payable.

Step 5. The computed value, if needed. Where the domestic sales cannot be had, the computed value builds from the producer's costs — and the file cites the producer's figures for each element.

What does this example demonstrate? The transaction value is the valuation's start; where it fails — and the failure is shown — the sequential walk begins, and each method's evidence is cited.

Key lesson: the walk is shown, not asserted — the file names why each earlier method failed and where the landed method's evidence came from.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: The identical-goods fallback in practice, abroad
BODY:
Step 1. The scene. At a European administration, a consignment of fittings is imported free of charge from a parent company — the classic no-sale case. The transaction value cannot be determined.

Step 2. The identical-goods search. The administration's records show identical fittings — same in all respects — sold for export to that country within three months of the goods' export date. The transaction value of those identical goods becomes the customs value: the first fallback, landed.

Step 3. The window's force. The administration tests the window: the identical goods' exports must fall within three months of the valued goods' export date. A matching sale from six months earlier would not anchor the fallback — the provision's window is its own.

Step 4. The transfer. Ghana's section 68(1)(a) carries the same structure: identical goods, the three-month window, the transaction value. The learner who sees the foreign fallback's logic has seen section 68(1)(a) working — the definitions and the window do the deciding.

What does this example demonstrate? The first fallback lands on defined comparators inside a defined window — and the window is as much the rule as the comparators are.

Key lesson: identical is defined, the window is stated, and both are checked before the fallback's value is used.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The order is the law — no method shopping
BODY:
Section 68(1)'s "proceeding sequentially" makes the hierarchy's order mandatory: identical, similar, deductive, computed — in that order, each tried before the next. The prohibition this implies is method shopping: a valuation that jumps to the computed value because the identical-goods search is inconvenient, or to the deductive method because its result is lower, has not followed the section — it has followed a preference, and the preference is visible in the skipped steps. The learner's file discipline: every fallback valuation states why each earlier method failed to produce a value, with the evidence of the search — the identical-goods search's scope and dates, the similar-goods search's criteria — so the walk is shown, not asserted. The officer's review tests the walk; the walk's showing is the valuation's justification — and a skipped step is a skipped provision, findable in one read.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of goods is imported free of charge by a parent company to its Ghanaian branch. Which option best states the valuation position under Act 891?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The goods' value is zero, because nothing was paid.
2. The transaction value is the parent's production cost, declared as such.
3. No sale exists — no price was paid or payable — so the transaction value cannot be determined, and section 68's secondary methods apply sequentially: identical goods' transaction values, then similar goods', then the deductive value, then the computed value.
4. The goods take the branch's selling price as the customs value.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 67(1) of Act 891 defines the customs value as the transaction value — the price actually paid or payable — except where another method is specifically provided. A free-of-charge supply has no price: no payment was made or to be made by the buyer to the seller or a third party for the seller's benefit, so the transaction value cannot be determined, and section 68(1) directs the sequential walk through the secondary methods — identical goods, similar goods, deductive, computed.

Answer 1 mistakes the absence of price for the absence of value — the goods still have a customs value, determined by the fallback methods. Answer 2 invents a production-cost transaction value that the section does not carry; the producer's costs are the computed value's inputs, reached sequentially. Answer 4 substitutes the branch's selling price for the method's route; the deductive method's domestic-sale approach is the provision's, and it runs when the identical and similar searches fail.

The takeaway: no sale, no transaction value — and the hierarchy's walk is the law's answer, shown step by step in the file.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
The first fallback method under section 68(1)(a) applies where identical goods were sold for export to Ghana. Which option best states the provision's window?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Identical goods exported at any time before the valued goods.
2. Identical goods exported within three months of the date of export of the goods being valued.
3. Identical goods exported within the same calendar year.
4. Identical goods exported whenever the exporter chooses to rely on them.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 68(1)(a) of Act 891 provides the first secondary method: the transaction value of identical goods sold for export to this country and exported within three months of the date of export of the goods being valued. The window is the provision's own — three months from the valued goods' export date — and it exists because the fallback's evidence must speak for the market at the relevant time: values from years past are values from a different market.

Answer 1 removes the window — the provision states it expressly. Answer 3 stretches a three-month window into a calendar year. Answer 4 hands the window to the trader's choice; the window is statutory, and the trader's reliance does not extend it.

The takeaway: the fallback's comparators must be inside the three-month window — and "identical" is tested by the definition, while "within three months" is tested by the dates.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
A valuation skips the identical-goods and similar-goods fallbacks and applies the computed value directly, because the computed result is lower. Which option best evaluates the approach?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The approach is correct: the lowest lawful value is the customs value.
2. The approach is correct where identical goods cannot be found in three months.
3. The approach is unlawful: section 68(1) requires the customs value to be determined by proceeding sequentially through the methods — the identical-goods and similar-goods fallbacks must be tried (and their failure shown) before the deductive and computed values; skipping the order is method shopping, not valuation.
4. The approach is correct where the trader and the officer agree.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 68(1) of Act 891 provides that where the transaction value cannot be determined, the customs value shall be determined by proceeding sequentially through the secondary methods — identical goods, similar goods, deductive, computed. The word "sequentially" is the hierarchy's law: each method is tried in its order, and the file shows why each earlier method failed. A computed value applied while the earlier fallbacks were untried — chosen because its result is lower — has not walked the section; it has shopped the methods.

Answer 1 invents a lowest-value rule that the section's sequence refutes — the lawful value is the first method's result, not the cheapest. Answer 2 half-states the test: the failure to find identical goods must be shown, and the walk continues only to the next method — not to the end. Answer 4 substitutes agreement for the provision; the sequence is statutory, and it binds the officer too.

The takeaway: the hierarchy's order is the law — the walk is shown step by step, and the skipped steps are skipped provisions.
