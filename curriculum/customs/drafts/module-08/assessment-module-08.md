=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 8.1 ===
QUESTION:
An importer declares a consignment whose price to the seller is 30,000 dollars, but the importer also paid 5,000 dollars directly to the seller's design consultant as a condition of the sale. Which option best states the transaction value under section 67(2)?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. 30,000 dollars — payments to third parties are outside the transaction value.
2. 35,000 dollars — section 67(2) defines the price actually paid or payable as the total payment made or to be made by the buyer to the seller, or by the buyer to a third party for the benefit of the seller, and includes all payments made as a condition of sale; the design payment is inside.
3. 30,000 dollars, with the 5,000 dollars treated as a buying commission excepted from the value.
4. 5,000 dollars, because the design payment is the only conditional one.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 67(2) of Act 891 defines the price actually paid or payable as the total payment made or to be made by the buyer to the seller, or by the buyer to a third party for the benefit of the seller, for the imported goods — and it includes all payments made or to be made as a condition of sale of the imported goods. The scenario's design payment is a payment to a third party for the seller's benefit, made as a condition of the sale: both routes of the definition place it inside.

Answer 1 narrows the payment to the seller's own invoice; the definition's third-party route is express. Answer 3 misapplies the buying-commission exception — section 67(4)(b) excepts the fee paid to the importer's own purchasing representative, not a payment to the seller's consultant as a condition of sale; and section 151's buying-commission definition requires the importer's agent representing the importer in the purchase. Answer 4 inverts the base.

The takeaway: the transaction value is the total payment for the goods, by whatever route and on whatever condition — the definition's width is what stops value moving out of the declaration by moving the payment's destination.

=== ASSESSMENT Q2 | tests lesson 8.1 ===
QUESTION:
A valuation skips section 68's identical-goods and similar-goods fallbacks and applies the deductive value directly, asserting that "the domestic sales are the best evidence anyway". Which option best evaluates the approach?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The approach is correct: the deductive method is the most reliable fallback.
2. The approach is unlawful: section 68(1) requires the customs value to be determined by proceeding sequentially through the secondary methods — identical goods, similar goods, deductive, computed — and the file must show why each earlier method failed before the next is used; skipping the order is method shopping.
3. The approach is correct if the trader writes down the skipped steps.
4. The approach is correct because the deductive method's deductions make it the lowest.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 68(1) of Act 891 provides that where the customs value cannot be determined on the basis of transaction value, it shall be determined by proceeding sequentially through the secondary methods: the transaction value of identical goods, then of similar goods, then the deductive value, then the computed value. The word "sequentially" is the hierarchy's law — each method is tried in its order, and the file shows why each earlier method failed before the next is used.

Answer 1 substitutes reliability for the order — the provision's sequence is the law, whatever each method's evidentiary merits. Answer 3 mistakes writing for walking: the order is the walk itself, and the written note of skipped steps does not skip them lawfully. Answer 4 exposes the motive — the deductions' lowering effect is exactly why the sequence exists, and a lowest-result method choice is the method shopping the provision refuses.

The takeaway: the fallbacks are a sequence, not a menu — the walk is shown step by step, and the skipped steps are skipped provisions.

=== ASSESSMENT Q3 | tests lesson 8.1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader proposes a fallback comparison with goods that have "like characteristics and like component materials enabling the same functions and commercial interchangeability", but which differ from the valued goods in several respects. Which option best states which fallback comparator the goods match, and what the match means?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The goods match the identical-goods definition, and their value is used directly.
2. The goods match the similar-goods definition — section 151: goods not alike in all respects but with like characteristics and like component materials enabling the same functions and commercial interchangeability — so the comparison supports the similar-goods fallback of section 68(1)(b), which operates only after the identical-goods fallback of 68(1)(a) has been tried and failed.
3. The goods match no definition, because they differ in several respects.
4. The goods' match decides the customs value by itself.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines identical goods as goods the same in all respects, including physical characteristics, quality, and reputation, disregarding minor differences in appearance — and similar goods as goods which, although not alike in all respects, have like characteristics and like component materials which enable them to perform the same functions and to be commercially interchangeable. The scenario's goods — like characteristics, same functions, commercially interchangeable, differing in several respects — match the similar-goods definition. The match supports section 68(1)(b)'s fallback, which runs only after 68(1)(a)'s identical-goods method has been tried and failed.

Answer 1 claims the identical definition — the several differences refute it, and the definition disregards only minor differences in appearance. Answer 3 overreads the differences: "several respects" is the similar-goods definition's own territory. Answer 4 dissolves the sequence — the match supports a fallback, and the fallback runs in the order section 68 provides.

The takeaway: the definitions are the comparators' test — identical is all-respects sameness, similar is like-characteristics interchangeability — and the matched fallback still runs in the sequence's order.

=== ASSESSMENT Q4 | tests lesson 8.2 ===
QUESTION:
An FOB invoice prices goods at 25,000 dollars; the freight to Ghana was 1,800 dollars; the marine insurance was 150 dollars, declared by the importer. Which option best states the additions section 67(3) makes, and the resulting build?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. No additions: the FOB invoice is the customs value as lodged.
2. The additions are the transport cost (section 67(3)(a), 1,800 dollars) and the insurance (section 67(3)(c), 150 dollars, declared) — the loading and handling charges where incurred (s.67(3)(b)) — bringing the build to the base the charges compute on; the term told the build which lines the invoice omitted.
3. The additions are the invoice's 25,000 plus a flat 10 percent surcharge.
4. The additions depend on the shipping line's freight invoice, not on section 67(3).
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 67(3) of Act 891 adjusts the customs value to include: the cost of transport for importing the goods into the country (subsection (a) — the freight's 1,800 dollars); the loading, unloading and handling charges associated with that transport (subsection (b)); and the rate of insurance where the importer fails to declare the insurance paid (subsection (c) — here the importer declared the 150 dollars, and the declared figure enters). The FOB term told the build which lines the invoice omitted — the freight and insurance were the buyer's costs — and the additions bring them in.

Answer 1 mistakes the term for the value: the FOB invoice states where the price stops, and section 67(3) states where the value must reach. Answer 3 invents a surcharge that no provision carries — the additions are the section's named lines. Answer 4 confuses the commercial evidence (the freight invoice) with the statutory authority (section 67(3)(a)) — the addition is made because the provision says so, and the invoice evidences the figure.

The takeaway: the Incoterm tells the build what the invoice omitted; section 67(3) tells the build what the value must reach — and the additions' every line cites its paragraph.

=== ASSESSMENT Q5 | tests lesson 8.2 ===
QUESTION:
An importer paid two commissions: 400 dollars to her own purchasing agent for representing her in the purchase, and 600 dollars to the seller's agent for services connected with selling the goods. Which option best states the treatment under the Act's definitions?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both are added, because all commissions are part of the value.
2. Both are excepted, because commissions are the agents' incomes.
3. The 400 dollars buying commission is excepted — section 151 defines it as the fee paid by the importer to the importer's agent for representing the importer in the purchase — and the 600 dollars selling commission is added — section 151 defines it as fees paid to the seller's agent for selling services; section 67(4)(b) adds commissions and brokerage "except the buying commission".
4. Both are added only if the agents are licensed customs house agents.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 151 of Act 891 defines the two commissions in the placement's terms: a buying commission is a fee paid by the importer to the importer's agent for the service of representing the importer in the purchase of the goods being valued — the buyer's own purchasing representation — and a selling commission is fees paid to the agent of the seller for services performed in connection with selling the imported goods. Section 67(4)(b) adds commissions and brokerage incurred by the buyer but not already included in the price, excepting the buying commission. The 400 dollars is the excepted buying commission; the 600 dollars is the added selling commission.

Answer 1 reads section 67(4)(b) without its exception. Answer 2 overcorrects into a blanket exception — the selling commission's definition places it inside. Answer 4 invents a licence test; the placement's test is whose agent and for what service.

The takeaway: the two definitions are the placement's text — the buyer's purchasing fee out, the seller's selling fee in — and the file's evidence is whose agent the commission paid.

=== ASSESSMENT Q6 | tests lesson 8.2 ===
QUESTION:
The buyer supplied free-of-charge moulds and the engineering designs for the exported goods' production, and the goods' value must include the contribution. Which option best states the provision and its families?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Section 67(4)(d): the value, apportioned as appropriate, of goods and services supplied by the buyer free of charge or at reduced cost for use in the production or sale for export — its families being the materials and components incorporated; the tools, dies and moulds; the materials consumed in production; and the engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans undertaken outside the country necessary for the production.
2. Section 67(3)(a): the transport cost, apportioned.
3. Section 69: the moulds and designs converted at the exchange rate.
4. Section 2(3): the moulds and designs are removal articles and exempt.
CORRECT: 1
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 1. Section 67(4)(d) of Act 891 adds the value, apportioned as appropriate, of goods and services supplied directly or indirectly by the buyer free of charge or at a reduced cost for use in connection with the production or sale for export of the imported goods — and its families are the scenario's: (i) materials, components and parts incorporated in the goods; (ii) tools, dies and moulds — the moulds; (iii) materials consumed in the production; and (iv) engineering, development, artwork, design work and plans and sketches undertaken outside the country necessary for the production — the designs.

Answer 2 misplaces the assists in the transport addition. Answer 3 misplaces them in the currency conversion. Answer 4 misplaces them in the removal-article exemption — whose definition (section 151) excludes tools of trade of an agricultural, commercial or industrial character from removal articles anyway; the assists are a valuation addition, not an exemption.

The takeaway: the assists are section 67(4)(d)'s four families — the buyer's free-or-reduced-cost contributions to the production — apportioned as the provision states.

=== ASSESSMENT Q8 | tests lesson 8.3 ===
QUESTION:
Which of the following is NOT a moment at which section 71(1) provides that a customs liability is incurred?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Imported goods subject to duties are entered for consumption by that person and the declaration is accepted.
2. Goods are unlawfully removed from a customs-controlled area or a free zone to another part of the country.
3. The goods are examined and released, and the trader sells them in the market.
4. That person fails to comply with the conditions governing the use of a customs procedure the goods were placed under.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 71(1) of Act 891 lists the liability moments: imported goods subject to duties entered for consumption and the declaration accepted; goods unlawfully introduced into the country; goods unlawfully removed from a customs-controlled area or a free zone to another part of the country; the failure to comply with the conditions governing a customs procedure's use; and goods in a free zone or customs-controlled area used, consumed or lost other than as permitted. A lawful examination and release is the clearance working — the liability was incurred at the entry's acceptance, and the lawful sale afterwards is free circulation, not a new liability moment.

Answers 1, 2 and 4 restate the provision's own moments: the entry-and-acceptance, the unlawful removal, the procedure-condition failure.

The takeaway: the liability moments are the provision's list — the entry's acceptance is the ordinary one, the unlawful cases the extraordinary ones — and the lawful clearance's aftermath is free circulation, not liability.

=== ASSESSMENT Q9 | tests lesson 8.3 ===
QUESTION:
A returning resident imports goods that meet section 151's removal-article definition, and separately imports a spare engine for his vehicle. Which option best states the duty position under section 2(3)?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both are exempt: section 2(3) exempts all personal imports.
2. The goods meeting the removal-article definition are exempt from duty under section 2(3); the spare engine is not — the definition expressly excludes spare parts and building materials, along with arms, ammunition and motor vehicles.
3. Both are dutiable, because section 2(3) exempts only companies.
4. The engine is exempt and the removal articles are not, because engines are mechanically propelled.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 2(3) of Act 891 provides that a removal article is exempt from duty, and section 151 defines the removal article precisely: movable personal belongings or effects, domestic appliances, household provisions normally kept in stock, collectors pieces, pet animals and any tools of trade other than agricultural, commercial or industrial plant necessary for the exercise of the calling, trade or profession — but the definition expressly does not include arms, ammunition, motor vehicles, spare parts and building materials. The spare engine is a spare part: outside the definition, inside the duty.

Answer 1 invents a personal-imports category the section does not carry — the exemption's categories are the defined ones. Answer 3 invents a company-only exemption; the removal article is the returning resident's defined relief. Answer 4's mechanical reasoning has no basis — the definition's exclusions are its own list.

The takeaway: the removal article is a defined category with defined exclusions — the exemption is looked up in the definition, and the spare parts exclusion is its own line.
