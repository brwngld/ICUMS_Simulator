=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The vehicle machinery: the provisions mapped
BODY:
Act 891's motor-vehicle machinery runs from section 55 to section 61, and the learner's map is the sections' own subjects.

Section 55: the motor vehicle tax — the importer pays the import duty and any other imposts prescribed by law, unless the vehicle is exempted. Section 56: the duties of the motor vehicle licensing authority — the authority shall not register a motor vehicle unless the applicant produces the customs entry and the related customs documents; the Commissioner-General issues the Certificate of Payment of Duties. Section 57: the importation of a motor vehicle is as prescribed in the Schedule. Section 58: the prohibited motor vehicle — a right-hand steering vehicle shall not be imported unless approved by the Minister; the Commissioner-General shall seize it, and it is forfeited to the State. Section 59: the forfeiture of the overstayed vehicle — not entered and cleared within sixty days after the final discharge (or the overland crossing), forfeited and disposed of or sold "as is". Section 60: the valuation of the used vehicle — the section's own method, despite any other enactment. Section 61: the local dealer furnishes the manufacturer's details.

The map's discipline: the vehicle's questions are answered by the sections' own subjects — the tax by s.55, the registration by s.56, the importation by s.57, the prohibition by s.58, the overstay by s.59, the valuation by s.60, the dealer by s.61.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The definitions: motor vehicle and vehicle
BODY:
Section 151 defines the machinery's terms, and their discrimination is the map's first check.

A "motor vehicle" is a mechanically propelled vehicle for use on a road, other than a cycle, motor cycle, side-car or other similar vehicle. A "vehicle" is a conveyance for the transport by land of goods or persons — the wider term, covering the non-motorised conveyances the motor-vehicle provisions do not reach.

The discrimination's work: the motor-vehicle provisions (ss.55-61) apply to the mechanically propelled road vehicles — the imported cars, the used vehicles, the prohibited right-hand steering vehicles. The "vehicle" definition's wider reach covers the land conveyances generally — the trailers, the carts — and the provisions' applicability is read from the definitions. The learner's discipline: the goods' description is matched to the definitions before the provisions are applied — the motor vehicle's machinery reaches the motor vehicles, and the vehicle definition's reach is the machinery's boundary.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The prohibition and the overstay: sections 58 and 59
BODY:
Section 58(1) provides the prohibition: a person shall not import a right-hand steering motor vehicle into the country unless approved by the Minister. Subsection (2) provides the response: the Commissioner-General shall seize the prohibited motor vehicle, and it is forfeited to the State.

Section 59(1) provides the overstay's forfeiture: the imported motor vehicle not entered and cleared within sixty days after the final discharge of the ship or aircraft — or, for the overland import, the date it crossed the national borders — is forfeited to the State. Subsection (2): the Commissioner-General disposes of the forfeited vehicle or sells it on an "as is" basis. Subsection (3): the disposal's or sale's price includes the duty and taxes eligible on the vehicle.

The provisions' disciplines: the prohibition's condition is the Minister's approval; the overstay's clock is the sixty days from the discharge or the crossing — statutory deadlines, stated with their sections. The forfeiture's price's duty-inclusion mirrors the auction's (section 93(2)): the State's sale includes the duty.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The used vehicle's valuation: section 60's method
BODY:
Section 60 provides the used vehicle's valuation method, and its opening words state its priority: despite the provisions of any other enactment, this section applies in determining for customs purposes the value of a used motor vehicle imported under the Act.

Subsection (2): the used vehicle's value is the price of the vehicle as assessed in accordance with the section, together with the freight, insurance, commission and any other costs, charges and expenses incidental to the delivery of the vehicle at the port or place where it first entered the country. Subsection (3) begins the age tiers: where the vehicle's age does not exceed six months, the price is deemed to be the first purchase price — and the section's tiers continue for the older vehicles.

The method's teaching: the used vehicle's valuation is its own statutory method — the price assessed per the section's tiers, plus the incidental costs — and the general valuation hierarchy of section 67 yields to this section's express priority ("despite the provisions of any other enactment"). The learner's reading: the used vehicle's value is section 60's, and the section's tiers are its method — quoted from the Act, stated with the subsections.

QUESTION:
A trader imports a used vehicle and does not clear it within sixty days of the vessel's discharge. Which option best states the position under section 59?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:

3. The vehicle's forfeiture requires a court order first.
4. The sixty-day clock starts when the trader decides to clear the vehicle.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 59(1) of Act 891 provides the overstay's forfeiture: a person who imports a motor vehicle and does not enter and clear it within sixty days after the final discharge of the ship or aircraft — or, for the overland import, the date it crossed the borders — forfeits the vehicle to the State. Subsection (2) provides the Commissioner-General's disposal or the "as is" sale; subsection (3) provides that the disposal's or sale's price includes the duty and taxes eligible on the vehicle.

Answer 1 invents a consequence-free overstay the provision's deadline refutes. Answer 3 invents a court-order precondition the subsection does not state — the forfeiture's machinery is the section's own. Answer 4 inverts the clock: the sixty days run from the discharge or the crossing, not from the trader's decision.

The takeaway: the vehicle's overstay clock is the section's — sixty days from the discharge or the crossing — and the forfeiture's machinery, the disposal and the duty-inclusive price are the provision's own terms.
