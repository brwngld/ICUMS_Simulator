=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 13.1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's consignment of goods is on the import-prohibition list. The trader obtains a certificate from a private laboratory stating the goods are safe, and lodges it with the declaration. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The certificate cures the prohibition, because the goods are now evidenced as safe.
2. The certificate is irrelevant: the goods are prohibited goods within section 151's definition — importation prohibited by law — and no private certificate creates an allowance; the professional's check is the law's list, not a trader's hope.
3. The certificate works if it is issued abroad.
4. The certificate works if the trader declares the goods under a different description.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines prohibited goods as goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law — an absolute state, with no permit machinery behind it. The lesson's discrimination: prohibited means no lawful entry; restricted means lawful entry against a satisfied condition, evidenced by the controlling agency's allowance. The private laboratory's certificate is quality evidence, not a statutory allowance — the prohibition is not a safety condition; it is an exclusion.

Answer 1 mistakes quality evidence for a statutory allowance — the prohibition is not a condition; it is an exclusion. Answer 3 invents a geographic cure; the prohibition follows the importation. Answer 4 is the serious aggravation: describing the goods to evade the prohibition is a false statement — the offence machinery's subject.

The takeaway: the states' discrimination is the first check — prohibited means no route, restricted means the route's evidence — and the private certificate is neither the law's list nor the agency's allowance.

=== ASSESSMENT Q2 | tests lesson 13.1 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of folded woven goods arrives with pieces marked 9.144 metres; the measured length of one piece is 9.130 metres. Which option best states the position under sections 99 and 100?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The piece breaches section 99(1)(b)'s marking requirement and is forfeited.
2. The marking is compliant (the piece is marked with its metres), and the shortfall of 1.4 centimetres is within section 100(3)(a)(i)'s tolerance of 10.26 centimetres for pieces marked 9.144 metres or under — the penalty does not apply.
3. The shortfall exceeds the tolerance, and the goods are forfeited.
4. The tolerance applies only to goods above 21.03 metres.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 99(1) of Act 891 requires the folded woven goods to be folded in folds of not less than one metre, each piece marked with the number of metres and inches, and each piece stamped with a mark. Section 100(3)(a) states the tolerance table: a shortfall of not more than 10.26 centimetres in a piece marked 9.144 metres or under does not attract the penalty. The scenario's piece — marked 9.144 metres, measured 9.130 metres — has a 1.4-centimetre shortfall: within the tolerance.

Answer 1 conflates the marking requirement (the piece IS marked) with the length shortfall (within the tolerance). Answer 3 misstates the tolerance — 1.4 centimetres is well inside the 10.26-centimetre allowance. Answer 4 misreads the tolerance table — 21.03 metres is the highest tier's boundary, not the only tier.

The takeaway: the machinery's tolerances are stated to the centimetre — and the learner's check applies the tolerance table to the measured shortfall, piece by piece.
