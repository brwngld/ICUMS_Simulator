HEADING: The health regulator's statutory categories, quoted
BODY:

The deck's mandate slide adds the section 82 functions: ensuring adequate and effective standards for the regulated products; monitoring compliance through the District Assemblies; advising the Minister.

The learner's discipline: the categories are the statute's own words, quoted from the deck — and the goods are read into the families from the statute's list, not extended by guesswork. A product outside the families follows its own regulator's statute.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A pharmaceutical consignment's agency threads
BODY:
Step 1. The categories read. A consignment of pharmaceuticals arrives: the goods are read into the statute's categories — drugs, under section 118(1)'s registration regime. The category's condition: the product's registration by the Authority.

Step 2. The registration's evidence. The product's FDA registration number is in the file — the registration obtained before the importation (the deck's general requirement). The evidence's check: the registration number in full, matching the consignment's products.

Step 3. The permit's evidence. The eMDA — the electronic permit secured in ICUMS before import, per the deck's instruction — is in the file: the consignment's allowance evidenced. The permit's details (the product's name, the manufacturer, the quantities) match the consignment.

Step 4. The inspection's findings. The goods' inspection finds them conforming: the products' registration, labelling and condition meet the regime's requirements. The non-conforming finding would detain the goods (the deck's rule) — the conforming finding proceeds.

Step 5. The release. Section 6(1)'s conditions assemble: examination done, no offence, documents acquired (including the FDA's evidence), stated conditions met. The goods are released into the licensed storage's custody.

What does this example demonstrate? The regulated product's clearance is the home-use machinery with the agency's threads — the registration, the permit, the conformity's finding — and the release assembles every thread's closure.

Key lesson: the threads' evidence is the file's; the release waits for every thread; and the categories' reading is where the threads are found.

HEADING: A labelling refusal abroad, and Ghana's fields
BODY:

Step 3. Ghana's fields. Ghana's labelling rules — LI 1541's fields as the standards deck quotes them — state the fields Ghana's border checks: the product's name, the instructions for use, the manufacturer's name and address, the country of origin, the date of manufacture, the batch or lot number. What does this example demonstrate? The labels are border evidence in every regime that requires them — and the fields' review happens at the factory, because the border's label check is not a negotiating step.

Key lesson: the fields are the check's checklist — Ghana's or the destination's — and the review before shipment is the check's preparation.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of herbal medicinal products arrives. Which option best states the product's regulatory condition under the deck's quoted provisions?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:

2. The product is in section 118(1)'s registration regime: a person shall not import a herbal medicinal product unless the article has been registered by the Authority — the registration is the condition, obtained before the importation.
3. The product's condition is the Ghana Standards Authority's conformity certificate.
4. The product's condition is the trader's discretion, because herbal products are traditional.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The FDA deck quotes section 118(1) of Act 851 in the statute's own words: a person shall not manufacture, prepare, import, export, distribute, sell, supply or exhibit for sale a drug, herbal medicinal product, cosmetic, medical device or household chemical substance unless the article has been registered by the Authority. Answer 1 abandons the quoted list — the herbal medicinal product is named in it. Answer 3 misassigns the regime: the standards thread's conformity is additional where it applies, and the registration condition is the quoted section's. Answer 4 invents a traditional-products discretion the quoted section's list does not carry.

The takeaway: the categories are the statute's own words — the herbal medicinal product is in section 118(1)'s list, and the registration is its condition, obtained before the importation.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
An FDA-regulated consignment's registration and eMDA are in order, but the border inspection finds the products non-conforming. Which option best states the deck's rule and the clearance's position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The goods are released, because the documentary thread was complete.
2. The goods are detained for further regulatory actions — the deck's own rule for non-conforming products — and the release under section 6(1) waits, because the stated conditions include the conformity the inspection has just found wanting.
3. The goods are released with a labelling correction to be made in the market.
4. The registration is cancelled automatically, and the goods are confiscated.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The deck's general-requirements slide states the response in its own words: all non-conforming products shall be detained for further regulatory actions. The scenario's documentary thread was complete — the registration and the eMDA — but the inspection's conformity finding is the FDA thread's own control, and its failure detains the goods. Section 6(1)'s release conditions are conjunctive: the stated conditions include the regime's evidence being in order, and the detention is the thread's open state.

Answer 1 lets the documentary thread override the conformity finding — the inspection exists to catch exactly this. Answer 3 defers the condition into the market, which the detention's machinery and the release's conjunctive conditions both refuse. Answer 4 invents an automatic cancellation and confiscation that the deck does not state — the detention is "for further regulatory actions", whose content is the agency's machinery.

The takeaway: the FDA thread's inspection is a real control — the non-conforming finding detains under the deck's own rule, and the customs release waits for the thread's resolution.
