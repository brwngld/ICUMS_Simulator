=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The three states, defined in the Act's own words
BODY:
The border's goods question has three lawful answers, and section 151's definitions state them. "Prohibited goods" means goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law. "Restricted goods" means goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law. Between the two sits the conditionally permitted case: the goods whose entry the law allows against a condition — the permit, the licence, the registration — evidenced before release.

The states' discrimination is the first professional check: a prohibited good has no route — no permit cures it; a restricted good's route is the condition's satisfaction, evidenced; a conditionally permitted good is the restricted good whose condition is its route. The learner's discipline: the goods' state is looked up in the law's list — the Schedules and the provisions — never assumed from the goods' name or the trader's hope.

The machinery that follows — the offences (ss.98-100), the seizure (s.124), the disposal (s.126) — operates on the states: the prohibited good's importation is the offence; the restricted good's unconditional entry is the breach; and the conditionally permitted good's unevidenced entry is the file's gap.

BODY:

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's consignment of goods is on the import-prohibition list. The trader obtains a certificate from a private laboratory stating the goods are safe, and lodges the certificate with the declaration. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The certificate cures the prohibition, because the goods are now evidenced as safe.
2. The certificate is irrelevant: the goods are prohibited goods within section 151's definition — importation prohibited by law — and no private certificate creates an allowance; the professional's check is the law's list, not a trader's hope.
3. The certificate works if it is issued abroad.
4. The certificate works if the trader declares the goods under a different description.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines prohibited goods as goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law — an absolute state, with no permit machinery behind it. The lesson's discrimination: prohibited means no lawful entry; restricted means lawful entry against a satisfied condition, evidenced by the controlling agency's allowance. The private laboratory's certificate is quality evidence, not a statutory allowance — the prohibition is not a safety condition; it is an exclusion.

Answer 1 mistakes quality evidence for a statutory allowance. Answer 3 invents a geographic cure; the prohibition follows the importation. Answer 4 is the serious aggravation: describing the goods to evade the prohibition is a false statement — the offence machinery's subject.

The takeaway: the states' discrimination is the first check — prohibited means no route, restricted means the route's evidence — and the private certificate is neither the law's list nor the agency's allowance.
