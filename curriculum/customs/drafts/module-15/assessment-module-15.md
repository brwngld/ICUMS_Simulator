=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 15.1 ===
QUESTION:
Which option best states the relationship between the agent's licence and the agent's written authority?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The licence is the authority: one paper serves both purposes.
2. The licence authorises the profession (section 43's grant to carry out customs business), and the written authority authorises the engagement (section 44's paper for the specific act) — the two papers' jobs are distinct, and the officer's demand under section 44 is answered with the engagement's paper.
3. The licence is the engagement's authority; the written authority is the profession's.
4. The two papers are interchangeable, and either satisfies section 44's demand.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's two-papers discipline states the separation: section 43's licence grants the profession's authorisation — the person licensed to carry out customs business, per section 151's definition — and section 44's written authority authorises the specific engagement: the principal's instruction for the act performed on the principal's behalf. The officer's demand under section 44 is answered with the engagement's paper, because the demand's question is "did this principal instruct this lodgement?" — and the licence answers a different question.

Answer 1 merges the two papers — the merge is the confusion the section exists to prevent. Answer 3 reverses the papers' jobs. Answer 4 invents an interchangeability that the sections' distinct requirements refute — the licence is the profession's entry; the authority is the engagement's authorisation.

The takeaway: the licence and the authority are two papers with two jobs — the profession's entry and the engagement's authorisation — and the officer's demand is answered with the engagement's paper.
