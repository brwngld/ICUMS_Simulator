=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The advance ruling: the prospective instrument
BODY:
Section 12's advance ruling is the dispute management's prospective instrument, and the learner holds it from lesson 7.3's teaching with the dispute's additions.

The definition (section 151): a binding written statement issued by customs that interprets and applies customs laws and regulations to a specific set of facts upon application by a trade about a prospective transaction. The provision (section 12(1)-(3)): the Commissioner-General's written ruling applying the customs law to a particular set of facts submitted by an interested party — the interested party being the person or authorised agent with a demonstrable interest, and the importer or exporter; the ruling's subjects being the tariff classification, the customs value, the country of origin, or any other activity to which the customs law applies.

The ruling's force is its boundary: binding on the stated facts, prospective in application. The dispute management's use: the trader's uncertainty about a future transaction's treatment is the ruling's subject — and the ruling bought before the goods ship is the certainty the border's assessment cannot retroactively give.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The objection: the retrospective instrument
BODY:
Section 13 provides the objection — the dispute's retrospective instrument — and its provisions state the objectable decisions, the persons and the contents.

Subsection (1) names the objectable decisions: the assessment of duty, including the underlying findings and determinations as to the customs value, the tariff classification, the country of origin, the rate and amount of duty assessed, and the person liable; the exclusion of goods as prohibited; the refusal to pay a drawback claim; the refusal to refund or remit duty; and a customs advance ruling which is binding. Subsection (2) names the objecting persons: the importer or exporter, the person whose liability or refund right is affected, the drawback claimant, and the authorised agent.

The objection's contents (subsection (3)): the name and address of the objecting person; the number and date of the entry involved; the date and nature of the decision being objected; and the description of the grounds.

The learner's reading: the objection's subjects are the assessment's findings — the value, the classification, the origin, the rate, the person — and the objection's contents are the decision's identification and the grounds' statement.
