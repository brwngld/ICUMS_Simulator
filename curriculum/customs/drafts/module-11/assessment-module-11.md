=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 11.1 ===
QUESTION:
A forwarder moves goods from Tema through Ghana to a landlocked buyer, and separately tranships a container from a feeder vessel to an ocean vessel in Tema's harbour. Which option best states the procedures each movement takes?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both are transit under section 95, because both goods leave Ghana.
2. Both are transhipment under section 96, because both goods enter Ghana's ports.
3. The inland movement is transit under section 95(1) — goods moved from one port of entry to another under a bond or by a bonded carrier, or under the TIR and ATA conventions — and the harbour transfer is transhipment under section 96(1): goods transferred from an importing conveyance to an exporting conveyance within the same port of entry, without payment of duty.
4. Both movements require home-use entries, because the goods are in Ghana.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 95(1) of Act 891 provides the transit procedure for goods moved from one port of entry to another port within the country — the corridor movement — under a bond or by a bonded carrier, or both, without payment of duty, or under the TIR and ATA conventions the subsection names. Section 96(1) provides transhipment for goods transferred from an importing conveyance to an exporting conveyance within the same port of entry, without payment of duty. The geography identifies the procedure: cross-country is transit; same-port conveyance-to-conveyance is transhipment.

Answer 1 and Answer 2 each collapse the two procedures into one — the sections' geographies differ. Answer 4 dissolves the procedures' design: the goods' non-entry into commerce is what the transit and transhipment procedures provide, and the home-use entry would defeat it.

The takeaway: the geography identifies the procedure — the corridor's bond and acquittal, the port's transfer and responsibility — and the goods' movement tells the learner which section applies.

=== ASSESSMENT Q2 | tests lesson 11.1 ===
QUESTION:
Under section 96(2), which responsibilities fall on the person who declares goods and places them under the transhipment procedure?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Only the payment of the transhipment duty.
2. The unloading of the goods and securing them in a customs-controlled area approved for that purpose; compliance with the measures required to ensure the goods' identification; and the loading of the goods onto the exporting conveyance.
3. The construction of a new warehouse for the goods.
4. Nothing: the shipping line carries all transhipment responsibilities.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 96(2) of Act 891 states the declarant's responsibilities in its own words: the unloading of the goods and securing them in a customs-controlled area approved for that purpose; compliance with any measures required to ensure the identification of the goods; and the loading of the goods onto the exporting conveyance. The declarant's responsibility is the transfer's security: the goods identified, secured, and loaded as declared.

Answer 1 invents a duty payment — section 96(1) provides the transfer without payment of duty. Answer 3 invents a construction obligation that no provision carries. Answer 4 dissolves the declarant's responsibilities into the line's — the shipping line carries the goods, and the declarant answers for the procedure.

The takeaway: the transhipment's declarant owns the transfer's security — the unloading, the identification, the loading — and the responsibilities are the provision's own list.

=== ASSESSMENT Q3 | tests lesson 11.1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's goods arrive at Tema under a transit declaration from a neighbouring country's port. The trader then sells the goods in Accra instead of moving them to the exit. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The sale is lawful: transit goods may be sold at any point before the exit.
2. The sale is the procedure's breach: transit goods do not enter free circulation — the goods' destiny under the procedure was the exit, and the market sale places them in Ghanaian commerce without the home-use entry and its duty.
3. The sale is lawful if the buyer is also a transit trader.
4. The sale is lawful because the goods' seals were intact.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's non-entry block states the procedures' design: transit goods do not enter free circulation — the duty not paid because the goods do not stay, the control not relaxed because the goods do not enter. The goods' sale in Accra is the goods' entry into Ghanaian commerce without the home-use entry and its duty: the procedure's destiny was the exit, and the market is not it. The breach engages the machinery the procedures' failures carry — the security answers, and the unlawful dealing is the Act's subject.

Answer 1 invents a sale permission the procedure's design refutes. Answer 3's buyer identity is irrelevant — the goods' entry into commerce is the breach, whatever the buyer. Answer 4 mistakes the identification for the permission: the intact seals prove the goods' identity, not the sale's lawfulness.

The takeaway: transit and transhipment goods do not enter free circulation — the market sale is the procedure's breach, and the lawful sale is a different procedure with its duty.

EXPLANATION:

Answer 1 ends the security at the sale; the security's life is the movement's, and its release is the acquittal's. Answer 3 halves the machinery's run. Answer 4 misplaces the machinery — the security serves the deferred duty wherever it runs, including transit's.

The takeaway: the security is taken, held, and released — its life is the movement's, and its release is the acquittal's proof that the liability can no longer arise.

The takeaway: the acquittal is the identity's proof spent — the broken seals and the missing packages are the proof's failure, and the provisions' machinery answers where the proof ends.

CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's diversion block states the provisions: section 41 of Act 891 provides the forfeiture of goods unlawfully loaded or unloaded — the market discharge is the unlawful unloading; section 124(1) provides the conveyance's forfeiture — a conveyance used in the movement of uncustomed, forfeited, prohibited or restricted goods is liable to forfeiture to the State. The bond's machinery answers the duty the diversion put at risk, and the enforcement's chase provisions (ss.135-136) are named as the boundary — Module 14's file.

Answer 1 mistakes the bond for a routing permission — the bond secures the duty; it does not authorise the diversion. The takeaway: the diversion engages the goods' forfeiture, the conveyance's forfeiture, and the security's answer — and the enforcement's chase provisions are the boundary the learner names.

The takeaway: the free zone's existence is the designating statute's act — Act 504's designation — and the customs law's provisions (section 94 and the movements) govern the goods' treatment inside it.

The takeaway: the zone defers the duty; the exit decides it — the export's exit is the deferral's closure without payment, and the market's exit is the deferral's end with payment.
