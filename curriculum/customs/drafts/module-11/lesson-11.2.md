=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The security machinery applied to the corridor
BODY:
The transit movement's duty question is secured, and the security's machinery is the Act's own — sections 108 to 111, taught in Module 10 and applied here to the moving goods.

The forms of security (section 108): a cash deposit or its equivalent; a bond given by a reputable bank, financial institution or insurance guarantor; or an undertaking by an authorised economic operator. The surety's joint and several liability with the principal (section 109(1)). The release and cancellation of the guarantee (section 110) — released where the liability is extinguished or can no longer arise, the corresponding part released where the liability is partly extinguished. The bond's validity (section 111): taken on behalf of the Authority, valid, cancellable by the Commissioner-General's order.

Applied to the corridor: the transit bond secures the duty the goods' passage defers; the surety stands behind the bond; and the acquittal's closure releases the security — the liability extinguished by the goods' lawful exit. The learner's chain: the security is taken at the movement's start, held through the corridor, and released at the acquittal.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Diversion risk: the provisions that meet it
BODY:
The corridor's risk is diversion — the goods leaving the procedure's route, entering the market without the duty, or disappearing. The Act's provisions meet the risk at its points.

Section 41: goods unlawfully loaded or unloaded are liable to forfeiture — the goods' unauthorised exit from the craft or the movement. Section 124(1): a conveyance used in the removal or movement of uncustomed, forfeited, prohibited or restricted goods is liable to forfeiture to the State — the vehicle that carried the diverted goods is itself in jeopardy. Section 52's uncleared-goods machinery and section 53's State-warehouse deposit are the goods' defined destination when they are found.

The learner's reading: the diversion's provisions are the movement's failure modes, named — and the securities' machinery answers the duty the diversion puts at risk. The chase provisions (sections 135 and 136: the ship and vehicle forfeited for offence during chase) are named here as the enforcement's boundary — Module 14's file.

QUESTION:
A transit movement's goods are found at the exit with their seals broken and two packages missing. Which option best states the acquittal's position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The acquittal proceeds, because most of the goods arrived.
2. The acquittal is refused: the goods' identity cannot be proven through the movement — the broken seals and the missing packages break the identification the procedure requires — and the security answers the duty the missing packages put at risk, with the diversion's provisions (sections 41 and 124(1)) engaged.
3. The acquittal proceeds if the carrier apologises.
4. The acquittal proceeds because the goods were under bond.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's identification block states the requirement: the goods that entered must be provably the goods that leave — the seals, the marks and the documents matching the entry's records. The broken seals and the missing packages break the identification: the acquittal cannot confirm the goods' lawful passage, and the movement's closure is refused. The security answers the duty the missing packages put at risk — the bond's machinery — and the diversion's provisions engage: section 41's forfeiture for the goods unlawfully unloaded, section 124(1)'s conveyance liability for the vehicle that carried them.

Answer 1 grants the acquittal on the majority's arrival — the identification's requirement is per the movement's declared goods. Answer 3 substitutes an apology for the proof. Answer 4 confuses the security's existence with the identification's proof — the bond secures the duty; the acquittal requires the identity.

The takeaway: the acquittal is the identity's proof spent — the broken seals and the missing packages are the proof's failure, and the provisions' machinery answers where the proof ends.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
Half of a transit consignment is lawfully acquitted at the exit; the other half remains under the movement's security, its route still running. Which option best states the security's position under section 110?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The whole security remains liable for all the goods, because securities are indivisible.
2. The corresponding part of the guarantee is released: section 110(2) provides that where the liability is extinguished in part, the Commissioner-General releases the corresponding part on the request of the person concerned — the acquitted half's duty question is closed, and the security's exposure drops to the remaining half.
3. The whole security is released once half the goods exit.
4. The security is void when the movement is split.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 110(2) of Act 891 provides the partial release: where the customs liability or liability for other charges is extinguished in part, or may arise only in respect of part of the amount which has been secured, the Commissioner-General shall release the corresponding part of the guarantee on the request of the person concerned. The acquitted half's duty question is closed — the goods left as the procedure required — and the corresponding part of the security is released. The remaining half's exposure continues until its own acquittal.

Answer 1 invents an indivisibility the subsection's proportional language refutes. Answer 3 releases the whole security on the half's acquittal — the remaining goods' duty question is still open, and the security's exposure matches it. Answer 4 invents a voiding that no provision carries.

The takeaway: the security's release follows the liability's extinction, part for part — the acquitted half's closure is the corresponding release, and the open half's exposure continues.
