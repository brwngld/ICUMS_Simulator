=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Transit: the crossing procedure and its three lawful modes
BODY:
Section 95(1) of Act 891 provides the transit procedure: a person may move goods imported into the country from one port of entry to another port within the country, in three lawful ways.

(a) Under a bond or by a bonded carrier, or by both, without the payment of duty — the duty secured rather than paid, the carrier licensed (section 45's bonded carrier, defined in section 151 as the carrier licensed to transport bonded goods within the country). (b) In accordance with the Customs Convention on the International Transport of Goods under Cover of TIR Carnets, provided the movement began or is to end outside Ghana, or runs between two points in Ghana through another country's territory — the Act incorporating the international convention for corridor movements. (c) In accordance with the ATA carnet and temporary-admission conventions.

The unifying idea: the goods cross Ghana without entering its commerce, the revenue is secured rather than collected, and the procedure's machinery — the bond, the carrier, the convention — is what the border trusts instead of the payment.

The learner's reading of the modes: the bond route is the domestic corridor's; the TIR route is the international convention's; the ATA route is the temporary-admission goods'. Each mode's evidence is its own paper.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Transhipment: the same-port transfer
BODY:
Section 96(1) provides transhipment: a person may transfer goods that are imported from an importing conveyance to an exporting conveyance within the same port of entry, without the payment of duty. No inland movement at all — the goods change vessels inside the port, and the transfer's security is structural: the goods never leave the port's control.

Section 96(2) places the responsibility on the declarant: the person who declares the goods and places them under the transhipment procedure is responsible for the unloading of the goods and securing them in a customs-controlled area approved for that purpose; for compliance with any measures required to ensure the identification of the goods; and for their loading onto the exporting conveyance.

The contrast with transit is the discriminator: transit moves goods between ports under bond or convention; transhipment moves them between conveyances inside one port, under the declarant's personal responsibility. Both defer duty; both require the goods to leave as declared; neither places the goods in free circulation.

The learner's diagnostic: same port, two conveyances, no duty — transhipment. Cross-country, secured movement — transit. The geography identifies the procedure.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The defined places and craft of the procedures
BODY:
The procedures' vocabulary is the Act's definitions, and the learner holds them exactly.

The transit-shed: a building in a customs-controlled area, designated by the Commissioner-General by notice in writing, for the deposit of un-entered, un-declared goods — the goods' first controlled place when the transit begins. The customs-controlled area: the perimeter where the Authority has control over goods, persons and conveyances — the shed lives inside it. The bonded carrier: the carrier licensed to transport bonded goods within the country — the transit's licensed mover. Cargo: goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage — the manifest chain's declared goods.

The definitions' work: the transit's goods are deposited in the transit-shed (a controlled building), moved by the bonded carrier (a licensed mover), inside the controlled perimeter (the area), and manifested as cargo (the declared goods). Each definition names a piece of the movement's legal furniture — and the learner's reading of any transit operation checks the pieces: the shed designated, the carrier licensed, the area's control, the cargo manifested.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The contrast with home use: what the procedures defer
BODY:
The procedures are understood against the default they differ from, and the default is home use (section 74): duty paid, free circulation.

Transit defers the duty and defers the entry: the goods never enter free circulation — they cross. The deferral's security is the bond or the convention's guarantee; the deferral's closure is the acquittal at the exit. Transhipment defers the duty and skips the entry: the goods change vessels and leave as they came. The deferral's security is the port's control and the declarant's responsibility for identification and loading.

Home use pays; transit and transhipment secure. The learner's discrimination test: did the goods enter free circulation? Home use yes; transit and transhipment no. Did the goods move inland? Transit yes; transhipment no. The two questions pick the procedure — and the procedure's provisions follow.

The module's map: section 74's home use is the root; sections 95 and 96 are the crossing procedures; and the free zones (section 94) are the territory variant — the next lessons' subject.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The documentation chain: report, declaration, acquittal
BODY:
The transit movement's paper runs the cargo's chain: the master's report on arrival (section 17) brings the goods in; the transit declaration places them under the procedure with the bond or carrier's security; the movement's documents identify the goods through the corridor; and the acquittal at the exit closes the procedure — the goods out as declared.

The declarations' linkage rule (Module 5) applies: the transit declaration links to the reported consignment, and the goods' identity travels with the movement — the seals, the marks, the documents matching the report and the bills. The acquittal's proof is the movement's closure: the goods that entered under the procedure left under it, and the security's release follows the acquittal.

The learner's discipline: the transit file is the movement's story — report, declaration, security, identification, acquittal — and the file's completeness is the procedure's proof. A transit without its acquittal is a movement the system still counts as open.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A corridor movement: Tema to a landlocked buyer, under bond
BODY:
Step 1. The intention. A forwarder moves machinery discharged at Tema to a buyer beyond Ghana's northern border. The goods will not enter Ghanaian commerce: the procedure is transit under section 95(1)(a) — the movement under a bond, by a bonded carrier, or both, without payment of duty.

Step 2. The security assembled. The transit declaration is lodged with the bond in place; the carrier moving the goods is licensed — the bonded carrier's definition satisfied. The goods are secured in the transit-shed pending the departure: the shed's designation, the deposit.

Step 3. The identification travels. The goods are sealed and their documents identify them: the seals, the marks and the accompanying papers match the report and the bills — the linkage rule of Module 5, applied to a moving consignment. The declarant's responsibilities mirror section 96(2)'s: unloading, securing, identification, loading.

Step 4. The crossing. The truck crosses Ghana under control. At the northern border post, the customs administration confirms the goods leaving match the goods that entered: the transit procedure is acquitted, and the security's release follows the acquittal.

Step 5. The buyer's side. The goods enter the destination country under its import procedures — the transit's closure is the import's opening, and the two procedures join at the border.

What does this example demonstrate? Transit is import's opposite twin: the same control spine, the duty secured instead of collected, and the acquittal instead of the release into the market.

Key lesson: identification and acquittal are the corridor's law — the goods must be provably the same at exit as they were at entry.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A TIR movement across multiple territories
BODY:
Step 1. The convention's route. A European forwarder moves goods from Hamburg through several territories to a West African buyer. The movement's documents run under the TIR Carnets convention — the convention Act 891's section 95(1)(b) names, for movements that began or end outside Ghana or cross another territory.

Step 2. The carnet's role. The TIR carnet is the movement's guarantee document: the convention's machinery secures the duties across the territories, so the goods move sealed under the carnet's cover without each territory collecting its own security.

Step 3. The transit's local entry. The goods' passage through Ghana runs under section 95(1)(b) — the convention route — with the Act's transit provisions applying: the goods under control, the identification measures, the acquittal at the exit.

Step 4. The learner's reading. The TIR movement shows the convention route's shape: the international guarantee carries the duty question across territories, and the national provisions govern the goods' passage through each.

What does this example demonstrate? The Act's incorporation of the TIR convention (section 95(1)(b)) is the corridor procedure's international mode — the same goods, the same control, the guarantee from the convention.

Key lesson: the modes differ in their security's source — bond, TIR, ATA — and the provisions' control is the constant.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The goods never enter commerce — that is the procedure's point
BODY:
Transit and transhipment are defined by what the goods do not do: they do not enter free circulation. The learner's common confusion is reading the procedures as imports with extra steps — but the procedures' whole design is the goods' non-entry: the duty not paid because the goods do not stay, the control not relaxed because the goods do not enter. The practical consequences follow: the goods' sale in Ghana under transit or transhipment is the procedure's breach — the goods' destiny was the exit, and the market is not it. The learner's advice discipline: the transit or transhipment client's obligations are the movement's obligations — the security, the identification, the acquittal — and the goods' market sale is a different procedure (home use), not a casual change of plan.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A forwarder's goods arrive at Tema bound for a Burkina Faso buyer. The forwarder wants to move them inland without paying Ghanaian duty. Which option best states the lawful route under section 95(1)?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Declare the goods for home use, pay the duty, and claim a refund when the goods leave.
2. Move the goods under the transit procedure: section 95(1)(a) — under a bond or by a bonded carrier, or both, without payment of duty — or, where the convention applies, under the TIR carnet route of section 95(1)(b).
3. Move the goods without any procedure, because they will leave Ghana anyway.
4. Declare the goods for transhipment, because the destination is outside Ghana.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 95(1) of Act 891 provides the transit procedure for goods moved from one port of entry to another port within the country: under a bond or by a bonded carrier, or both, without the payment of duty — or, where the movement began or ends outside Ghana, under the TIR Carnets convention the subsection names. The corridor movement's goods cross Ghana without entering its commerce, and the transit procedure is the lawful route for exactly that movement.

Answer 1 buys certainty at the cost of the transit procedure's purpose — the duty's payment and refund is not the transit's design; the deferral is. Answer 3 is the unlawful movement: goods crossing the country outside the transit procedure are goods the border's machinery does not account for. Answer 4 confuses transhipment's geography — section 96's transfer is within the same port of entry, not across the country.

The takeaway: the corridor's lawful route is the transit procedure — the bond or the convention's security, the goods under control, the acquittal at the exit.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
Under section 96(2), which responsibilities fall on the person who declares goods and places them under the transhipment procedure?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Only the payment of the transhipment duty.
2. The unloading of the goods and securing them in an approved customs-controlled area; compliance with the measures required to ensure the goods' identification; and the loading onto the exporting conveyance.
3. The construction of a new warehouse for the goods.
4. Nothing: the shipping line carries all transhipment responsibilities.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 96(2) of Act 891 states the declarant's responsibilities in its own words: the unloading of the goods and securing them in a customs-controlled area approved for that purpose; compliance with any measures required to ensure the identification of the goods; and the loading of the goods onto the exporting conveyance. The declarant's responsibility is the transfer's security: the goods identified, secured, and loaded as declared.

Answer 1 invents a duty payment — section 96(1) provides the transfer without payment of duty. Answer 3 invents a construction obligation that no provision carries. Answer 4 dissolves the declarant's responsibilities into the line's — the shipping line carries the goods, and the declarant answers for the procedure.

The takeaway: the transhipment's declarant owns the transfer's security — the unloading, the identification, the loading — and the responsibilities are the provision's own list.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's goods arrive at Tema under a transit declaration from a neighbouring country's port. The trader then sells the goods in Accra instead of moving them to the exit. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The sale is lawful: transit goods may be sold at any point before the exit.
2. The sale is the procedure's breach: transit goods do not enter free circulation — the goods' destiny under the procedure was the exit, and the market sale places them in Ghanaian commerce without the home-use entry and its duty.
3. The sale is lawful if the buyer is also a transit trader.
4. The sale is lawful because the goods' seals were intact.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's non-entry block states the procedures' design: transit goods do not enter free circulation — the duty not paid because the goods do not stay, the control not relaxed because the goods do not enter. The goods' sale in Accra is the goods' entry into Ghanaian commerce without the home-use entry and its duty: the procedure's destiny was the exit, and the market is not it. The breach engages the machinery the procedures' failures carry — the security answers, and the unlawful dealing is the Act's subject.

Answer 1 invents a sale permission the procedure's design refutes. Answer 3's buyer identity is irrelevant — the goods' entry into commerce is the breach, whatever the buyer. Answer 4 mistakes the identification for the permission: the intact seals prove the goods' identity, not the sale's lawfulness.

The takeaway: transit and transhipment goods do not enter free circulation — the market sale is the procedure's breach, and the lawful sale is a different procedure with its duty.
