=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: What an MDA is, and why customs does not clear alone
BODY:
The trade's abbreviation "MDA" — ministries, departments and agencies — names the bodies whose approvals sit beside customs at the border. The term is trade vocabulary, not Act 891 language: the Act defines prohibited and restricted goods, and the machinery that governs them, without using the abbreviation. But the reality the abbreviation names is statutory: certain goods may cross the border only as the controlling law allows, and the allowance is granted by an agency under its own statute.

Why customs alone cannot release such goods: the restriction's condition is not customs's to satisfy. A food product's safety registration is the health authority's to grant; a standards requirement is the standards authority's to certify. Customs's control (section 3) and release (section 6(1)) operate on the whole clearance — and the release condition "the required documents have been acquired" includes the agency's evidence. Customs verifies that the allowance exists; it does not create the allowance.

The learner's picture: the border is a shared checkpoint. Customs holds the declaration, the classification, the value and the revenue; the agencies hold their statutes' controls; and the release waits for both threads to close.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Prohibited and restricted: whether a permit is even possible
BODY:
Section 151's two definitions decide, before any application is made, whether an agency permit can help.

"Prohibited goods" are goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law. For these, no permit applies: the prohibition is absolute, and the goods cannot be lawfully imported at all. The professional's check is the law's list — the Schedules and the provisions — not the trader's hope.

"Restricted goods" are goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law. For these, the restriction's condition is the route: the goods move only as the law allows, and the allowance is evidenced — by the controlling agency's permit, certificate or registration. The permit does not make the goods un-restricted; it evidences that the restriction's condition is satisfied for this consignment.

Section 37 provides the customs-side control frame, and section 99 prohibits the imports the law forbids. The learner's discrimination: prohibited means no lawful entry; restricted means lawful entry against a satisfied condition, evidenced. Every agency control this module teaches operates in the restricted space — which is why the distinction is the module's first block.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The agencies a typical import may touch
BODY:
The training decks name two agencies concretely, and this lesson names those two and keeps the rest generic.

The Ghana Standards Authority (GSA): the national standards body, whose import requirements and inspection the standards decks present — conformity assessment, destination inspection, certification, and the labelling rules. The Food and Drugs Authority (FDA): the health regulator, whose mandate the FDA deck presents through its Centre for Import and Export Control — registration of regulated products before import, permits, and the detention of non-conforming goods.

Beyond the two decks, a typical import may touch other regulators depending on the goods — and the lesson's discipline is to describe them generically: "the sector regulator for the goods". The institutions fact sheet (ghana-customs-institutions.md, planned) will carry the named detail — which body controls which goods, and the licensing routes — with citations, when written.

The learner's working map until then: standards goods ask GSA; food, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices and household chemicals ask FDA; everything else asks its own controlling statute's body — and the declaration's file proves whichever allowance the goods needed.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The electronic permit's place in a declaration
BODY:
The FDA deck shows the modern permit's shape in its own words: importers shall secure an electronic permit (eMDA) in the Integrated Customs Management System (ICUMS) for all import consignments prior to import, with the relevant documents and information uploaded — the product's full name including the brand name, the FDA product registration number in full, the manufacturer's name and address and details, the unit of quantity, and the appropriate HS code for the product.

The lesson reads the deck's shape as the documentary rule: the permit is an electronic record — section 151 defines an electronic record as a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means — and it enters the declaration's supporting file as the evidence of the allowance. The officer's check reads it: the goods' description and classification indicate the restriction; the declaration claims compliance; the permit evidences it.

What the permit is not: a waiver of the clearance. The goods are still declared, examined or exempted, and released under section 6(1)'s conditions — the permit satisfies one of the stated conditions, and the rest of the machinery runs as it runs for any goods.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: One clearance, several statutes
BODY:
A single consignment can sit at the intersection of several statutes, and the learner must see the intersection without confusing it.

The customs statute (Act 891) governs the declaration, the classification, the value, the duty and the release. The health statute (Act 851, Parts 6 to 8) governs the FDA's regulated products — their registration, their warehouses, their detention when non-conforming. The standards legislation — the deck's history running from NRCD 173 through the Ghana Standards Authority Act, 2022 (Act 1078) — governs the standards requirements. Each statute's control applies to the same goods at the same border, and the clearance cannot close until each thread's condition is satisfied.

The practical consequence is the multi-file clearance: the customs file (the declaration and its supporting documents), and beside it the agency files (the registration, the permit, the certificate of conformity). The declaration references the agency evidence; the agencies' evidence never replaces the customs machinery.

The learner's discipline: name each thread's statute, each thread's condition, and each thread's evidence — and hold the threads separate in the file, because the escalation and the remedies differ by thread.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Naming actors: the sourcing discipline
BODY:
This lesson names two agencies on the decks' authority — the Ghana Standards Authority and the Food and Drugs Authority — and it names nothing beyond them, for the reason the spec makes a rule: institutional facts come only from an anchor, a fact sheet, or a verified authoritative source (spec C4.5).

The decks give the two agencies their mandates, their centres and departments, and their requirements; the institutions fact sheet (ghana-customs-institutions.md, planned) will give the wider map — which body controls which goods across the full agency list, the licensing routes, and the DO-NOT-CLAIM list the plan promises. Until it is written, every other regulator is described generically: "the sector regulator for the goods".

The discipline also governs the named system the FDA deck uses: the deck names ICUMS and the eMDA, and the lesson quotes the deck's own description — the permit secured in ICUMS prior to import, with the documents uploaded. The quote carries its source; anything beyond the deck's description waits for the systems fact sheet. Named with a source, or generic without one: there is no third option.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A cosmetics consignment touches two agencies
BODY:
Step 1. The goods. An importer brings in a consignment of skincare creams and lotions — products that sit in two agencies' orbits at once: they are cosmetics (FDA's registration regime) and they carry labelling requirements (the standards rules).

Step 2. The health thread. The FDA deck's rule: regulated products must be registered before importation, under section 118(1) of Act 851 — a person shall not import a cosmetic unless the article has been registered by the Authority. The importer's products hold FDA registration numbers, obtained before shipment.

Step 3. The standards thread. The consignment's labels must satisfy the labelling rules the standards deck presents: product name, instructions for use, the manufacturer's name and address, country of origin, date of manufacture, batch or lot number. The importer checks the labels against the rules before shipping.

Step 4. The declaration. The agent lodges the declaration with the file: the commercial invoice, the transport document, the packing list, the FDA registration evidence for each product. The officer reads the description, sees the restricted category, and verifies the allowance's evidence in the file.

Step 5. The release. The conditions of section 6(1) assemble: the goods examined or exempted, no offence, documents acquired — including the registration evidence — and the stated conditions met. The goods are released into the market their registrations already qualified them for.

What does this example demonstrate? One consignment, two agency threads, one customs clearance — and the file carries each thread's evidence where the officer reads it.

Key lesson: the registration happens before the shipping; the evidence travels in the file; and the release waits for every thread.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: The same intersection at a foreign border
BODY:
Step 1. The scene. At a European border, a consignment of electrical appliances arrives. The customs declaration lodges the goods; the market's product-safety regime requires conformity evidence; the energy-labelling regime requires labels.

Step 2. The threads. The customs thread assesses the duty; the safety thread requires the conformity certificate the market's regime names; the labelling thread checks the labels against its rules. Three threads, one consignment — the same intersection Ghana's cosmetics consignment sat at, in different statutes.

Step 3. The mechanics. The importer's file carries the conformity documentation with the customs declaration; the officer verifies the evidence against the goods; the release follows when the threads close.

Step 4. The transfer. The learner who understood Ghana's intersection — customs's machinery plus the agencies' evidenced allowances — reads the foreign intersection without new concepts: the statutes differ, the threads do not. The certificate of conformity abroad plays the registration's role at home; the customs declaration plays the same statutory role in both; and the release assembles the threads' closures in both jurisdictions.

Step 5. The failure mode transferred. Had the appliance arrived without its conformity certificate, the foreign border's hold would be the same hold Ghana's border applies to unlabelled cosmetics: an unmet condition on a named thread, with the release waiting. The diagnosis — which thread, which owner, which remedy — transfers with the learner.

What does this example demonstrate? Multi-agency borders are the international norm: the customs clearance is one thread among the threads the goods' own statutes demand, and the thread-diagnosis skill is what travels.

Key lesson: the goods' product categories summon their regulators; the clearance completes when every summoned thread closes.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: "MDA" is trade vocabulary; the statutes are the law
BODY:
The abbreviation MDA — ministries, departments and agencies — is how the trade speaks about the controlling bodies, and the training decks use it. Act 891 does not: the Act speaks of prohibited and restricted goods, of the conditions their movement carries, and of the documents the entry requires. The learner should be fluent in both vocabularies and precise about which one a document uses. A client who says "MDA requirements" is pointing at a real legal state — the restriction's condition and its evidence — but the advice that follows must land on the provisions: the definition of restricted goods, the controlling statute's condition, and the release condition the evidence satisfies. Trade vocabulary opens the conversation; the provisions carry the advice.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: One product, several agencies
BODY:
A single product can touch more than one agency, and the file must carry each thread's evidence. The decks' own boundary question makes the point: the standards training asks its audience what the difference in work is between the Ghana Standards Authority and the Food and Drugs Authority — because the two mandates meet on the same shelves. A cosmetic is FDA's registration regime; its label is the standards rules' territory. An electrical appliance is GSA's conformity regime; its plug and wiring may be another rule's. The learner's discipline is per-product: for each product in the consignment, name every statute whose condition touches it, and carry every condition's evidence. One product, several agencies — and the clearance completes when the last thread closes, not the first.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's consignment of skin-lightening creams is on a list of goods whose importation is prohibited outright. The trader obtains a certificate from a private laboratory stating the creams are safe. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The certificate cures the prohibition, because the goods are now evidenced as safe.
2. The certificate is irrelevant: the goods are prohibited goods within section 151's definition — importation is prohibited by law — and no private certificate creates an allowance; the professional's check is the law's list, not a trader's hope.
3. The certificate works if it is issued abroad.
4. The certificate works if the trader declares the goods as cosmetics rather than skin-lightening creams.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines prohibited goods as goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law — an absolute state, with no permit machinery behind it. The distinction the lesson teaches is between prohibited (no lawful entry; the professional's check is the law's list) and restricted (lawful entry against a satisfied condition, evidenced by the controlling agency's allowance). A private laboratory's safety certificate is an evidence of quality, not an allowance the prohibition's law recognises.

Answer 1 mistakes quality evidence for a statutory allowance — the prohibition is not a safety condition; it is an exclusion. Answer 3 invents a geographic cure; the prohibition follows the importation, wherever the certificate was issued. Answer 4 is the serious aggravation: describing the goods to evade the prohibition is a false statement, not a cure.

The takeaway: prohibited and restricted are different legal states — the first has no permit route, and the second's route is the controlling agency's allowance, evidenced. Knowing which state the goods are in is the first professional check.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
The FDA deck instructs importers to secure an electronic permit (eMDA) in ICUMS before importation, listing the product details to upload. Which option best states what the permit does in the declaration's file?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The permit replaces the declaration for FDA-regulated goods.
2. The permit exempts the goods from examination.
3. The permit is the electronic record evidencing the FDA allowance — it enters the declaration's supporting file, and the officer's check reads it against the goods' description and classification as the evidence that the restriction's condition is satisfied.
4. The permit transfers the goods into free circulation at the port of departure.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The deck's instruction is the anchor for the permit's shape: importers secure an electronic permit (eMDA) in ICUMS prior to import, uploading the product's full name including the brand name, the FDA product registration number in full, the manufacturer's details, the unit of quantity, and the appropriate HS code. The lesson reads that shape through section 151's electronic-record definition — a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means — and places it as the evidence of the allowance: the officer's check reads the restriction indicated by the goods, the compliance claimed by the declaration, and the permit evidencing the allowance.

Answer 1 makes the permit a declaration substitute; the permit evidences one condition, it does not state the goods' full particulars. Answer 2 turns the allowance into an examination waiver — the goods are still examined or exempted under the risk-based control. Answer 4 invents a transfer that no provision carries.

The takeaway: the permit is the allowance's electronic evidence in the file — the clearance still runs its whole machinery, and the officer reads the evidence where the check happens.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of food products needs the health regulator's registration, and its labels must meet the standards rules. The importer has the registration but the labels are non-compliant. Which option best states what the file and the clearance need?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Only the registration matters; labelling is a retail concern that customs never checks.
2. Both threads must close: the registration evidences the health regulator's allowance, and the labelling condition is the standards thread's — the file carries both evidences, and the clearance completes when the last thread closes, not the first.
3. The registration cures the labelling, because registration covers all product requirements.
4. The labelling can be fixed after the goods are released into the market.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's one-product-several-agencies discipline carries the answer: for each product, name every statute whose condition touches it, and carry every condition's evidence. The health thread's evidence is the registration — the FDA deck's rule that regulated products must be registered before importation under Act 851's provisions. The standards thread's evidence is the labelling compliance — the labelling rules the standards deck presents with their required fields. The clearance's release under section 6(1) requires the stated conditions met; a non-compliant label is an unmet condition, whichever thread owns it.

Answer 1 drops the standards thread — the deck's labelling rules are border-relevant requirements, not retail afterthoughts. Answer 3 invents a cure-by-registration; the registration evidences one statute's condition, not another's. Answer 4 defers a condition past release — the release's stated conditions must be met before release, which is why the labels are checked at the border.

The takeaway: one product, several agencies, several conditions — and the file that carries every condition's evidence is the file whose clearance completes.
