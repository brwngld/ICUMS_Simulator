=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 6.1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's consignment of goods is on the import-prohibition list. His agent suggests obtaining "an MDA permit" from a sector regulator to clear them. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The agent's suggestion is sound: any agency permit evidences an allowance that overcomes a prohibition.
2. The agent's suggestion is sound if the regulator is the FDA.
3. The suggestion misunderstands the states: prohibited goods — importation prohibited by law (section 151) — have no permit route, while restricted goods move against a satisfied condition evidenced by the controlling agency's allowance; the first professional check is which state the goods are in.
4. The suggestion is sound as long as the permit is obtained before the goods arrive.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 151 of Act 891 defines prohibited goods as goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law, and restricted goods as goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law. The two states are different in kind: the prohibition is absolute — no allowance exists to evidence — while the restriction's condition is the route the permit evidences. Section 37's control frame and section 99's prohibited imports are the provisions behind the states.

Answer 1 and Answer 2 treat an agency's paper as a cure for a prohibition; an allowance evidences a restriction's condition, and a prohibition has none to satisfy. Answer 4 adds timing to a state question — the goods' legal state decides the route before any timing matters.

The takeaway: the first professional check is the goods' legal state — prohibited means no route, restricted means the route is the condition's evidence — and the check reads the law's list, not a trader's hope.

=== ASSESSMENT Q2 | tests lesson 6.1 ===
QUESTION:
The FDA deck instructs importers to secure the eMDA in ICUMS prior to import, uploading the product details. In the declaration's file, what is the permit, and what does the officer's check do with it?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The permit is the declaration itself for FDA-regulated goods.
2. The permit is an electronic record — section 151's definition — evidencing the FDA allowance; the officer reads it against the goods' description and classification as the evidence that the restriction's condition is satisfied, while the clearance machinery runs as for any goods.
3. The permit is a customs assessment of the goods' health risk.
4. The permit transfers the goods into free circulation before the vessel sails.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The deck's instruction is the anchor for the permit's shape — the eMDA secured in ICUMS prior to import, with the product's full name including the brand name, the FDA registration number in full, the manufacturer's details, the unit of quantity, and the HS code uploaded — and section 151's electronic-record definition gives its shape: a record created, generated, sent, received or stored by electronic means. The permit's place is the declaration's supporting file: the evidence of the allowance, read by the officer's check against the restriction the goods indicate.

Answer 1 makes the permit a declaration substitute; it evidences the allowance, it does not state the goods' full particulars. Answer 3 invents a health-risk assessment role that belongs to the agency's own machinery. Answer 4 invents a pre-clearance transfer that no provision carries.

The takeaway: the permit is the allowance's electronic evidence in the file — the clearance still runs its whole machinery, and the officer reads the evidence where the check happens.

=== ASSESSMENT Q3 | tests lesson 6.1 ===
QUESTION:
A client's consignment contains three product lines: a food product, a cosmetic, and an electrical appliance. Which option best states the agency threads the clearance must close?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. One thread — customs — because the declaration covers all three product lines.
2. Only the FDA thread, because food and cosmetics are in its list.
3. Three threads at least: the food and cosmetic lines carry the FDA's registration conditions (the deck's quoted ss.97(1) and 118(1) of Act 851), the appliance carries the standards thread's conformity requirements (the GSA deck's rules), and the customs clearance completes when every summoned thread closes — with each thread's evidence in the file.
4. The threads depend on the container's size, not the products'.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's one-product-several-agencies discipline runs per product: the food line is in the FDA's section 97(1) registration regime, the cosmetic line in its section 118(1) regime — both quoted from the deck — and the electrical appliance is in the standards deck's conformity and high-risk machinery. Each product's condition summons its regulator, and each condition's evidence enters the declaration's file. The customs clearance under Act 891 completes when the stated conditions are met — which means every summoned thread's evidence is in the file.

Answer 1 collapses the threads into the declaration; the declaration carries the threads' evidence, it does not replace their conditions. Answer 2 drops the appliance's standards thread. Answer 4 invents a container-size test that no provision carries.

The takeaway: per product, per statute, per condition — the file carries every thread's evidence, and the release assembles every thread's closure.

4. The statement is wrong because the Authority has no founding instrument.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The standards deck's history slide states the sequence: established as the National Standards Board in 1967; reconstituted as the Ghana Standards Board by the Standards Decree, NRCD 173, in 1973; made custodian of the Weights and Measures Decree, 1975 (NRCD 326) — legal metrology — in 1975; renamed the Ghana Standards Authority in 2011; and the Ghana Standards Authority Act, 2022 (Act 1078) consolidated NRCD 173 and NRCD 326, re-establishing the Authority.

Answer 1 skips the history the deck states — the 2022 Act consolidated instruments that go back to 1967, and "created" erases them. Answer 3 misattributes LI 1541, which the notes deck quotes as the labelling rules of 1992. Answer 4 is false on the deck's face.

The takeaway: the mandate's history is the mandate's sources — the learner states the sequence as the deck states it, and names the instruments the consolidation collected.

QUESTION:
An importer brings in a herbal medicinal product without registering it with the FDA, planning to register "when sales start". Which option best states the position under the deck's quoted provisions?

1. The position is lawful: herbal products are registered at the point of sale.

EXPLANATION:

Answer 1 moves the condition to the sales stage; the statute's list begins earlier. Answer 3 misassigns the regime — the deck's quoted provisions place the herbal medicinal product in the FDA's registration regime, and the standards thread is additional where it applies. Answer 4 invents a calendar-year cure for a condition that precedes the act.

The takeaway: the registration condition precedes the importation in the statute's own words — the deck's quotation is the border's rule, and the product's registration number is what the file carries before the goods ship.

=== ASSESSMENT Q9 | tests lesson 6.3 ===
QUESTION:
An FDA-regulated consignment's registration and eMDA are in order, but the border inspection finds the products non-conforming. Which option best states the deck's rule and the clearance's position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The goods are released, because the documentary thread was complete.
2. The goods are detained for further regulatory actions — the deck's own rule for non-conforming products — and the release under section 6(1) waits, because the stated conditions include the conformity the inspection has just found wanting.
3. The goods are released with a labelling correction to be made in the market.
4. The registration is cancelled automatically, and the goods are confiscated.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The deck's general-requirements slide states the response in its own words: all non-conforming products shall be detained for further regulatory actions. The scenario's documentary thread was complete — the registration and the eMDA — but the inspection's conformity finding is the FDA thread's own control, and its failure detains the goods. Section 6(1)'s release conditions are conjunctive: the stated conditions include the regime's evidence being in order, and the detention is the thread's open state.

Answer 1 lets the documentary thread override the conformity finding — the inspection exists to catch exactly this. Answer 3 defers the condition into the market, which the detention's machinery and the release's conjunctive conditions both refuse. Answer 4 invents an automatic cancellation and confiscation that the deck does not state — the detention is "for further regulatory actions", whose content is the agency's machinery.

The takeaway: the FDA thread's inspection is a real control — the non-conforming finding detains under the deck's own rule, and the customs release waits for the thread's resolution.

CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Answer 1 invents a partial-credit rule the conjunctive conditions refuse. Answer 2 drops a core file document — the packing list is the physical facts the consistency check runs on. The takeaway: two open threads are two unmet conditions — the release waits for the last one, and the professional's value is working both in parallel while telling the client which door opens next.

QUESTION:
An agency query has held a consignment for three weeks, and the agent has not tracked the customs-side dates. The goods were entered under a procedure on day one. Which option best states the exposure the agent should have been managing?

3. The only exposure is the agency's fee schedule.
4. The exposure starts after six months, when the goods are auctioned.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's customs-clock block carries the answer: the agency thread's delay does not pause the customs machinery. Section 4(3) of Act 891 runs for entered goods in a customs-controlled area — the fourteen days make the goods liable to forfeiture unless the failure to move them is explained to the satisfaction of the Commissioner-General — and the agency hold is exactly the explanation's content, submitted with the file before the deadline. Section 52's uncleared-goods machinery is the next state the drift reaches, and section 53's deposit the destination.

Answer 1 invents a suspension that the provisions do not carry — the clocks run with the hold. Answer 3 narrows the exposure to a commercial fee; the statutory clock is the one that bites. Answer 4 skips the machinery to a distant auction — the fourteen-day rule and the uncleared-goods provisions are the nearer, defined stages.

The takeaway: the agency hold is the explanation's content, not the clock's stop — the professional's file carries both dated, and the explanation reaches the Commissioner-General before the deadline.
