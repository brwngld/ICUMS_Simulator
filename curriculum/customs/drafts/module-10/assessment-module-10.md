=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 10.1 ===
QUESTION:
A bonded warehouse keeper's stock verification finds goods recorded as secured that are physically missing. Which option best states the provisions the regime's response runs through?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Section 85's removal provisions — the goods are treated as lawfully removed to another warehouse.
2. Section 80's records (the goods were accounted as secured), section 83's unauthorised removal and section 84's destruction and stealing provisions (the failure named), and the security machinery of sections 108-111 (the bond answering the duty the missing goods put at risk).
3. Section 88's entry provisions — the goods are treated as entered into home use with duty paid.
4. Section 52's uncleared-goods machinery — the goods are treated as never warehoused.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The regime's failure machinery is the answer's chain. Section 80 of Act 891 required the keeper's records to account for the goods as secured — the records show what the regime lost. Section 83's unauthorised removal and section 84's destruction and stealing provisions name the failure's kinds. And the security behind the suspense — the bond deposited under section 47(1)(b), running through sections 108 to 111's machinery (the forms of security, the surety's joint and several liability, the release and cancellation rules, the bond's validity) — answers the duty the missing goods put at risk.

Answer 1 converts the failure into a lawful removal — the opposite of the records' account. Answer 3 converts the failure into a duty payment that was never made. Answer 4 dissolves the regime's records into a state the goods never held.

The takeaway: the failure provisions name the regime's risks, the records prove what was lost, and the bond answers the duty — the three provisions are the failure's answer, read together.

3. Both movements require the duty's payment first, because suspense goods cannot move.
4. The movements run without records, because the goods remain under the same bond.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 88 of Act 891 provides the entry of warehoused goods — the suspense's resolution: the goods enter home use, the duty is paid, the regime's account closes for them. Section 85 provides the removal — from a transit shed or from one warehouse to another — the goods changing controlled places under control, the suspense travelling with them. The keeper's records under section 80 account for both movements: the removal and the entry, the goods' position at both ends.

Answer 1 dissolves the regime's movement machinery into a prohibition — the movements are the regime's choreography, provided for. Answer 3 forces a duty payment the removal machinery exists to defer — the movement between controlled places is precisely the suspense's continuation. Answer 4 dissolves the records duty; the bond's exposure is measured by the records, and the unrecorded movement is the regime's breach.

The takeaway: the exit and the movement each name their provision — section 88's entry pays, section 85's removal moves — and the records account for both, because the bond's exposure follows the records.

=== ASSESSMENT Q4 | tests lesson 10.2 ===
QUESTION:
At an auction conducted by a proper officer under section 93, a buyer offers below the proper officer's estimated value for the goods. Which option best states the position?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The sale proceeds at the buyer's offer, because auctions follow the bids.
2. The sale proceeds at the buyer's offer, minus the customs duty the buyer then pays separately.
3. The subsection's floor applies: section 93(3) provides that the value of duty for the goods auctioned is not less than the value estimated by the proper officer, and section 93(2) provides that the auction price includes the customs duty — so the bid below the estimate cannot stand as the duty's value.
4. The auction is cancelled permanently because one bid was low.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 93 of Act 891 states the auction's machinery: subsection (1) — goods may be auctioned under the Commissioner-General's direction; subsection (2) — the price at which goods are sold at an auction sale includes the customs duty; subsection (3) — the value of duty for the goods auctioned is not less than the value estimated by the proper officer. A bid below the estimate cannot set the duty's value below the officer's estimate — the subsection's floor applies to the duty's computation.

Answer 1 reads the auction as free-market bidding; the provision's floor is the revenue's protection. Answer 2 splits the price and the duty — subsection (2) makes the auction price duty-inclusive. Answer 4 invents a permanent cancellation; the floor governs the duty's value, and the auction's process continues under its machinery.

The takeaway: the auction's price includes the duty, and the duty's value floors at the proper officer's estimate — the revenue's protection is written into the sale itself.

CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The scenario's facts meet the conditions: the contract, the paid duty, the non-conformity, the approved return, the non-use.

Answer 1 abandons the relief the provision provides. The takeaway: the contract-mismatch refund's conditions are conjunctive — the contract, the paid duty, the non-conformity, the approved return, the non-use — and the claim's file evidences all of them.
