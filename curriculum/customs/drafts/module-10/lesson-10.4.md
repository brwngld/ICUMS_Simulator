=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The promise structure of relief
BODY:
Every relief is a promise. The warehoused goods' duty is deferred on the promise that the goods will be entered (and the duty paid) or re-exported. The temporary admission's duty is deferred on the promise that the goods will leave. The drawback's refund is paid on the promise that the goods will be exported. The security given for a procedure is given on the promise that the procedure's conditions will be kept.

The promise's two sides: the trader's side (the goods used as authorised, the departure performed, the records kept) and the revenue's side (the deferred duty secured, the security held, the reconciliation performed). Section 122 names the failure's subject — goods used contrary to authorised purpose — and Module 14 carries its offence machinery.

The learner's picture: the relief's conditions are the promise's terms, the security is the promise's collateral, the records are the promise's evidence, and the reconciliation is the promise's audit. This lesson teaches the reconciliation — how the system checks that the relief was deserved and the promises were kept.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The security machinery: forms, surety, release, validity
BODY:
Sections 108 to 111 are the security machinery the reliefs' promises run on, and each provision's words are the machinery's parts.

Section 108, the forms of security: a cash deposit or its equivalent; a bond given by a reputable bank, financial institution or insurance guarantor; or an undertaking by an authorised economic operator. Section 109, the surety: jointly and severally liable with the principal on the bond — with the Commissioner-General's power to require a new bond where the bonded person or a surety dies, becomes bankrupt, departs with insufficient property, or is otherwise unable to satisfy the security. Section 110, the release and cancellation: the guarantee released where the liability is extinguished or can no longer arise; the corresponding part released where the liability is partly extinguished; no release where the amount does not justify it; the bond cancellable where the breaching person pays a lesser amount or fulfils other conditions. Section 111, the validity: the bond taken on behalf of the Authority, valid even if given by a person under twenty-one, cancellable by the Commissioner-General's order.

The learner's chain: the security is taken (s.108), backed by the surety (s.109), released as the liability extinguishes (s.110), and valid throughout (s.111).

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The keeper's records and the audit's reading
BODY:
The reconciliation's evidence is the records, and the warehousing regime's records duty is section 80's: the keeper's account of every entry, removal and delivery. The audit that reads them is section 7's post-clearance audit — the mechanism that revisits the clearance and the procedure after the goods have moved.

The audit's origin questions are the reconciliation's questions: the goods entered under the procedure — were they used as authorised? The goods removed — under which provision, to where, with the duty settled how? The records answer: the keeper's entries and removals, the trader's declarations, the payments' evidence.

Section 9's record-keeping duty is the trader's side: the records kept for the retention period the provision states. The learner's discipline: the relief's file is retained — the security's papers, the movements' records, the duty's settlements — because the reconciliation's questions are asked after the goods have moved, and the records are what answer them.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The reconciliation walk: declared relief, actual outcome, adjustment
BODY:
The reconciliation's walk runs on three states: the relief as declared (the goods admitted to the procedure, the duty deferred or the refund claimed), the outcome as actual (the goods' destiny — entered, exported, used, lost), and the adjustment where the two differ.

The walk's cases: the warehoused goods fully entered into home use — the deferred duty paid in full, the security released under section 110(1). The warehoused goods partly entered — the duty paid on the entered share, the corresponding part of the guarantee released under section 110(2), the rest still secured. The temporary-admitted goods exported — the deferral closed by the departure, the security released. The goods used contrary to their authorised purpose — section 122's case, the deferred duty's question revived, the adjustment in the revenue's favour.

The learner's deliverable: the reconciliation's file, showing the declared relief, the actual outcome, and the adjustment's provision — because the reconciliation that cannot show its walk is a reconciliation the audit re-runs.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Section 122: the failure mode named
BODY:
Section 122 provides the reconciliation's failure mode: goods used contrary to authorised purpose. The goods admitted under a procedure with conditions — used, consumed or disposed of outside those conditions — are the provision's subject, and the deferred duty's question revives for them.

The provision's subject is named here; the offence machinery — the penalties, the prosecution, the amounts — is Module 14's territory, with the spec's C4.4 ban enforced there and here. What this lesson requires: the failure's identification (the goods' actual use against the authorised purpose), the duty's revival (the deferred question coming due), and the reconciliation's adjustment (the file showing the breach and the revenue's response).

The learner's advice discipline: a client whose goods' use is drifting from the authorised purpose is advised before the drift becomes the breach — because the reconciliation's adjustment is the lawful consequence, and the breach's offence machinery is the separate file the Module 14 lessons carry.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The file the audit reads
BODY:
The reconciliation's deliverable is the file the audit reads, and its parts are the relief's life in papers: the procedure's authorising documents (the admission, the warehousing entry), the security's papers (the bond, the surety, the forms of section 108), the movement records (the removals under section 85, the entries under section 88), the duty's settlements (the entries into home use), and the export's evidence (for the drawback and the deferral closures).

Section 9's record-keeping duty keeps the trader's side; section 80's records keep the keeper's; section 7's audit reads both. The learner's discipline: the file's parts are named, dated, and cross-referenced — because the reconciliation's questions are the file's questions, and the file that answers them before the audit asks is the file the audit walks without findings.

The module's closing discipline: the relief's promise, the security's collateral, the records' evidence, the reconciliation's walk — the four pieces are the relief's control system, and the file is where they meet.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A warehouse's partial entry: the reconciliation walked
BODY:
Step 1. The declared relief. An importer's machinery consignment — 100 machines — sits in a private bonded warehouse under the suspense: the duty deferred, the bond secured, the keeper's records accounting for the 100 machines' entry and position.

Step 2. The actual outcome, part one. A buyer purchases 60 machines for the Ghanaian market. The exit is section 88's entry of warehoused goods: the duty on the 60 machines' value is paid, the goods leave the regime, and the keeper's records show the removal and the entry.

Step 3. The reconciliation. The declared relief covered 100 machines; the actual outcome is 60 machines entering home use with duty paid, 40 machines still secured. Section 110(2)'s machinery releases the corresponding part of the guarantee — the security's exposure reduced to the 40 machines' duty — and the reconciliation's file shows the declared relief, the partial outcome, and the proportional release.

Step 4. The remaining suspense. The 40 machines' suspense continues: the bond's remaining exposure, the keeper's records still accounting, the goods' next destiny — another entry, or a re-exportation — to be reconciled when it happens.

Step 5. The audit's readiness. The file shows the walk: the declared relief, the partial entry, the duty's payment on the entered share, the guarantee's proportional release. The audit's questions are answered before they are asked.

What does this example demonstrate? The reconciliation's walk is the relief's story in three states — declared, actual, adjusted — and section 110(2)'s proportional release is the machinery's answer to the partial outcome.

Key lesson: the partial reconciliation is the normal case — the file that shows the walk is the file the audit trusts.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A bond's release abroad, and the discharge discipline
BODY:
Step 1. The scene. At a European administration, a trader's warehoused goods are fully entered into home use: the duty paid, the procedure's suspense resolved. The bond that secured the duty is due for discharge.

Step 2. The discharge machinery. The administration's security release runs the section 110 analogue: the customs liability is extinguished by the payment, and the guarantee is released — the bond's discharge documented, the surety's liability ended with the principal's.

Step 3. The discipline. The discharge's file shows the liability's extinction (the payment's evidence), the guarantee's release, and the records' closure — the security's life from taking to release, documented.

Step 4. The transfer. Ghana's section 110(1) provides the same machinery in the same logic: the guarantee released where the customs liability is extinguished or can no longer arise. The learner who understands the release's condition — the liability's extinction — reads any administration's discharge correctly.

What does this example demonstrate? The security's life is the liability's shadow — taken when the duty is deferred, released when the duty is extinguished — and the discharge's file is the shadow's end.

Key lesson: the bond's release is the liability's extinction, documented — and the file that proves the extinction is what the release runs on.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader's 100 machines sit in a bonded warehouse under suspense. The trader enters 60 into home use with duty paid, and asks about the bond securing the remaining goods. Which option best states the security's position under section 110?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The bond remains fully liable for all 100 machines, because bonds are indivisible.
2. The corresponding part of the guarantee is released: section 110(2) provides that where the liability is extinguished in part, or may arise only in respect of part of the secured amount, the Commissioner-General releases the corresponding part on the request of the person concerned — the 60 machines' duty is settled, and the bond's exposure drops to the 40 machines' duty.
3. The bond is void entirely once any goods are entered.
4. The bond's release requires the warehouse's full closure first.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 110(2) of Act 891 provides the partial release: where the customs liability or liability for other charges is extinguished in part, or may arise only in respect of part of the amount secured, the Commissioner-General releases the corresponding part of the guarantee on the request of the person concerned. The scenario's 60 machines' duty settlement extinguishes that share of the liability, and the corresponding part of the bond's security is released — the 40 machines' exposure remaining.

Answer 1 invents an indivisibility the subsection's proportional language refutes. Answer 3 dissolves the whole security on a partial entry — the machinery's answer is the proportional release. Answer 4 invents a sequencing condition; the release runs on the liability's extinction, per request.

The takeaway: the security's release follows the liability's extinction, part for part — the partial entry's duty settled is the partial bond's release, claimed by the person concerned.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
The surety on a procedure's bond dies, and the Commissioner-General doubts the remaining security's adequacy. Which option best states the machinery's response?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Nothing happens until the duty becomes payable.
2. Section 109(2) provides the new-security power: the Commissioner-General may require a new bond or other security where the person under the bond or a surety dies, becomes bankrupt, departs the country leaving insufficient property, or is otherwise unable — in the Commissioner-General's opinion — to satisfy the security; and section 111 states the bond is taken on behalf of the Authority and cancellable by the Commissioner-General's order.
3. The surety's death voids the bond, releasing the goods from the procedure.
4. The warehouse keeper assumes the surety's liability automatically.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 109(2) of Act 891 provides the new-security power expressly: where the person under the bond or security or a surety dies, becomes bankrupt or enters into an arrangement or composition with or for the benefit of creditors, departs the country without leaving sufficient property to satisfy the whole amount, or is for any other reason — in the Commissioner-General's opinion — unable or likely to be unable to satisfy the bond or security, the Commissioner-General may require a new bond or other form of security. Section 111 frames the security as taken on behalf of the Authority, valid, and cancellable by the Commissioner-General's order.

Answer 1 waits for the liability — the power is preventive, exercised on the security's adequacy. Answer 3 dissolves the procedure on the surety's death; the machinery's answer is the new security, not the regime's collapse. Answer 4 invents an automatic transfer that no provision carries.

The takeaway: the security's adequacy is a standing state under review — the surety's death engages the new-security power, and the regime's protection is the machinery's response.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
Goods admitted under a procedure for processing are instead sold in the local market. Which option best states the lesson's treatment of this use?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The sale is the procedure's normal completion, and no adjustment follows.
2. The sale is section 122's subject — goods used contrary to authorised purpose — and the lesson's treatment is the reconciliation's: the deferred duty's question revives for the sold goods, the file shows the breach and the revenue's response, and the offence machinery for the false or unauthorised dealing is Module 14's boundary, with spec C4.4's amounts ban enforced there.
3. The sale triggers the goods' immediate destruction.
4. The sale is allowed if the buyer pays the transport costs.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 122 of Act 891 names the failure's subject: goods used contrary to authorised purpose. The lesson's reconciliation block states the treatment: the deferred duty's question revives for the goods whose use broke the procedure's conditions — the file shows the breach (the goods' actual use against the authorised purpose) and the revenue's response (the duty's settlement at the home-use rate) — and the offence machinery for the dealing is Module 14's file, with the spec's C4.4 ban on amounts enforced there and here.

Answer 1 normalises the breach as a completion — the procedure's completion is the authorised use, and the sale is its contradiction. Answer 3 invents a destruction that no provision states. Answer 4 invents a cost-based cure for a condition's breach.

The takeaway: the goods' use contrary to the authorised purpose is the reconciliation's failure mode — the duty revives, the file records the breach and the adjustment, and the offence machinery is the boundary's other file.
