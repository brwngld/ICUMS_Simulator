=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The relief family, mapped by its provisions
BODY:
The reliefs are the revenue's adjustments: the duty charged can be removed (exemption), reduced (abatement), returned (refund), or repaid on export (drawback) — and each relief's provision states its own conditions. Section 104 provides the refund and remission; section 105 the abatement of duty; section 106 the drawback; and section 2 the exemptions — the exempt goods in the Harmonised System, the scheduled persons and organisations, and the removal article.

The family's discipline is the claim's discipline: every relief is claimed against its provision's conditions, evidenced to the provision's requirements, and looked up rather than remembered. The exempt goods are specified in the Harmonised System; the exempt persons are in the Schedules; the refund's conditions are the contract's non-conformity; the abatement's conditions are the damage's timing and proof; the drawback's conditions are the export.

The learner's map: four provisions, four reliefs, four sets of conditions — and the client's question is matched to the provision whose conditions the client's facts can satisfy.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Refund and remission: section 104(1)'s contract case
BODY:
Section 104(1) provides the refund where goods were imported in pursuance of a contract of sale, duty was paid, and the goods at release did not match the contract — the description, quality, state or condition not in accordance with it. The refund's conditions are the provision's own, and none is optional.

The goods are returned to the supplier with the Commissioner-General's approval, or abandoned in accordance with the laid-down procedure, or destroyed under customs control. And the goods have not been subjected to use after release beyond what was necessary to discover the non-conformity.

The conditions' teaching: the refund is for the goods that failed their contract AND were not used — the importer who discovers the mismatch, returns or abandons or destroys the goods under control, and has not used them. The use condition is the provision's integrity: goods used after release are goods whose mismatch the market absorbed, and the refund's machinery does not follow them.

Subsection (2) adds the second refund case: goods shipped without the consignee's consent, duty paid — the refund allowed where the goods are returned to the consignor, abandoned to the Authority, or destroyed under supervision.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Abatement: the damaged goods' proportional relief
BODY:
Section 105 provides the abatement of duty for damaged goods, and its conditions are timing and proof.

Subsection (1): the Commissioner-General shall not allow an abatement claim on goods imported on account of damage unless the claim is made on the first examination of the goods, or the person proves to the Commissioner-General's satisfaction that the damage was sustained before the goods were delivered out of customs control. The timing is the claim's gate: first examination, or proven pre-delivery damage.

Subsection (2): the Commissioner-General assesses the damage and may allow the abatement in proportion to the damage — the relief is proportional, not total. Subsection (3): the abatement is not allowed on account of damage for tobacco, cigars, cigarettes, beer, wine or spirits — the provision's own exclusion, quoted and applied. Subsection (4): derelict, jetsam, flotsam, lagan or wreck brought into the country pays the same duty as similar imported goods.

The learner's reading: the abatement's relief is proportional to proven pre-delivery damage, claimed at first examination, with the provision's own exclusion of the alcohol and tobacco lines — every condition quoted, none extended.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Drawback: the export-linked refund
BODY:
Section 106 provides the drawback — defined in subsection (1) as a refund of all or part of duties in relation to goods that are exported or used in a manner or for a prescribed purpose.

Subsection (2) states the two cases: goods imported and subsequently exported in the same condition as imported; and imported goods used for the manufacture of goods in the country and subsequently exported. The drawback's logic is the deferral family's mirror: the duty was paid on goods whose destiny is export, and the provision returns it.

Subsection (3) states the deemed exports: goods placed in a free zone or a duty-free shop; goods exported; goods designated as stores and supplied to a ship or aircraft outside the customs territory; goods used for the equipment, repair or construction of a prescribed ship or aircraft; and goods used or designated in any other manner the Commissioner-General determines.

Subsection (4) states the claim's deadline: a person applies for drawback within the period the subsection provides — a statutory deadline under section 106(4), stated with its provision.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The removal article: the defined personal relief
BODY:
Section 2(3) provides the removal article's exemption: a removal article is exempt from duty. Section 151's definition is the exemption's body: movable personal belongings or effects, domestic appliances, household provisions normally kept in stock, collectors pieces, pet animals, and any tools of trade other than agricultural, commercial or industrial plant necessary for the exercise of the calling, trade or profession — with the definition's own exclusions: arms, ammunition, motor vehicles, spare parts and building materials are not removal articles.

The definition's teeth are its exclusions: the returning resident's motor vehicle is not a removal article even though it is movable and personal; the spare parts are not; the building materials are not. The learner's reading: the removal article is a defined category, and the definition's exclusions are checked before the exemption is claimed.

The relief's discipline: the removal article's exemption is looked up in the definition — the categories and the exclusions — and the claim's file shows the goods within the definition's terms and outside its exclusions.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The claimant's evidence: what each relief demands
BODY:
Each relief's provision states what the claimant must show, and the learner's file is organised by the provision's demands.

The refund (s.104(1)): the contract of sale, the duty's payment, the non-conformity (description, quality, state, condition), the return or abandonment or destruction under control, and the non-use after release. The abatement (s.105): the claim's timing (first examination) or the proven pre-delivery damage, and the damage's assessment. The drawback (s.106): the import's duty, the export in the same condition or the manufacture-and-export, the deemed-export ground, the application within the period. The exemption (s.2): the goods' specification in the Harmonised System or the Schedules, or the removal article's definition met.

The learner's deliverable: the relief's claim file, organised by the provision's conditions, every condition evidenced, every figure labelled. The claim that cannot evidence a condition is a claim the provision does not grant — and the learner's advice says which condition is missing before the claim is made.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Damaged tiles: the abatement claim worked
BODY:
Step 1. The facts. The Kumasi trader's tile consignment (the Module 8 worked example's goods) arrives with one container's contents cracked — shipping damage discovered at the first examination. The duty was assessed on the full consignment.

Step 2. The provision matched. The damage is the abatement's subject: section 105(1)'s conditions — the claim made on the first examination of the goods — are met. The claim's timing is satisfied.

Step 3. The damage assessed. The Commissioner-General assesses the damage per section 105(2): the cracked tiles' extent is measured, and the abatement is allowed in proportion to the damage — not the whole consignment's duty, the damaged share's.

Step 4. The exclusion checked. The goods are tiles: section 105(3)'s exclusion — tobacco, cigars, cigarettes, beer, wine, spirits — does not reach them. The abatement's path is open.

Step 5. The claim's file. The file states the provision (s.105(1)-(2)), the timing's satisfaction (first examination), the damage's assessment, the proportional relief, and the exclusion's check. The figures are the claim's, labelled per the spec's rules.

What does this example demonstrate? The abatement's relief is proportional, timing-gated, and exclusion-checked — the provision's conditions worked in order on real goods.

Key lesson: the claim's file mirrors the provision's conditions — timing, assessment, exclusion — and the relief follows the file.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Drawback on re-exported machinery
BODY:
Step 1. The goods and the duty. A trader imports machinery, pays the duty through a home-use clearance, and the machinery is subsequently exported in the same condition — a buyer abroad takes it.

Step 2. The provision matched. Section 106(2)(a): goods imported and subsequently exported in the same condition — the drawback's first case. The duty paid on the import is the duty the drawback refunds.

Step 3. The deemed-export check. The machinery's export is an actual export; had the goods been placed in a free zone or a duty-free shop, or designated as stores for a ship outside the customs territory, subsection (3)'s deemed-export grounds would apply — the provision's own list, cited.

Step 4. The application's deadline. The claim is filed within the period section 106(4) provides — the statutory deadline, stated with its provision. The late claim is the missed claim.

Step 5. The file. The import's duty evidence, the export's evidence, the same-condition's proof, the application within the period — the drawback's claim file mirrors the provision's conditions.

What does this example demonstrate? The drawback is the export-linked refund: the duty paid on goods whose destiny was export, returned under the provision's conditions and deadline.

Key lesson: the same-condition export is the first case; the manufacture-and-export is the second; the deadline is statutory — and the file proves all three.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The tobacco and alcohol exclusion is the section's own
BODY:
Section 105(3) excludes the abatement on account of damage for tobacco, cigars, cigarettes, beer, wine or spirits — the provision's own exclusion, quoted and applied. The learner's discipline is to quote it rather than explain it: the exclusion's policy reasons are the legislature's, and the lesson does not improve them. The practical consequence: a damaged consignment of beer or spirits claims no abatement for the damage, however proven — the provision's own line closes the relief for those goods. The exclusion is also a teaching example of the provisions' specificity: the relief family's conditions include their own exclusions, and the claim's file checks the exclusions as carefully as the conditions. The section's line is quoted with its section; the learner's advice states the exclusion as the provision's, without extending it to goods the section does not name.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The relief's deadlines are statutory — stated with their sections
BODY:
The relief family's deadlines are statutory, and the learner states them with their provisions. Section 106(4) provides the drawback application's period — the claim filed within it, stated with the section. Section 105(1) provides the abatement's timing gate — the claim on the first examination of the goods, or the proven pre-delivery damage, to the Commissioner-General's satisfaction. Section 104's refund conditions run on the non-use after release. The deadlines and conditions are the reliefs' gates, and the file that misses a gate loses the relief's claim — lawfully, because the provision's conditions are the relief's terms. The learner's advice: the relief's claim is filed early, evidenced fully, and checked against the provision's conditions and exclusions — the gate's timing is the claim's first test, and the missed gate is the claim's lawful end.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
An importer discovers at first examination that imported machinery does not match the purchase contract's specifications. The duty was paid; the machinery is unused. The importer returns the goods to the supplier with the Commissioner-General's approval. Which option best states the relief position under section 104(1)?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. No relief: the duty was paid and the contract's failure is the supplier's problem.
2. The refund's conditions are met: the goods were imported in pursuance of a contract of sale, duty was paid, the goods at release did not match the contract's description or quality, the goods are returned to the supplier with the approval, and the goods were not used beyond discovering the mismatch — section 104(1)'s refund applies.
3. The relief is an abatement, proportional to the machinery's value.
4. The relief applies only if the machinery is destroyed.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 104(1) of Act 891 provides the refund where goods were imported in pursuance of a contract of sale, duty was paid, and the description, quality, state or condition of the goods at the time of release from customs custody was not in accordance with the contract — and the importer returns the goods to the supplier with the Commissioner-General's approval, or abandons or destroys them under customs control, and the goods have not been used after release beyond discovering the mismatch. The scenario's facts meet the conditions: the contract, the paid duty, the non-conformity, the approved return, the non-use.

Answer 1 abandons the relief the provision provides. Answer 3 misnames the relief — the abatement (section 105) is the damaged-goods relief, and the machinery's case is the contract-nonconformity refund. Answer 4 narrows the conditions — the provision offers return, abandonment, or destruction under control, and destruction is one of three.

The takeaway: the contract-mismatch refund's conditions are conjunctive — the contract, the paid duty, the non-conformity, the approved return, the non-use — and the claim's file evidences all of them.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of beer arrives with bottles cracked in transit. The damage is discovered at first examination, and the importer claims an abatement proportional to the damage. Which option best states the position under section 105?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The abatement is allowed proportionally, as with any damaged goods.
2. Section 105(3) provides the exclusion: the Commissioner-General shall not allow an abatement claim on account of damage in respect of tobacco, cigars, cigarettes, beer, wine or spirits — the beer's damage claims no abatement, whatever the proof.
3. The abatement is allowed because the claim was made at first examination.
4. The abatement is allowed at half the duty.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 105(3) of Act 891 provides the exclusion in its own words: the Commissioner-General shall not allow a claim for abatement of duty on account of damage in respect of tobacco, cigar, cigarette, beer, wine or spirit. The scenario's beer is squarely in the excluded list — the timing condition of subsection (1) (the claim at first examination) is met, and the exclusion still closes the relief, because the exclusion operates after the timing gate.

Answer 1 reads subsection (2)'s proportional-assessment machinery without the exclusion — the exclusion operates on the claim itself. Answer 3 treats the timing gate as the relief's whole; the gate is subsection (1)'s condition, and subsection (3)'s exclusion is the provision's own limit on the relief. Answer 4 invents a half-relief the provision does not state.

The takeaway: the relief family's exclusions are the provisions' own lines — the beer's damage is the section's named exclusion, and the learner quotes the exclusion rather than explaining it away.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
An importer pays duty on imported packaging materials, uses them to manufacture goods in Ghana, and exports the finished goods. Which option best states the relief and its conditions under section 106?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. No relief: the duty was properly paid on the import.
2. The drawback applies: section 106(2)(b) provides the drawback for imported goods used for the manufacture of goods in the country and subsequently exported — and the claim is filed within the period section 106(4) provides.
3. The relief is an abatement under section 105, proportional to the export.
4. The relief applies only if the imported materials are exported unchanged.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 106 of Act 891 provides the drawback — subsection (1) defines it as a refund of all or part of duties in relation to goods that are exported or used in a manner or for a prescribed purpose — and subsection (2)(b) states the case: imported goods used for the manufacture of goods in the country and subsequently exported. The scenario's imported materials, used in the manufacture and exported in the finished goods, are the provision's case — and subsection (4)'s application period is the claim's deadline, stated with its section.

Answer 1 ignores the relief the provision provides — the duty's refund follows the export. Answer 3 misnames the relief: the abatement (section 105) is the damaged-goods relief; the export-linked refund is the drawback. Answer 4 confuses the drawback's cases — subsection (2)(a) is the same-condition export; subsection (2)(b) is the manufacture-and-export, which is the scenario's.

The takeaway: the drawback's two cases — same-condition export, manufacture-and-export — with the deemed exports of subsection (3) and the deadline of subsection (4): the claim states its case and files in time.
