=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Section 3's mandate: the risk-based control, quoted
BODY:
Section 3(1) of Act 891 provides: the Authority shall conduct customs controls including random checks. Section 3(2): a customs control conducted under subsection (1) shall primarily be based on risk management, with the objective of (a) identifying and evaluating risks, and (b) developing counter-measures on the basis of risk management criteria developed and updated regularly from international, national and local level data.

The two provisions are the risk mandate's law: the control is conducted — including random checks — and the control's primary basis is risk management, with the objectives stated. The learner's reading: the Act does not permit a customs administration that examines everything; the Act requires a customs administration that manages risk, with random checks in the toolkit, and the objectives written into the provision.

The mandate's force is the module's foundation: the profiles, the registers, the targeting and the treatments of the next lessons are the mandate's implementation — and each lesson's machinery traces back to section 3's two provisions.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The objectives: identify, evaluate, counter-measure
BODY:
Section 3(2)(a)-(b) states the risk mandate's two objectives, and the learner should hold them as the module's definition of done.

The first objective: identifying and evaluating risks. The identification is the finding of what can go wrong — the risks to the revenue, the security and the facilitation; the evaluation is the measuring of which risks matter — the likelihood and the consequence.

The second objective: developing counter-measures on the basis of risk management criteria developed and updated regularly from international, national and local level data. The counter-measures are the responses — the profiles, the checks, the treatments — and their basis is the data: international (the conventions, the WCO's frameworks), national (the national risk picture), and local (the port's and the post's own experience).

The discipline the objectives teach: the counter-measures are data-driven and regularly updated — a counter-measure whose basis is habit rather than data is not the provision's counter-measure. The module's method — identify, analyse, evaluate, treat, review — is the objectives' implementation.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Why risk-based control, not uniform examination
BODY:
The Act's choice of risk-based control over uniform examination is a design decision with reasons the trade's scale makes plain.

The volumes: the training deck's workload exercise puts Tema's containers at roughly 500,000 TEUs a year — nearly two thousand TEUs per working day across the officer complement, and about two TEUs per officer per examination hour. Uniform examination of that flow is arithmetically impossible, and the deck's exercise makes the impossibility concrete: the officers' hours cannot cover the TEUs.

The design's answer: the risk system selects the goods whose examination matters, the rest clear on documents, and the random checks of section 3(1) keep the system's selections unpredictable — so the trader cannot game a known pattern. The design's trade-off is the trader's certainty: the examination is selective by law, and the trader's protection is the release conditions, the objection machinery and the audit — not a right to uniform treatment.

The learner's reading: the risk-based design is not an efficiency preference — it is the Act's stated method (section 3(2)), and its impossibility arithmetic is the deck's demonstration of why.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Joint control: the provision's international dimension
BODY:
Section 3(3) extends the risk mandate across borders: the Authority, in conjunction with cooperating foreign customs administrations, may carry out joint control and targeting activities based on risk management, to increase effectiveness in ensuring security in shipment and in combating transnational crime.

The provision's subjects: the joint control and targeting — the two administrations' systems and officers working the same movements together — based on risk management. The purposes: shipment security and the combat of transnational crime. The learner's reading: the risk mandate is not confined to Ghana's border; the provision authorises the cross-border cooperation that modern corridors require, and the cooperation's basis is the same risk management the domestic control runs on.

The module's boundary: the cooperation's operational detail — the shared systems, the joint teams — is the named-system and institutions territory (the fact sheets, planned), and this lesson's provision is the authority's anchor. The learner states the provision's authorisation and waits for the fact sheets for the machinery.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Confidentiality: the risk data's protection
BODY:
Section 3(4) provides the risk data's protection: specific information regarding risk management, including the risk register, the risk profile and other risk assessment data, is confidential. Subsection (5) adds the controlled exception: the Authority may employ a consultant to assist in the development and implementation of risk management programmes, as long as the confidential information is protected from disclosure.

The protection's subjects are named: the risk register (the recorded risks), the risk profile (the targeting criteria), and the other risk assessment data. The protection's reach: the trader cannot demand the criteria behind a selection, and the consultant's access is conditional on the protection's maintenance.

The learner's discipline: the confidentiality is the trader's boundary and the professional's. The trader's demand to know why his consignment was selected is answered by the provision: the risk data is confidential. The professional's advice does not speculate about the criteria — the speculation is the confidentiality's breach in spirit, and the provision's protection is the answer's authority.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The deck's foundations: the mandate in practice
BODY:
The risk deck grounds the mandate's practice in four frames the learner should carry.

The customs mandate's objectives (the deck's slide 3): protect import revenue and facilitate secure international trade; support public health, product standards and State security; compile trade data and perform duties for partner agencies; apply risk assessment to select controls and review results. The risk's definition (slide 4): risk is the effect of uncertainty on objectives — with the cause, the event and the impact distinguished. The risk sources (slide 5): external events, people and knowledge, relationships, processes and systems. The purpose (slide 6): identify, assess and prioritise threats; direct scarce staff and equipment towards higher risks; reduce the likelihood or impact of non-compliance; record decisions and improve controls through feedback.

The deck's frame and the Act's mandate meet: the section 3 objectives are the deck's purpose's statutory form, and the deck's definitions are the practice's vocabulary. The learner holds both: the provision's words and the practice's frame.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A consignment's risk-based selection at Tema
BODY:
Step 1. The arrival's data. A container vessel's manifest arrives electronically before the vessel: the goods, the origins, the routes, the traders — the advance data the control's first level reviews (the deck's control levels: pre-arrival, clearance, post-clearance, investigation).

Step 2. The system's selection. The risk system reads the manifest against the criteria: the goods' category, the route's pattern, the trader's history. One container is selected for examination; the rest are cleared on documents. The selection's basis is the risk data — confidential under section 3(4).

Step 3. The examination. The proper officer examines the selected container: the goods against the declaration, the quantities against the packing list. The findings are recorded — the deck's clearance slide: record the grounds and the examination findings, for the feedback and the future targeting.

Step 4. The feedback's loop. The examination's findings feed the risk criteria: the trader's history updated, the route's pattern confirmed or corrected — the criteria "developed and updated regularly" per section 3(2)(b), in operation.

Step 5. The trader's view. The importer's other consignments clear on documents; the selection is not a pattern the trader can predict — the random checks of section 3(1) and the confidential criteria keep the system's selections unpredictable.

What does this example demonstrate? The risk-based control's loop — data, selection, examination, feedback — is section 3's objectives in operation, and the confidentiality is the loop's protection.

Key lesson: the selection's why is confidential; the selection's lawfulness is section 3; and the trader's protection is the release conditions and the objection, not the criteria.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Risk-based control abroad: the same architecture
BODY:
Step 1. The scene. At a European administration, a consignment's declaration is lodged electronically before arrival: the advance data reviewed, the risk system selects, the goods clear on documents or are examined.

Step 2. The architecture's identity. The selection's basis is the risk criteria; the criteria are updated from the administrations' data; the criteria are confidential to the administration. The WCO's Revised Kyoto Convention's Chapter 6 standards — which the deck quotes: controls limited to compliance needs; risk management in customs control; risk analysis guides the examination of high-risk cargo — are the architecture's international statement.

Step 3. The Ghana connection. Act 891's section 3 states the same architecture in Ghana's statute: the controls primarily risk-based, the objectives stated, the data's levels named. The learner's reading: the architecture is the international design, and section 3 is its Ghanaian statute.

Step 4. The trader's protection abroad. The trader's protections abroad mirror Ghana's: the release conditions, the objection machinery, the audit — and the selection's criteria are confidential there too.

What does this example demonstrate? The risk-based architecture is the international design of customs control, and section 3 is its Ghanaian statement — the learner's knowledge is portable.

Key lesson: the architecture's concepts travel; the statute's words are the local anchor — and the learner holds both.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The selectivity is the law's, not the officer's mood
BODY:
The risk-based control's selections are the system's and the criteria's — and the learner must hold the selection's lawfulness against the opposite confusion: that an individual officer selects by preference. The provision's method (section 3(2)) is the criteria's risk management; the officer's role is the proper officer's acts — the examination, the release — performed within the selection's framework, by the competence the definitions assign. The learner's discipline: an examination's lawfulness is the selection's lawfulness plus the proper officer's competence — and the trader's challenge to a selection is a challenge to the system's criteria, which section 3(4)'s confidentiality protects. The two disciplines — the system's criteria and the officer's competence — are the selection's law, and neither is the officer's mood: the mood is not a provision, and the provisions are what the border's acts cite.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A trader demands to know the exact criteria behind his consignment's examination selection, citing his right to fair treatment. Which option best states the position under section 3?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The trader is right: the criteria must be disclosed for the examination to be lawful.
2. The trader's demand is answered by section 3(4): the specific information regarding risk management, including the risk register, the risk profile and other risk assessment data, is confidential — and the trader's protections are the release conditions and the objection machinery, not the criteria's disclosure.
3. The trader is right only if he requests the criteria in writing.
4. The criteria are disclosed to licensed agents on request.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 3(4) of Act 891 provides that specific information regarding risk management — including the risk register, the risk profile and other risk assessment data — is confidential. The trader's demand for the criteria is answered by the provision's protection: the criteria are the confidential risk data, and their disclosure is not the fairness mechanism the trader asserts. The trader's protections are the clearance's conditions (section 6(1)), the objection machinery (section 13), and the audit's records (sections 7 and 9).

Answer 1 invents a disclosure right that the confidentiality provision denies. Answer 3 invents a writing-based cure for a confidentiality rule. Answer 4 invents an agent disclosure that no provision carries — the agents hold the same confidentiality the officers do.

The takeaway: the risk data's confidentiality is statutory — the trader's protections run through the clearance's conditions and the dispute machinery, and the criteria stay behind section 3(4)'s wall.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
Which option best states the two objectives section 3(2) sets for the risk-based control?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Collect more revenue and reduce staff numbers.
2. Identifying and evaluating risks, and developing counter-measures on the basis of risk-management criteria developed and updated regularly from international, national and local level data.
3. Examining every consignment and prosecuting every discrepancy.
4. Eliminating all risk from international trade.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 3(2) of Act 891 states the risk-based control's objective in its own words: identifying and evaluating risks, and developing counter-measures on the basis of risk management criteria developed and updated regularly from international, national and local level data. The two objectives are the module's definition of done — the risks identified and evaluated, the counter-measures built on the data's levels and regularly updated.

Answer 1 substitutes revenue and staffing for the provision's objectives — the revenue's protection is the objectives' consequence, not their statement. Answer 3 is the uniform-examination design the provision's risk-based method replaces. Answer 4's elimination is not the objective — risks are identified, evaluated and countered, not eliminated; the uncertainty's effect is managed.

The takeaway: the objectives are the provision's — identify and evaluate, counter-measure on the data's levels — and the module's machinery is their implementation.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
The Authority engages a consultant to help build a new risk-profiling programme. Which option best states the condition section 3(5) attaches?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. No condition: consultants receive all risk data freely.
2. The Authority may employ the consultant to assist in developing and implementing risk management programmes as long as the confidential information — the risk register, the risk profile and the other assessment data — is protected from disclosure.
3. The consultant must be a foreign national.
4. The consultant's engagement must be approved by the trader whose goods are profiled.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 3(5) of Act 891 provides the consultant's controlled exception: the Authority may employ a consultant to assist in the development and implementation of risk management programmes, as long as confidential information is protected from disclosure. The subsection's structure is the confidentiality's design: the expertise may come in, the confidential data's protection is the condition of its use.

Answer 1 removes the condition the subsection states — the protection is the engagement's own term. Answer 3 invents a nationality requirement the provision does not carry. Answer 4 invents a trader-approval that would defeat the confidentiality — the profiled traders are exactly the people the criteria are protected from.

The takeaway: the consultant's exception is conditional — the expertise in, the confidentiality maintained — and the provision's condition is the engagement's own term.
