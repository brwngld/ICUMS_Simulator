=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The declaration as the goods' legal statement
BODY:
Module 4 defined the declaration and this lesson opens with its weight: a "declaration" is a statutory form filed or to be filed with respect to imported or exported goods at the time of entry or export. Statutory form means the particulars are legal statements — the goods' description, quantity, value, origin and procedure are not notes to the officer but assertions the law holds the maker to.

The "declarant" is the person who makes the declaration for customs purposes: the owner of the goods, the importer, the exporter, or the agent. Four identities, each with its own responsibility for what is stated — and in the electronic environment, the declarant's statement is lodged through the medium the previous lesson defined, with the electronic record preserving it and the electronic signature authenticating it.

The lesson's teaching point is the weight: preparation is the discipline of making statements that are true before they are lodged. Every particular the declaration carries must be verified against the supporting file before lodgement, because after lodgement the statements are the goods' legal position — and the false-statement definition reaches material errors and material omissions alike.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The consignment link: why the declaration must tie to reported goods
BODY:
A declaration does not float free: it belongs to a consignment — the goods that a conveyance actually reported on arrival. The link between the declaration and the reported goods is what the unique consignment reference exists to express: one reference that identifies the consignment, so that every declaration, every manifest line and every movement of the goods attaches to the same identified lot.

The rule behind the reference is the lesson's substance: the goods a declaration describes must be the goods the border actually received. The arrival report (section 17) brought the consignment into the system; the declaration claims goods from it; and the unique reference is how the system knows the claim is against the right lot. Without the linkage, a declaration could describe goods nobody reported — and the border would be assessing duties and granting releases for phantom consignments.

The learner's discipline: before lodging, fix the consignment — its reference, its reported quantities, its packages — and make the declaration's particulars match the report. The reference is the thread; the match is the rule; and the system's validation is that rule, wired.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The declarant's responsibility for the particulars
BODY:
Section 49 provides the declaration; the declarant makes it; and the declarant's identity is one of the four the Act fixes — the owner of the goods, the importer, the exporter, or the agent. The identity matters because the responsibility follows it: whoever makes the declaration has made statements about the goods, and the false-statement definition reaches those statements — false or misleading in a material respect, or a material omission from a required statement.

In the electronic environment, the responsibility is unchanged and the trail is complete: the lodged particulars are preserved as electronic records, authenticated by the electronic signature the Commissioner-General prescribes. The maker of the statement is identifiable; the statement is datable; and the audit provisions can revisit both years later.

The agent's position deserves its own sentence: the agent who lodges on behalf of a principal makes the particulars the agent's own statements too — the written authority under section 44 authorises the lodgement, but the verification of the particulars is the agent's professional discipline. The environment's speed never dilutes the statement's weight; it only makes the trail longer and more complete.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Pre-entry timing in the electronic environment
BODY:
Section 50's pre-entry — the entry of goods prior to their importation into Ghana — is the declaration's timing lever, and the electronic environment is what makes it powerful.

The pre-entry is a full declaration lodged before the goods arrive: the particulars prepared and verified, the supporting file assembled, the consignment identified from the bill of lading's voyage data. When the conveyance arrives and reports, the pre-entry links to the reported consignment, and the goods' entry machinery is already in motion — the release can follow examination or exemption quickly, because the statement was made and checked before the border ever saw the goods.

What the environment adds is the linkage and the trail: the pre-entry's electronic record persists, its authentication identifies the maker, and the consignment link is testable the moment the report arrives. The timing lever works because the system can match a pre-lodged declaration to a reported consignment — the linkage rule of this lesson, applied.

The learner's discipline: a pre-entry is not a draft. Its particulars are statements that take effect, and they are verified before lodgement exactly as any other declaration's are.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The supporting file and its failure mode
BODY:
Section 14 requires the documents to be submitted with the entry, and the electronic environment carries the requirement as attachments: the invoice, the transport document, the packing list, the permits and certificates the goods' procedure demands — lodged with the declaration as electronic records, authenticated and preserved.

Section 51 provides the failure mode: missing or incomplete documents. The entry that arrives without its full supporting file engages a provision built for exactly that state — and in the electronic environment the state is detectable at lodgement, because the attachment requirement is checkable before the declaration is accepted into the process.

The learner's discipline from Module 2 carries over unchanged: assemble the file first, verify it second, lodge it third. The electronic environment's contribution is that the file, once assembled, is complete in the record — the attachments are preserved with the declaration, and the audit trail of section 7 can read them years later.

The failure mode's lesson: the file is not an annex to the declaration; it is the declaration's proof — and the system's checks exist to refuse an unproven entry before it becomes an unproven clearance.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Preparation as verification: the rule before the lodging
BODY:
The declaration-preparation lesson closes with the rule that organises it: preparation is verification. The declarant's work before lodgement is not typing — it is checking, in a fixed order: the consignment identified (the reference, the reported goods), the particulars prepared (description, quantity, value, origin, procedure), the supporting file assembled (section 14's documents), the particulars verified against the documents, and the documents verified against each other.

Only after the verification chain is complete does the lodgement happen — and in the electronic environment, the lodgement is the moment the statements take legal effect, authenticated and preserved. The environment's validation will check the particulars again; the professional does not lodge particulars that need the system to find their errors, because a caught error is a delayed clearance and an uncaught error is a false statement.

The rule stated plainly: verification before lodgement, in that order, every time. The environment can validate; only the professional can verify — and the difference is the difference between a fast clearance and a false statement.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A pre-entry prepared and linked for a rice consignment
BODY:
Step 1. The consignment fixed. An importer's agent receives the bill of lading for a rice consignment still at sea: vessel, voyage, container numbers, 2,000 bags. The agent fixes the consignment's identity — the reference the declaration will link to — from the transport document.

Step 2. The particulars prepared. From the commercial invoice: description "long-grain white rice", quantity 2,000 bags, value in dollars, delivery term stated. From the packing list: forty containers, fifty bags each, weights per container. The agent prepares the declaration's particulars from these sources — not from memory or habit.

Step 3. The verification chain. The agent verifies particulars against documents: quantity matches invoice and packing list; the value matches the invoice; the delivery term matches the parties' arrangement. The documents verify each other: container counts agree across the bill and the list. The verification chain is complete before lodgement.

Step 4. The pre-entry lodged. The declaration is lodged electronically as a pre-entry under section 50 — the particulars taking their prepared form, authenticated by the prescribed electronic signature, the consignment reference linking the declaration to the goods the vessel will report.

Step 5. The arrival. The vessel arrives and reports; the report matches the bill of lading; the pre-entry links to the reported consignment. Examination or exemption follows, and release under section 6(1) comes quickly — because the statement was made and checked before the border saw the goods.

What does this example demonstrate? Preparation is verification in a fixed order, and the pre-entry's speed is earned by the checking done before it.

Key lesson: the environment can validate the lodgement; only the preparer can verify it — and the order is verification first.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A broken linkage caught before it became a clearance
BODY:
Step 1. The mismatch. At a foreign customs system, a broker lodges a declaration whose consignment reference does not match any reported arrival — the reference was typed against an earlier voyage's documents.

Step 2. The catch. The system's linkage check refuses the match: the declaration describes goods no report brings in. The lodgement is returned to the broker for correction — before any assessment, before any release, before the error could mature into a cleared consignment of the wrong goods.

Step 3. The correction. The broker re-checks the transport document, fixes the reference to the correct voyage, and re-lodges. The linkage now matches the reported consignment, and the process proceeds.

Step 4. The transfer. Ghana's environment applies the same rule through the same logic: the declaration must link to reported goods, and the linkage is checked before the declaration's consequences flow. The broker's correction cost minutes; the uncaught version would have cost a clearance of the wrong lot.

What does this example demonstrate? The consignment link is the declaration's first validation — and the rule's purpose is to stop wrong-lot clearances before they start.

Key lesson: the reference is the thread between report and declaration; a broken thread is caught cheaply at lodgement and expensively after release.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The consignment reference is a rule of linkage, not a screen field
BODY:
The unique consignment reference is taught in this module as a rule: the declaration must be linked to the consignment the border actually received, and the reference is how the link is expressed. This lesson does not teach the reference's format, its display, or the screen that carries it — those are the systems fact sheet's (ghana-electronic-systems.md, planned) named-system facts, and spec C7 assigns them out of the theory's scope. The reason the rule comes first is that formats change and rules do not: a reference's structure can be revised by the system's owners, but the linkage obligation — declaration matches reported goods — is the provision-backed principle that any format expresses. Learn the rule, and any format the system shows becomes a local expression of something you already know.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: Corrections exist; accuracy comes first
BODY:
The electronic environment provides correction machinery — a lodged declaration's errors can be amended through the processes the Act and the environment provide — and the learner must not read the existence of correction as permission for carelessness. The reason is the statement's weight: the lodged declaration is the goods' legal position from the moment it takes effect, and the trail of its original particulars is preserved as records. A corrected declaration leaves both versions in the trail; a pattern of corrections is a pattern the audit reads. The professional order is therefore fixed: verify before lodgement, correct through the machinery when errors surface, and never treat the amendment route as the verification step's substitute. The environment forgives slower than it forgets.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A broker lodges a declaration whose consignment reference does not match any arrival report in the system. Which option best describes what the linkage rule requires to happen?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The declaration proceeds, because the particulars are complete and the reference can be reconciled later.
2. The linkage check refuses the match — the declaration must link to the consignment the border actually received — and the lodgement returns for correction before its consequences flow.
3. The declaration proceeds and is treated as a new consignment that arrived without a report.
4. The reference field is optional, because the goods' description identifies them sufficiently.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's linkage rule states the substance behind the unique consignment reference: the declaration must be linked to the consignment the border actually received — the goods a conveyance reported on arrival under section 17. A reference that matches no report describes goods nobody brought in, and the system's linkage check exists to refuse exactly that state before the declaration's consequences (assessment, release) flow.

Answer 1 defers the linkage to a later reconciliation — the rule's point is that the match is made before the consequences, not after. Answer 3 invents a consignment from a declaration, reversing the direction the provisions run: the report brings the goods in; the declaration claims goods from the report. Answer 4 makes the reference optional on the strength of descriptions — but the description is the assertion, and the reference is the thread that ties the assertion to reported goods; without the thread, the assertion has no anchor.

The takeaway: the linkage rule is the declaration's first validation — the thread between report and declaration must hold before anything else happens.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
Which option best states the declarant's responsibility for the particulars lodged electronically?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The responsibility transfers to the system, because the system validates the lodgement.
2. The responsibility transfers to the shipping line, because the goods came on its vessel.
3. The declarant's responsibility is unchanged: the lodged particulars are the declarant's statements under the false-statement definition — verified against the supporting file before lodgement — and the electronic record and signature preserve and authenticate them for the audit trail.
4. The responsibility applies only to the value; descriptions and quantities are informational.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 151 of Act 891 defines the false statement as a statement made with respect to goods subject to customs control that is false or misleading in a material respect, or a material omission from a required statement — and the declaration's particulars are those statements, whoever lodges them. The electronic record definition preserves the lodgement as a record, and the electronic signature authenticates the maker, so the trail is complete: statement, maker, moment.

Answer 1 transfers responsibility to the software; the environment's validation is a check, not a liability shift — the statements are the declarant's. Answer 2 assigns the statements to the carrier; the carrier's documents are the movement facts the file proves, not the declaration's maker. Answer 4 narrows the statements to value; the definition reaches every material particular — description, quantity, origin, procedure.

The takeaway: the environment preserves and authenticates the statements; it does not make them — the declarant's verification before lodgement is the responsibility, and the trail is its permanent witness.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
An agent prepares a pre-entry for goods still at sea. Which option best states the professional order the lesson requires before the pre-entry is lodged?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Lodge first from habit, then verify the particulars against the documents as they arrive.
2. Fix the consignment identity from the transport document, prepare the particulars from the source documents, verify the particulars against the documents and the documents against each other — and only then lodge, because the pre-entry's particulars take legal effect and are preserved.
3. Lodge the pre-entry with blank particulars and fill them in when the goods arrive.
4. Skip the pre-entry, because lodging before arrival is unlawful.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's preparation rule is verification in a fixed order: the consignment identified (the reference from the transport document), the particulars prepared from the source documents (the invoice, the packing list), the particulars verified against the documents, and the documents verified against each other — the Module 2 consistency check — before lodgement. Section 50's pre-entry is a full declaration made early: its particulars take legal effect within the entry machinery, and the electronic record preserves them.

Answer 1 inverts the order — lodge from habit, verify later — which is the pattern of corrections the audit reads. Answer 3 invents a blank-form pre-entry; the pre-entry is a full entry whose particulars are statements, not placeholders. Answer 4 is false: section 50's pre-entry is the Act's own provision for lodging before importation, and its commercial value is exactly the speed the verified pre-entry earns.

The takeaway: preparation is verification in order — consignment, particulars, file, cross-checks — and the lodgement is the last step, not the first.
