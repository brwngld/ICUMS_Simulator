=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The manifest chain starts with the master's report
BODY:
The supporting-document chain does not begin with the declaration — it begins before the goods are discharged, with the master's report. Section 17 provides the report to be made on arrival of the conveyance: the craft's own statement of what it has brought into the country. Section 16 states the arrival obligations the report rides on, and section 18 supplies the consequence of failing to report or account for packages reported.

The report is the manifest chain's root because everything that follows reconciles to it: the discharge list counts what came ashore against what was reported; the declarations claim goods the report brought in; the transport documents' counts match the report's counts. In the electronic environment, the report's data enters the system at the border's edge, and every downstream document links back to it — the linkage rule of the previous lesson, applied at the arrival's edge.

The learner's picture of the chain: report (the craft's statement) → discharge (the supervised unloading under section 21) → storage in the controlled area (section 5(1)(b)) → declarations claiming goods from the report. The chain's proof is its consistency, and its root is the master's report.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Attachments: the evidence that travels with the entry
BODY:
Section 14 requires documents to be submitted with the entry, and the electronic environment carries them as attachments — the electronic record definition at work: a record created, generated, sent, communicated, received, or stored by electronic means. The invoice, the transport document, the packing list, the certificates: each lodged as an electronic record with the declaration, authenticated and preserved.

What the electronic form changes is reach and durability, not weight. The attachment is the same evidentiary statement the paper document was — the invoice's commercial facts, the packing list's physical facts — and the electronic record rules mean the evidence is preserved with the declaration for the audit trail of section 7. The authentication rules ensure the attachment's maker is identifiable: an electronic signature authenticates what was lodged and by whom.

The learner's discipline transfers intact: attachments are verified before lodgement, consistent with the declaration and with each other. The environment's contribution is that the verified file is complete in the record — the attachments cannot be separated from the declaration they prove.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Permits: the restricted-goods allowance, evidenced
BODY:
Certain goods may cross the border only as the law allows, and the allowance is evidenced by a permit or certificate from the controlling agency. Section 151's definitions frame the states: "prohibited goods" are goods whose importation or exportation is prohibited by law; "restricted goods" are goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law. Restricted goods move under the condition the restriction imposes — and the permit is the evidence that the condition is satisfied.

In the electronic environment, the permit travels as an attachment to the declaration — an electronic record evidencing the agency's allowance. The officer's check is the check this lesson's chain supports: the goods' description and code indicate the restriction; the declaration claims compliance; the attached permit evidences it. The permit does not waive the control — it evidences the allowance that makes the control's answer "yes".

The agencies' mandates — the Ghana Standards Authority's requirements, the Food and Drugs Authority's controls — are Module 6's subject. This lesson's scope is the documentary shape: the permit as the attachment that turns a restricted good's entry from an assertion into an evidenced claim.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: What the officer checks: the chain's consistency
BODY:
The supporting-document chain is checked for consistency, and the check runs in a fixed order: the manifest against the arrival report; the declaration against the manifest; the attachments against the declaration; the goods against all of them.

Each link's failure is a defined state. A manifest line with no declaration behind it is goods nobody claimed. A declaration referencing goods the manifest does not carry is a claim against goods nobody reported — the linkage rule of the previous lesson. An attachment inconsistent with the declaration it supports — a packing list's counts against the declared quantity — is a discrepancy section 51's missing-or-incomplete-documents provision addresses.

The electronic environment makes the check faster, not weaker: the linkage and consistency checks are wired into the system's validation, because the chain's rules are provisions and the provisions can be sequenced. The learner's contribution is unchanged: the file that arrives at the check should already be consistent — assembled, verified and reconciled before lodgement, so that the wired check confirms what the professional already knows.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Cargo and stores in the manifest
BODY:
The manifest chain's entries are cargo — and section 151's definition draws the line the manifest must respect: cargo is goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage. The consigned goods the declarations claim are cargo; the vessel's own supplies and the passengers' bags are not, and they follow their own provisions — section 24's aircraft and ship stores regime among them.

The split matters to the manifest because the manifest's entries are the declaration claims' source: a stores item listed as cargo would present goods for declaration that the procedures do not declare; a cargo item omitted as stores would be goods in the controlled area with no claim behind them. The reconciliation the chain performs assumes the categories are right.

The learner's discipline: read the manifest's categories against the definitions — cargo, stores, baggage — and treat a miscategory as a discrepancy to resolve before the declarations attach. Module 13 returns to the stores regime for duty-free shops and controlled consumption; the manifest's duty is to keep the categories straight at the chain's root.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Authentication: attachments that identify their makers
BODY:
The electronic signature definition — the electronic method of authentication prescribed by the Commissioner-General — is what makes the attachment chain accountable. Every electronic record in the chain — the declaration, the attachments, the permits — is authenticated by the prescribed method, which means the record's maker is identifiable and the record's content is attributable.

Why the accountability matters to a documents lesson: the supporting file is a system of statements. The invoice states the commercial facts; the packing list states the physical facts; the permit states the agency's allowance; the declaration states the trader's claim. Each statement is made by someone, and the authentication is how the system knows whose statement each record is. The officer's check reads the chain's consistency; the audit's check reads the chain's accountability; and the authentication serves both.

The learner's discipline: attachments are lodged under the prescribed authentication, by the party responsible for them — the trader's documents by the trader's side, the agency's permits by the agency's process. A file whose records cannot identify their makers is a file the chain's accountability cannot support.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A pharmaceutical consignment with its permit attached
BODY:
Step 1. The goods. An importer's consignment of pharmaceuticals arrives at Kotoka. The goods are restricted: their importation is allowed only as the law provides, with the Food and Drugs Authority's approval evidenced.

Step 2. The permit. Before lodgement, the importer's agency approval is obtained — the FDA's allowance for the consignment — and it enters the file as an electronic record, authenticated by the process that issued it. The permit names the goods, the quantities and the allowance it evidences.

Step 3. The lodgement. The agent lodges the declaration with the supporting attachments: the commercial invoice, the transport document, the packing list, and the permit. Each is an electronic record; each is authenticated; each is preserved with the declaration.

Step 4. The officer's check. The goods' description and classification indicate the restriction; the declaration claims compliance; the attached permit evidences the allowance — and the permit's goods, quantities and authenticity match the declaration and the file. The chain is consistent, and the control proceeds.

Step 5. The release. Section 6(1)'s conditions are met — examination or exemption, no offence, documents acquired, stated conditions (the permit) satisfied — and the goods are released.

What does this example demonstrate? The permit is the attachment that turns a restricted good's entry into an evidenced allowance — and the chain's consistency is what makes the evidence checkable.

Key lesson: the restricted good's file must carry its allowance; the officer's check reads the evidence, not the assertion.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A missing attachment caught at lodgement
BODY:
Step 1. The lodgement. An agent lodges a declaration for machinery consigned to a Ghanaian buyer, with the invoice and transport document attached — but the certificate the machinery's regime requires is not in the file; it is still with the issuing body.

Step 2. The wired check. The environment's attachment requirement is checkable at lodgement: the goods' procedure demands the certificate, the declaration's particulars indicate it, and the file lacks it. The lodgement is not accepted into the process as complete; the missing attachment is identified before the declaration's consequences flow.

Step 3. The correction. The agent obtains the certificate from the issuing body, attaches it as an electronic record, and the lodgement proceeds complete. The delay is measured in the obtaining, not in a post-release correction.

Step 4. The counterfactual. Had the environment accepted the incomplete lodgement silently, the gap would have surfaced at the officer's check — a clearance in process with an unproven claim, and a correction that costs more the later it happens.

What does this example demonstrate? Section 51's failure mode is wired into the environment — the missing attachment is caught at lodgement, where the correction is cheapest.

Key lesson: the wired checks are the provisions' protection; the professional's job is to arrive at them with the file complete.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: Permits evidence allowances; they do not waive controls
BODY:
A restricted good's permit evidences the allowance the restriction's condition requires — and the learner must not read it as a waiver of customs control. The goods are still declared, examined or exempted, and released under section 6(1)'s conditions; the permit satisfies one of the stated conditions — the allowance the restriction demands — while the rest of the clearance runs as it would for any goods. The distinction matters in advice: a client holding an agency permit has the restricted good's precondition, not an exemption from the clearance. The officer's check still runs — the permit is verified, the goods are compared, the conditions are met — and the permit's evidentiary role is exactly why it must be in the file: it is the document the check reads.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The agencies' portals are outside this lesson's scope
BODY:
The agencies' electronic processes — how the Ghana Standards Authority or the Food and Drugs Authority issue their approvals, and how those approvals reach the customs environment — are named-system facts and agency-process facts, and this lesson deliberately stops before them. The systems fact sheet (ghana-electronic-systems.md, planned) will carry the named-system detail; the institutions fact sheet will carry the agencies' process detail; and spec C7 keeps the screens out of the theory. What this lesson teaches is the documentary shape: the permit is an electronic record evidencing the allowance, attached to the declaration, authenticated, preserved. The rule is stable; the portals will change around it. The learner who needs the portal detail in practice verifies it from the agency's own published processes — with the citation.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of restricted goods arrives with its agency permit in the file. Which option best states what the permit does in the clearance?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The permit waives customs control over the goods entirely.
2. The permit replaces the declaration; only the permit needs to be lodged.
3. The permit evidences the allowance the restriction's condition requires — it satisfies one of section 6(1)'s stated conditions, while the rest of the clearance (declaration, control, release) runs as it would for any goods.
4. The permit transfers ownership of the goods to the issuing agency.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's permit block states the documentary shape: restricted goods — defined in section 151 as goods whose importation or exportation is restricted by law — move under the condition the restriction imposes, and the permit is the evidence that the condition is satisfied. The evidence's place in the clearance is one of section 6(1)'s stated conditions: the required documents acquired, the stated conditions met — while the declaration, the control decision and the release run as they run for any goods.

Answer 1 turns the allowance into a waiver; the goods remain subject to the clearance's full machinery — declared, examined or exempted, released. Answer 2 makes the permit a substitute for the declaration; the permit evidences one condition, it does not state the goods' particulars. Answer 4 invents a transfer that no provision carries.

The takeaway: the permit is the allowance's evidence, not a control's exemption — the restricted good's clearance runs the whole machinery with the permit in the file.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
In the manifest chain, which of the following would NOT be listed as cargo within the meaning of section 151?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. A consignment of declared tiles in a container.
2. Undeclared goods discovered in the same container, awaiting their declaration.
3. The vessel's own fuel and the crew's personal luggage.
4. A consignment of machinery for an importer, manifested on the voyage.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 151 of Act 891 defines cargo as goods other than stores or passenger or crew baggage. The vessel's own fuel is a store — the craft's own supply, which section 24's regime governs — and the crew's luggage is passenger or crew baggage: both are excluded from cargo by the definition's own words. The declared tiles, the undeclared goods awaiting declaration, and the manifested machinery are all cargo — goods the manifest chain's declarations claim, including the undeclared ones that section 5(1)(b)'s storage receives.

The distractors are all genuine cargo: the definition's exclusion is narrow and specific — stores and passenger or crew baggage — and the learner's discipline is to keep the manifest's categories aligned with it, because the declarations claim cargo and the other categories follow their own provisions.

The takeaway: cargo is the manifest chain's declared-and-declarable goods; stores and baggage travel under their own regimes — and the manifest that blurs the categories will disagree with the declarations it feeds.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
A declaration is lodged without the certificate its goods' procedure requires. Which option best states the position and the lesson's discipline?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The lodgement proceeds and the certificate can be attached whenever convenient.
2. The certificate is optional, because the declaration's particulars state the goods' origin.
3. Section 51 provides for missing or incomplete documents, and the environment's attachment requirement is checkable at lodgement — the missing certificate is identified before the declaration's consequences flow, and the discipline is to assemble section 14's file completely before lodging, so the correction is measured in the obtaining.
4. The missing certificate converts the goods into prohibited goods automatically.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 51 of Act 891 provides for missing or incomplete documents, and the lesson's worked example shows the wired check: the attachment requirement is checkable at lodgement — the goods' procedure demands the certificate, the declaration's particulars indicate it, the file lacks it — and the lodgement is not accepted into the process as complete. The correction happens where it is cheapest: at the obtaining, before the consequences flow.

Answer 1 defers the evidence indefinitely; the declaration's claim is unevidenced until the certificate is in the file, and the consequences should not flow ahead of it. Answer 2 mistakes the assertion for the evidence — the origin stated in the particulars is proved by the certificate, which is why the certificate is the file's requirement. Answer 4 confuses an evidentiary gap with a prohibition; the goods are not prohibited, the entry is incomplete.

The takeaway: the wired checks catch the gap at lodgement — and the professional arrives at them with the file complete, because the correction is cheapest at the obtaining.
