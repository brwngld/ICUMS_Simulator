=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The declaration's lifecycle as legal states
BODY:
A lodged declaration moves through states, and the learner must read each state as a legal condition — a statement about what has been satisfied — not as a screen label. The states the environment expresses are the provisions sequenced: lodged (the declaration has taken effect under section 49, with section 14's documents); validated (the particulars have been checked for completeness and consistency); assessed (the duty and charges have been computed on the customs value); paid (section 73's payment has settled the liability); released (section 6(1)'s conditions are met, and the goods leave customs control).

Each state exists because a provision requires it, and the states' order is the provisions' order: the goods cannot be assessed before they are declared, or released before the duty is settled and the conditions met. The environment cannot run the sequence out of order, because the environment is the provisions, wired.

The learner's discipline: when reading any declaration's position, ask which condition the state expresses and which provision requires it. The answer tells the professional what must still happen — and which provision's failure would explain a stall.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Validation: the particulars' check
BODY:
Validation is the environment's check that the declaration's particulars are complete and consistent — and its legal content comes from the provisions it serves.

Completeness: the declaration's required particulars are present, and section 14's documents are attached — the file the entry needs, with section 51 as the missing-documents failure mode. Consistency: the particulars agree with each other and with the attachments — quantities, weights, descriptions, values — because the declaration is proved by its file, and a mismatched file impeaches itself.

The check is wired: the environment validates at lodgement and flags what fails. But the wired check is the provisions' protection, not the professional's substitute — the lesson 5.2 discipline stands: verify before lodgement, so that validation confirms rather than discovers. A caught error is a delayed clearance; an uncaught error is a false statement in the record. The professional's aim is that validation finds nothing, because verification found everything first.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The payment hooks: liability, calculation, payment
BODY:
The declaration's assessed state rests on three provisions, and this lesson names the hooks — the arithmetic is Module 8's.

Section 71 provides liability for duty: who is liable, and for what. Section 72 provides the calculation: how the duty is computed from the classification, the customs value and the rates. Section 73 provides payment: when and how the liability is settled.

In the electronic environment, the assessed state is the calculation performed — the tariff row from the classification, the value from the declaration and its rules, the charges on their bases — and the paid state is section 73's settlement completed. The learner's discipline at this stage of the lifecycle: read the assessed amount's structure (which charges, which bases) and verify the inputs (the classification, the value) — because the payment settles what was assessed, and an assessed error is settled error unless corrected first.

The module's boundary holds here: the calculation's method and figures are Module 8's. The lifecycle lesson needs the hooks, not the arithmetic.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Release, applied electronically
BODY:
Section 6(1)'s release conditions are the lifecycle's goal, and the electronic environment applies them as literally as the paper era did: the goods have been examined or exempted from examination (the control decision, made in the environment by the proper officer's competence); no offence has been found; the required documents have been acquired (the attachments, validated); the stated conditions are met (the permits, the procedure's conditions); and the duty has been settled (the payment state).

Release is the Commissioner-General's act — the provisions invoke the office — expressed through the environment's processes. When the conditions are met, the release state follows; the goods leave customs control; and the delivery handover of Module 3 takes over.

The learner's reading of any stalled release: the conditions are the checklist, and the stall is the unmet condition. The environment shows the state; the provisions name the condition; the professional names the provision and the remedy — the permit outstanding, the duty unsettled, the documents incomplete — because every stall is one of section 6(1)'s conditions failing.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Record keeping: the trail the lifecycle leaves
BODY:
Every state the declaration passes through leaves records, and section 9's duty keeps them: the owner, importer, consignee, exporter, entry filer and others required to keep records maintain them — and section 151's definitions make the electronic trail records like any paper file: the electronic record covers information created, sent, received or stored by electronic means, and the record's width reaches statements, declarations, documents, data, books, papers, correspondence, accounts and financial accounting data.

The trail's purpose is the audit window: section 7's post-clearance audit revisits the clearance after release, and the records are what it reads — the declaration's states, the attachments, the payment, the officer's decisions. The environment preserves the trail as a by-product of its operation; the trader's duty is to keep the trader's side of it.

The learner's discipline: the lifecycle's records are not clutter — they are the clearance's proof, assembled as it runs. The declaration that was validated, the permit that was attached, the duty that was paid: each state's record is the answer to a question the audit may ask years later.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The failed states: what a stall means legally
BODY:
The lifecycle's stalls are as legal as its states, and the learner must read them the same way.

A validation failure means the particulars or the file failed the completeness and consistency check — section 51's territory, section 14's file. An assessment hold means the duty's inputs are unresolved — the classification, the value, the rate; Module 8's questions. A payment hold means section 73's settlement is incomplete. A release hold means section 6(1) has an unmet condition — the outstanding permit, the unresolved control.

Each stall names its provision, and the provision names the remedy. The learner's diagnostic is the same one Module 3 taught for port threads: name the state, name the provision, name the remedy — and never confuse a legal stall with an operational one, because their remedies live in different places.

The failed states are also provisions' protections: the environment refuses to run the sequence out of order, because the order is the law's. A stall that looks like obstruction is often the provisions holding the line the Act drew.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A declaration's journey from lodged to released
BODY:
Step 1. Lodged. The agent lodges the declaration electronically — the particulars of a consignment of tiles, the supporting file attached, the authentication identifying the maker. The lodged state: section 49's declaration has taken effect, and the electronic records preserve it.

Step 2. Validated. The environment's check confirms the particulars' completeness and consistency — the file agrees with the declaration, because the agent verified before lodging. The validated state: the provisions' check has confirmed what the professional already knew.

Step 3. Assessed. The classification's tariff row, the customs value and the rates produce the assessment: the duty, the import VAT, the charges — each on its base. The assessed state: sections 71 and 72's work is computed; section 73's settlement is next.

Step 4. Paid. The importer settles the assessed amount through the payment channel — section 73's payment completed. The paid state: the liability is settled, and the release conditions are nearly assembled.

Step 5. Released. The control decision exempts the goods from physical examination; no offence is found; the documents are acquired; the stated conditions — the goods' procedure carries none outstanding — are met. Section 6(1) is satisfied, and the Commissioner-General's release ends the customs control. The goods pass to delivery.

What does this example demonstrate? The lifecycle's states are the provisions, sequenced — lodged, validated, assessed, paid, released — and each state's condition is a provision's requirement.

Key lesson: read the states as provisions, and any stall names its own remedy.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A validation failure resolved where it was cheapest
BODY:
Step 1. The failure. A declaration lodged for machinery consignment fails validation: the declared quantity reads 12 units, while the attached packing list states 14 — and one attachment, the certificate the goods' procedure demands, is absent.

Step 2. The read. The validation failure names the provisions: the consistency check (the declaration against the file) and the completeness check (section 14's documents) — section 51's territory. The stall is legal, not operational: no remedy at the terminal applies.

Step 3. The correction. The agent establishes the true count from the source documents, corrects the declaration's quantity to 14, and obtains the missing certificate from the issuing body — attaching it as an electronic record. The corrected lodgement passes validation, because the file now agrees with the declaration and the file is complete.

Step 4. The counterfactual. Had the mismatch lodged silently, the discrepancy would have surfaced at the officer's check — or worse, at the audit — with the clearance further down the line and the correction more expensive.

What does this example demonstrate? A validation stall is the provisions holding the line — the particulars' check serving section 51 and section 14 — and the remedy is the file's correction, where it is cheapest.

Key lesson: the stall names the provision; the provision names the remedy; the remedy is cheapest at lodgement.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: Statuses are legal conditions, not screen labels
BODY:
The environment displays the lifecycle's states with labels, colours and queues — and the learner must read through the presentation to the legal condition each state expresses. "Validated" means the particulars' completeness and consistency check has passed; "assessed" means sections 71 and 72's computation exists on the classification, the value and the rates; "released" means section 6(1)'s conditions are met and the goods have left customs control. A label is the environment's rendering of a provision's satisfaction — and the rendering can change with the system, while the condition does not. The learner's discipline: when reading any status, ask which provision's satisfaction it expresses, and check the condition itself rather than the label's wording. The habit guards against the environment's changes, against the professional's own shorthand, and against the client who quotes a screen's colour as if it were a legal conclusion.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The audit reads the electronic trail
BODY:
The lifecycle's electronic trail — the lodged particulars, the validations, the attachments, the assessment, the payment — is a record within section 151's definition, and section 9's duty keeps it. Section 7's post-clearance audit is the reader: years after release, the trail can be revisited, and the declaration's states can be checked against the file and the goods. The learner's discipline follows: the trail is complete because the environment preserves it, and the trader's side — the source documents, the correspondence, the accounts — is kept under section 9's duty for the audit window the provision states. The electronic environment does not lighten the duty; it lengthens the trail's memory. The professional whose file matches the trail has nothing to fear from the audit's memory — and the professional whose trail disagrees with the file has already met the problem this lesson's verification discipline prevents.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A declaration fails validation because the declared quantity disagrees with the attached packing list. Which option best states what the validation failure means legally, and the correct remedy?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The failure means the system is faulty; the remedy is to retry the lodgement until it passes.
2. The failure means the goods are prohibited and must be destroyed.
3. The failure is the particulars' consistency check engaging — the declaration must be proved by its file, and the mismatch is section 51's missing-or-incomplete territory; the remedy is to establish the true count from the source documents, correct the declaration, and re-lodge — cheapest at this stage.
4. The failure means the declaration must be abandoned and a new consignment imported.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's lifecycle lesson reads the states as provisions: validation is the particulars' completeness and consistency check, serving section 14's document requirement and section 51's missing-or-incomplete-documents provision. A quantity mismatch between the declaration and its own attachment is the check finding a self-impeaching file — and the remedy is the Module 2 discipline: establish the truth from the source documents, correct the declaration, re-lodge. The stall is cheapest here, at lodgement, before the consequences flow.

Answer 1 blames the tool for what the check found — the wired check is the provisions' protection, and retrying without correcting lodges the same mismatch again. Answer 2 jumps to destruction, which is no part of the validation machinery. Answer 4 abandons a correctable entry for no provision's sake.

The takeaway: a validation stall is a provision holding the line — name the check that failed, correct the file or the particulars, and the sequence resumes where the law allows.

Which option best states the legal hooks behind the assessed and paid states of a declaration's lifecycle?

CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The lesson's payment block names the provisions the assessed and paid states serve: section 71 of Act 891 provides liability for duty — who is liable and for what; section 72 provides the calculation — how the duty is computed from the classification, the customs value and the rates; and section 73 provides the payment — the settlement of the assessed liability. === MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
An importer's goods were released three years ago. The Authority opens a post-clearance audit of the declaration. Which option best states what the audit will read, and why the trader's position is prepared or not?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The audit reads nothing, because released goods are beyond all review.
2. The audit reads only the officer's notes, because the trader's records are private.
3. The audit reads the electronic trail — the lodged particulars, the validations, the attachments, the payment — preserved as records under section 151's electronic-record definition — and the trader's own records kept under the section 9 duty; the trader whose file matches the trail is prepared, because section 7's audit window is exactly what the kept records answer.
4. The audit reads only the trader's bank statements.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 7 of Act 891 provides the post-clearance audit — the mechanism that revisits a clearance after release — and it reads the trail the lifecycle left: the lodged declaration and its particulars, the validations, the attachments, the assessment and the payment, all preserved as electronic records within section 151's definition (records created, generated, sent, received or stored by electronic means). The trader's side is section 9's duty — the records kept for the retention period the provision states — and the two sides together are what the audit reads. The trader whose source documents match the lodged particulars has the prepared position; the trader whose trail disagrees with the file meets the problem the lesson 5.2 verification discipline prevents.

Answer 1 invents a finality that section 7's existence denies — the audit's whole purpose is post-release review. Answer 2 and Answer 4 narrow the audit's read to single documents with no basis; the record definition's width is the audit's reach.

The takeaway: the lifecycle's trail is the audit's reading material — kept by the environment, owed by the trader, and matched by the professional.
