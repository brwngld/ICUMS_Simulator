QUESTION:
A trainee is asked to classify boneless fresh beef and opens the workbook. Which option best states the descent the trainee should run, with the verified rows?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Chapter 1, because beef comes from cattle, which are live animals.
2. Chapter 2 ("Meat and edible meat offal"), its Note 1 checked (the goods are fit for consumption, not excluded), heading 2.01 ("Meat of bovine animals, fresh or chilled"), then the dash rows discriminated — boneless lands on TSN 0201.30.00.00, with the row's SU Kg, CET ID 35, VAT 15 labelled illustrative of the structure.
3. Chapter 15, because beef is a fat product.
4. The trainee should find the TSN first and work upward, because the row is the law.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The workbook's Table 4 states the structure: Chapter 2 with its Note 1 exclusions, heading 2.01 ("Meat of bovine animals, fresh or chilled"), and the dash rows — 0201.10.00.00 (carcasses and half-carcasses), 0201.20.00.00 (other cuts with bone in), 0201.30.00.00 (boneless) — each carrying SU Kg, CET ID 35, VAT 15. The goods' facts (boneless, fresh, bovine) descend the hierarchy: chapter checked against its note, heading matched by its terms, TSN discriminated by the dash rows.

Answer 1 confuses the live-animal chapter with the meat chapter; the goods are not alive. Answer 3 misplaces the goods by their fat content's association. Answer 4 inverts the method — the row is where a correct classification lands, and the descent is how the landing is decided.

The takeaway: the descent runs chapter → note → heading → dash rows → TSN, and every landed row is cited to its table with the figures labelled.

=== ASSESSMENT Q5 | tests lesson 7.2 ===
QUESTION:
An unassembled complete bookcase is entered. Which option best states the GRI route, with the rule's own wording?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. GRI 2(a): any reference in a heading to an article includes that article complete or finished entered unassembled or disassembled — the unassembled bookcase takes the assembled bookcase's heading, provided the goods are the article entered unassembled.
2. GRI 3(c): the unassembled bookcase takes the last heading in numerical order.
3. GRI 6: the unassembled bookcase is compared at the subheading level only.
4. The bookcase cannot be classified until it is assembled.
CORRECT: 1
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 1. GRI 2(a), quoted from the verified text: any reference in a heading to an article shall be taken to include a reference to that article incomplete or unfinished, provided that, as entered, the incomplete or unfinished article has the essential character of the complete or finished article; it shall also include a reference to that article complete or finished (or falling to be classified as complete or finished by virtue of this rule), entered unassembled or disassembled. The unassembled bookcase is the rule's case: the article entered unassembled.

Answer 2 routes through GRI 3(c), which operates only where goods are prima facie classifiable under two or more headings after GRI 1 and 2 — not where GRI 2 has widened one heading to reach the goods. Answer 3 misassigns GRI 6, which governs the subheading comparison, not the unassembled question. Answer 4 abandons the machinery the rule provides.

The takeaway: GRI 2's limbs widen the headings — the unfinished, the unassembled, the mixed — and the widening happens before any competition question arises.

=== ASSESSMENT Q6 | tests lesson 7.2 ===
QUESTION:
Goods are prima facie classifiable under two headings, and neither provides a more specific description of the whole. Which option best states the GRI 3 route, and the descent that follows?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. GRI 3(a) decides by the trader's preference.
2. GRI 3(b) applies immediately: the goods take the essential character.
3. GRI 3's steps run in order — 3(a)'s specificity (with its equal-specificity qualification for part-only descriptions of sets and composites), then 3(b)'s essential character where 3(a) does not decide, then 3(c)'s last-in-order — and GRI 6 governs the descent into the subheadings, where only subheadings at the same level are comparable, mutatis mutandis.
4. GRI 3 does not apply; the officer chooses the heading.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The verified text of GRI 3 states the ordered steps: 3(a) — the heading providing the most specific description preferred to the more general, with the qualification that headings each referring to part only of the materials in mixed or composite goods, or part only of the items in a set put up for retail sale, are equally specific; 3(b) — classification as if the goods consisted of the material or component giving them their essential character, insofar as applicable; 3(c) — the heading last in numerical order among those equally meriting consideration. GRI 6 then governs the subheading descent — the terms of those subheadings and any related subheading notes, mutatis mutandis, with only subheadings at the same level comparable.

Answer 1 invents a preference route. Answer 2 skips 3(a) — the essential character operates where 3(a) does not decide. Answer 4 abandons the rules for discretion.

The takeaway: GRI 3's steps are ordered and exhaustive, and GRI 6's level-by-level comparability is what makes the descent legal — the rules run in order, and the classification stops at the first step that decides.

3. It is a court judgment that binds all importers.
4. It is the officer's verbal assurance at the border.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines the customs advance ruling as a binding written statement issued by customs that interprets and applies customs laws and regulations to a specific set of facts upon application by a trade about a prospective transaction, and section 12(1) states the provision's own words: the Commissioner-General may issue a written ruling applying the customs law to a particular set of facts submitted by an interested party. The correct answer is Answer 2. The takeaway: prospective facts, ruling (s.12); existing decisions, objection (s.13) — and the advice names the moment before it names the instrument.

=== ASSESSMENT Q12 | tests lesson 7.4 ===
QUESTION:
A trader argues his goods should take the same TSN as a consignment cleared last month, "because they are identical". The goods differ from the compared consignment in one material respect. Which option best states the position under the Act's definitions?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The trader is right: identical goods always take identical TSNs.
2. The trader's comparison must be tested against the definitions: section 151 defines identical goods as goods the same in all respects, including physical characteristics, quality and reputation, disregarding minor differences in appearance — and similar goods as goods with like characteristics and like component materials enabling the same functions and commercial interchangeability. A material difference takes the goods outside "identical"; the argument becomes a re-examination run on the rules and the rows.
3. The trader is wrong because identical goods never exist.
4. The trader's comparison decides the classification by itself.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 151 of Act 891 defines the comparators the dispute borrows: identical goods — the same in all respects, including physical characteristics, quality, and reputation, disregarding minor differences in appearance — and similar goods — goods which, although not alike in all respects, have like characteristics and like component materials which enable them to perform the same functions and to be commercially interchangeable. The scenario's material difference takes the goods outside the identical definition; the comparison's remaining value is as a question — the rules and the rows re-examined for the trader's goods on the trader's own trail.

Answer 1 applies the consequence without the definition's precondition. Answer 3 overstates the negative — the definitions exist because comparators matter. Answer 4 makes the comparison self-executing; the classification is decided by the rules and the rows, and the comparison is a question they re-examine.

The takeaway: "identical" and "similar" are defined states, tested by their definitions — and the comparison that fails the test becomes a re-examination, run on the cited trail.
