=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The Harmonised System is where the rate lives
BODY:
Section 1(1) of Act 891 imposes the duties and taxes on prescribed goods imported or exported "at the rate of duty and tax specified in the Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System" — the Harmonised System. Section 151 then defines that nomenclature: the headings, sub-headings and their related numerical codes, the section, chapter and sub-heading notes and the general rules for the interpretation of the Harmonised System, contained in the text of the International Convention approved by the Customs Co-operation Council on 14th June, 1983 and the relevant Schedules attached to it.

The two provisions together are the classification lesson's foundation: the Act imposes duty at a rate that lives in a nomenclature, and the nomenclature is incorporated into Ghanaian law by the definition. The learner's duty question — "what rate applies to my goods?" — is answered by finding the goods' place in that nomenclature, which is the skill this module teaches.

Section 1(2)(a) adds the second hook: the imposed duties and taxes may include any other tax, duty or levy on goods prescribed under any other enactment — the provision that carries the VAT and the levies alongside the customs duty.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The hierarchy: sections, chapters, headings, subheadings — and the TSN layer
BODY:
The Harmonised System is a hierarchy, and Ghana's tariff adds a layer beneath it.

At the top, sections group the chapters: Section I, "Live Animals", opens the workbook's schedule. Within each section, chapters carry numbered titles: Chapter 1, "Live animals"; Chapter 2, "Meat and edible meat offal" — the workbook's Table 4 shows the chapter heading and its notes. Within each chapter, headings carry four-digit codes and their goods descriptions: heading 2.01, "Meat of bovine animals, fresh or chilled". Within the headings, subheadings carry six-digit codes and dashes: the "- Carcasses and half-carcasses", "- Other cuts with bone in", "- Boneless" rows beneath heading 2.01.

Ghana's layer is the Tariff Subheading Number — the TSN — the ten-digit code beneath the HS subheading: 0201.30.00.00 for boneless bovine meat, in the workbook's Table 4. The TSN is where the rates attach: the workbook row's CET ID and VAT columns carry the charges for that TSN.

The hierarchy is the classification's map: the goods descend from section to chapter to heading to subheading to TSN, and every level's text and notes constrain the level below.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Reading a tariff row: each column's role
BODY:
A tariff row is a complete answer once its columns are read, and the workbook's columns each do one job.

The Heading column carries the HS heading or the structural marker — the chapter number, the heading number. The TSN column carries the ten-digit Tariff Subheading Number — the classification's final address. The Commodity Description column carries the goods text — the heading's description and the dash-level subdivisions beneath it, which are the words classification is decided by. The SU column carries the supplementary unit — "Kg" for the meat rows, "u" for units of live animals — the quantity measure the charges read. The CET ID column carries the customs duty figure for the TSN. The VAT column carries the VAT rate. The IE column carries the import-excision entry where one applies.

Reading the row 0201.30.00.00 — boneless bovine meat: heading 2.01, TSN 0201.30.00.00, description "Boneless", SU Kg, CET ID 35, VAT 15. The row is a complete rate answer for goods that classify there: the duty figure, the VAT figure, the unit.

What the row does not carry is the classification itself — the row is where a correct classification lands, and the next lesson teaches how the landing is decided.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The notes in the schedule: law, not commentary
BODY:
The workbook's tables carry more than rows: the sections and chapters carry notes, and the notes are law. Table 4 opens with Chapter 2's Note 1, quoted from the workbook: "This Chapter does not cover: (a) Products of the kinds described in headings 02.01 to 02.08 or 02.10, unfit or unsuitable for human consumption; (b) Guts, bladders or stomachs of animals (heading 05.04) or animal blood (heading 05.11 or 30.02); or (c) Animal fat, other than products of heading 02.09 (Chapter 15)."

The note excludes goods from the chapter by naming where they belong instead — the unfit products to Chapter 5's headings, the animal fat to Chapter 15. A classification that ignored the note would place goods in a chapter that legally does not cover them, whatever the heading text suggested.

The notes' binding force comes from the HS definition itself: section 151's definition includes "the section, chapter and sub-heading notes" in the nomenclature the Act incorporates. The notes are part of the law the learner applies — read them before trusting a heading's apparent fit, because the note may be the provision that moves the goods.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: Titles are for ease of reference only
BODY:
The first words of the General Rules for Interpretation — verified against the WCO's published text — are the hierarchy's warning: the titles of sections, chapters and sub-chapters are provided for ease of reference only; for legal purposes, classification is determined according to the terms of the headings and any relative section or chapter notes. The chapter's title, in other words, is a signpost, not the law. A good that seems to belong to a chapter by its title may belong elsewhere by a heading's terms or a note's exclusion — and the workbook's Chapter 2 note is exactly such a provision: it moves goods out of the chapter the title announces. The learner's discipline: treat every title as a pointer, and start the legal work at the heading text and the notes. The title tells you where to look; the headings and notes tell you where the goods legally are.
