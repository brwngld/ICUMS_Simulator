=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: What the certificate evidences, and what section 70(6) provides
BODY:
Section 70(6) states the origin claim's documentary forms: a person shall provide an invoice declaration of origin or, where applicable, a Certificate of Origin to the proper officer on request by the proper officer. Two forms, one purpose — the origin claim's evidence.

The invoice declaration of origin is the exporter's origin statement made on the invoice itself, in the form the regime requires. The Certificate of Origin is the attested document: the issuing body's authentication of the origin claim, describing the goods and certifying their origin. The form a claim uses is the regime's own — the agreements and their instruments state which form their claims carry, and the learner quotes the regime's requirement rather than assuming it.

What both forms evidence is the origin determination: the goods satisfy the regime's origin rules. The certificate does not create the origin — the goods' materials and transformations made it — and the certificate's evidentiary role is to make the origin checkable: the officer's verification reads the document against the goods and the file.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The certificate's data points
BODY:
The certificate's fields are the claim's handles, and the officer's verification tests each: the exporter identified; the consignee identified; the goods described — matching the commercial invoice's description; the origin criterion or rule the goods satisfy, stated; the reference to the commercial invoice that ties the certificate to the consignment's papers; the quantities and weights that match the packing list; and the issuing body's authentication — the mark, number and signature that make the attestation checkable.

Each field's purpose is the verification's handle: the description ties the certificate to the goods; the invoice reference ties it to the commercial file; the authentication ties it to the issuing body's records. A certificate whose fields do not match the consignment is a certificate whose claim cannot be verified — and the mismatch is found by the field check before any deeper question arises.

The learner's discipline: the certificate's fields are checked against the file before lodgement — the same consistency discipline Module 2 taught, applied to the origin document.

HEADING: Who issues and attests: the generic answer
BODY:
The issuing and attesting role for certificates of origin belongs to the bodies the regimes authorise — and this lesson describes the role generically, for the reason the sourcing discipline states: the institutions fact sheet (ghana-customs-institutions.md, planned) will carry the named bodies with citations, and the learner does not name an issuing chamber or authority from memory. The generic description covers the role: the issuing or attesting body receives the exporter's origin claim, examines it against the regime's rules and the supporting evidence, and authenticates the certificate — its mark and number making the attestation checkable at the border. The attestation is the body's own statement, and the border's verification can reach it through the authentication.

The learner's practice: the certificate's authentication is checked as a field — the mark, the number, the issuer's identity as the regime states them — without asserting a named body the sources do not name. Generic until sourced: the rule the whole programme's institutional statements run on, because a named body wrongly stated is a claim the body's own records can refute.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: How the certificate attaches to the declaration
BODY:
The certificate's journey to the clearance runs through the declaration's supporting file. Section 14 requires the documents to be submitted with the entry; the origin evidence is among them, and the electronic environment carries it as attachments — the electronic record definition at work, the certificate lodged, authenticated and preserved with the declaration.

The officer's check reads the chain: the declaration claims the origin; the certificate describes the goods and their origin criterion; the certificate's invoice reference matches the commercial invoice; the goods' quantities match the packing list; and the transport document ties the consignment together. The chain's consistency is the claim's checkability — and the certificate's fields are the chain's handles.

The learner's discipline: the certificate is lodged with the declaration it supports, not promised for later; the claim without its attached evidence is the unevidenced claim of Module 9.2 — and the officer's request under section 70(5)-(6) is answered by the file, not by assurances.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The evidence chain: four documents, one story
BODY:
The origin claim's evidence chain runs through four documents, and each contributes its layer.

The commercial invoice states the commercial facts: the parties, the goods, the quantities, the prices. The transport document states the movement facts: the craft, the route, the consignment's counts. The certificate of origin or invoice declaration states the origin facts: the goods' origin claim, under the regime's form and authentication. The declaration states the trader's claim to customs: the goods, their classification, their origin, their procedure.

The chain's proof is its consistency: the goods described in the invoice are the goods in the transport document are the goods in the certificate are the goods in the declaration. The chain's failure is the claim's failure: a description that differs at any link breaks the verification's path.

The learner's discipline: the origin claim's file is the whole chain — not the certificate alone — and the chain's consistency is checked before lodgement, because the officer's check reads the whole chain.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: Retention: the certificate in the records
BODY:
The origin evidence's life does not end at release. Section 9's record-keeping duty keeps the records — section 151's record definition reaching statements, declarations, documents, data, books, papers, correspondence, accounts — and the certificate is among them. Section 7's post-clearance audit is the reader: the origin claim verified at the border can be revisited after release, and the certificate in the records is the claim's answer.

The learner's discipline: the origin file — the certificate or invoice declaration, the commercial invoice, the transport documents, the production evidence where the regime requires it — is retained for the audit window the provision states, stated with its provision. The claim's afterlife is the audit's reading, and the file's retention is what makes the claim's afterlife survivable.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: Shea butter to Europe: the certificate chain
BODY:
Step 1. The claim. A Ghanaian processor exports shea butter to a European buyer under a preference regime whose rules the goods satisfy: the butter is wholly obtained and processed in Ghana, and the regime's form is a Certificate of Origin.

Step 2. The certificate obtained. The processor applies to the issuing body with the claim's evidence: the goods' description, the production records, the materials' origin. The body attests the certificate — the goods described, the origin stated, the invoice referenced, the authentication applied.

Step 3. The chain assembled. The agent checks the certificate's fields against the file: the description matches the commercial invoice; the invoice reference matches the invoice's number; the quantities match the packing list; the transport document's consignment matches the certificate's goods. The chain is consistent.

Step 4. The lodgement. The export declaration carries the certificate as its origin evidence — lodged, authenticated, preserved. The border's verification reads the chain, and the claim travels with the goods.

Step 5. The buyer's side. At the destination, the buyer's declaration claims the preference, and the certificate's chain answers the destination officer's check. The claim's evidence travelled with the goods.

What does this example demonstrate? The certificate's chain is built at the origin — fields checked, file assembled — and the chain is what the destination's verification reads.

Key lesson: the origin evidence is assembled before shipment, because the claim's checkability is the claim's value.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: An invoice declaration under an agreement abroad

Key lesson: whichever form the regime requires, the claim's evidence is the exporter's declaration or the body's attestation — and the officer's check reads the form's fields against the goods.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A preference regime's claim uses an invoice declaration of origin rather than a Certificate of Origin. Which option best states what section 70(6) provides and what the declaration is?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Section 70(6) requires a Certificate of Origin for every claim; the invoice declaration is insufficient.
2. Section 70(6) provides that a person shall provide an invoice declaration of origin or, where applicable, a Certificate of Origin to the proper officer on request — and the invoice declaration is the exporter's origin statement made on the invoice itself, in the form the regime requires; the form a claim uses is the regime's own.
3. Section 70(6) provides that origin claims need no documentation at all.
4. Section 70(6) requires both the invoice declaration and the Certificate of Origin for every claim.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 70(6) of Act 891 states the origin claim's documentary forms in its own words: a person shall provide an invoice declaration of origin or, where applicable, a Certificate of Origin to the proper officer on request by the proper officer. The provision names two forms, and the regime's requirement states which form a claim uses — the invoice declaration being the exporter's origin statement on the invoice, the Certificate being the attested document of the issuing body.

Answer 1 collapses the two forms into the certificate; the provision's "or" is express. Answer 3 abandons the documentation the claim's verification needs — the origin claim is provable under section 70(5)'s machinery, and the forms are its proof. Answer 4 doubles the requirement; the regime's own text states its form.

The takeaway: section 70(6) names the forms, and the regime picks — the invoice declaration is the exporter's statement, the certificate is the body's attestation, and the officer's request is answered with whichever the regime requires.

An origin claim's consignment is released, and two years later the Authority opens a post-clearance audit of the origin claim. Which option best states what answers the audit, and why?

2. The takeaway: the origin claim's afterlife is the audit's reading — and the retained file, kept under the section 9 duty, is what the claim's answer is written in.
