=== ASSESSMENT Q1 | tests lesson 9.1 ===
QUESTION:
A customs officer states a consignment's origin "for the marking and the trade statistics", and separately a buyer claims a preferential rate under a trade agreement for the same goods. Which option best states how section 70 organises the two determinations?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both determinations follow the preferential rules, because both concern origin.
2. Both determinations follow the non-preferential rules, because the goods are the same goods.
3. Section 70 distinguishes the families: subsection (1) provides the preferential rules — origin determined for applying preferential tariff or non-tariff measures (the buyer's claim); subsection (2) provides the non-preferential rules — origin for all other purposes, including the country-of-origin marking and statistics (the officer's statement). The purpose picks the family.
4. The families apply by the goods' value: high-value goods take the preferential rules.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 70(1) of Act 891 provides that preferential rules of origin are the rules used to determine the origin of goods for purposes of applying preferential tariff or preferential non-tariff measures; subsection (2) provides that non-preferential rules of origin are the rules used for all other purposes, including the country-of-origin marking and statistical purposes. The two determinations in the scenario serve different purposes — the buyer's claim is the preferential family's; the marking and the statistics are the non-preferential family's — and the purpose picks the family and its rules.

Answer 1 collapses the families; the preferential rules exist for the measures' claims, and the marking is expressly subsection (2)'s purpose. Answer 2 dissolves the distinction on the goods' identity — the same goods can have differently purposed origin determinations. Answer 4 invents a value rule the section does not carry.

The takeaway: the origin's purpose picks the family — the claim runs the preferential rules, the marking and the statistics run the non-preferential rules — and the learner names the purpose before the rules.

=== ASSESSMENT Q2 | tests lesson 9.1 ===
QUESTION:
A processor's cream blends locally grown shea butter with imported coconut oil. Which option best states the origin concept that applies, and the vocabulary it runs on?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Section 70(3): the cream is wholly obtained, because it was manufactured in Ghana.
2. Section 70(4): the cream was produced in more than one country, so it originates where it underwent its last substantial transformation — and the concept's vocabulary is section 151's: produced means grown, manufactured or mined; manufacture means to make, produce, or cause to be made or produced, goods; raw material means goods from which other goods are capable of being manufactured and any residue from any process of manufacture.
3. The cream's origin follows the imported coconut oil, as the dominant ingredient.
4. The cream's origin is undetermined, because mixtures have no origin rules.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 70(4) of Act 891 provides that goods produced in more than one country or territory are deemed to originate in the country or territory where the goods underwent their last substantial transformation — and the blended cream's inputs come from two origins, so the wholly-obtained concept of subsection (3) does not reach it. The concept's vocabulary is section 151's, quoted: produced means grown, manufactured or mined; manufacture means to make, produce, or cause to be made or produced, goods; raw material means goods from which other goods are capable of being manufactured and any residue from any process of manufacture. Whether Ghana's blending is the last substantial transformation is the regime's rule's question.

Answer 1 claims wholeness the imported input breaks. Answer 3 substitutes an ingredient-quantity intuition for the transformation's legal question. Answer 4 abandons the concepts that subsection (4) provides precisely for mixtures.

The takeaway: the blend's origin is its last substantial transformation — and the analysis runs on the Act's defined vocabulary, applied to the goods' materials and processing.

=== ASSESSMENT Q3 | tests lesson 9.1 ===
QUESTION:
A client asks whether his goods "can be Ghanaian origin because my company is Ghanaian, even though the materials and the processing are all from abroad". Which option best states the position under the origin concepts?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The client is right: the company's nationality makes the goods Ghanaian origin.
2. The client is right if the company's headquarters are in Ghana.
3. The client's reasoning attaches the origin to the wrong subject: section 70's concepts ask about the goods — wholly obtained where (s.70(3)), last substantially transformed where (s.70(4)) — and the materials and processing being from abroad places the goods' origin story abroad, whatever the company's nationality.
4. The client is right if the goods are imported by sea.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. Section 70's origin concepts attach to the goods: subsection (3) regards goods wholly obtained in a single country, or wholly produced from domestic materials, as originating there; subsection (4) deems multi-country goods to originate where they underwent their last substantial transformation. The client's goods — materials and processing from abroad — tell an origin story that is not Ghanaian under either concept, and the company's nationality is not among the concepts' elements.

Answer 1 imports the trader's nationality as the origin — the confusion the lesson's block dismantles. Answer 2 adds the headquarters to the same confusion. Answer 4 invents a transport-mode test with no basis in the concepts.

The takeaway: the origin is the goods' story — materials, processing, transformation — and the trader's pedigree is commercial background, not origin evidence. The claim's evidence is the goods' story.
