=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The verification: section 70(5)'s proof power in action
BODY:
Section 70(5) provides the origin claim's verification trigger: where a person indicates an origin of goods in a declaration, the Commissioner-General may require that person to prove the origin of the goods. The verification is the machinery's working moment — the claim made in the declaration is tested by the request for proof, and the declarant's obligation is to answer.

The proof's forms are section 70(6)'s: the invoice declaration of origin or the Certificate of Origin, provided to the proper officer on request — and the proof's wider file is the records the claim rests on: the production evidence, the materials' origin, the transformation's documentation. The verification's question is the origin rules' question — do the goods satisfy the regime's test? — and the proof answers it with the regime's evidence.

The learner's discipline: an origin claim is lodged with its proof ready, because the request can come — and the claim's file is the answer's body.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The records that answer
BODY:
The verification's answer is the records, and section 9's duty is what keeps them. Section 151's record definition is wide: information kept in the ordinary course of business pertaining to any customs activity — statements, declarations, documents, data, books, papers, correspondence, accounts, financial accounting data. The origin claim's records sit inside it: the production records showing the goods' making, the materials' origin documents, the transformation's documentation, the certificates and invoice declarations, the correspondence with the issuing bodies.

The records' answer has a shape: for each element of the origin rules' test, the records show the facts — the materials' sources for the wholly-obtained claim, the transformation's evidence for the substantial-transformation claim, the regime's documentary forms for the claim's formal requirements. The records that cannot answer an element leave the claim's element unproven — and the unproven element is the claim's weakness.

The learner's practice: the origin file is organised by the test's elements, with each element's evidence ready — because the verification's questions are the test's questions, and the file's structure is the answer's speed.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The failed claim: denial and re-assessment
BODY:
The verification that fails has a consequence, and the learner states it at the mechanism's level: the preference is denied — the goods do not receive the regime's rate — and the duty is re-assessed at the general rate, with the difference becoming the goods' additional liability.

The mechanism's disciplines: the denial's grounds are the origin rules' test failing — the goods do not satisfy the regime's origin, or the evidence does not satisfy the regime's forms. The re-assessment's amount is the general rate's duty on the same goods — the difference between the claimed preference and the general liability. The machinery's detail — the notices, the timelines, the recovery procedures — is the instruments' and the Regulations' content, and the lesson states the mechanism without inventing its detail.

The learner's boundary: the denial's consequence is stated at the mechanism's level (denied preference, re-assessed duty); the offence machinery for false origin claims is Module 14's territory; and the amounts stay labelled or unverified per spec C4.4.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The remedies: objection and ruling
BODY:
The origin dispute's lawful routes are the Act's two instruments, chosen by the moment.

The objection (section 13) is the retrospective route: the trader who disagrees with a decision already taken — the denial, the re-assessment — objects through the machinery the provision provides, with the timing and form the Act states. The advance ruling (section 12) is the prospective route: the trader whose goods' origin treatment is uncertain for a future transaction requests the written ruling — and section 12(3) names country of origin among the ruling's subjects expressly.

The learner's formula, from lesson 7.3: prospective facts, ruling (s.12); existing decisions, objection (s.13). An origin dispute worked with the wrong route costs the client the instrument's benefit — the ruling obtained for goods already assessed is a paper the assessment does not answer, and the objection filed for goods not yet shipped is a paper the process does not receive.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The audit connection: the claim revisited after release
BODY:
Section 7's post-clearance audit is the origin claim's long memory: the preference verified at the border can be revisited after release, and the audit reads the claim's trail — the certificate or invoice declaration, the production evidence, the records the section 9 duty kept.

The audit's origin questions are the test's questions, asked again: do the records show the goods satisfied the regime's test? The trader's answer is the retained file — and the file's completeness is what the audit's questions find. The claim whose file was complete at the border is the claim whose file answers the audit; the claim whose evidence was promised at the border is the claim whose audit finds the promise unkept.

The learner's discipline: the origin claim's afterlife is the audit's reading, and the retention discipline of section 9 is the claim's protection — the origin file retained for the audit window the provision states, with the production evidence the regime's rules require.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The professional's prevention: the verified claim's file
BODY:
The origin dispute's best resolution is its prevention, and the prevention is the verified claim's file: the origin rules' test known (from the regime's instrument or the fact sheet), the goods' facts documented against the test, the regime's documentary forms obtained before lodgement, and the file retained for the audit window.

The prevention's checklist runs per claim: which regime's preference is claimed; which test the regime's rules apply; which evidence the test demands; which documentary form the regime requires; whether the goods' facts satisfy the test as evidenced; whether the file is retained. Each check's answer is in the file — and the claim that passes the checklist is the claim whose verification finds what the file already showed.

The learner's deliverable: the origin claim's file as the verification's mirror — the claim's every element answered by the file's every section, retained past release, and structured for the audit's reading.
