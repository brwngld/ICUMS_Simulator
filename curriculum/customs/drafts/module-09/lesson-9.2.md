=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The two families' purposes, from the Act
BODY:
Section 70's opening distinction is the purpose distinction, and the learner holds it as the module's map.

Preferential rules of origin — subsection (1) — determine origin for the purpose of applying preferential tariff or preferential non-tariff measures: the goods claim a regime's benefit, and the regime's rules decide whether the goods earn it. Non-preferential rules of origin — subsection (2) — determine origin for all other purposes, including the country-of-origin marking and statistical purposes: the goods' origin is stated for the measures that apply by origin, without a benefit claim.

The families' evidence differs too. The preferential claim carries the regime's documentation — the invoice declaration of origin or the Certificate of Origin that section 70(6) names. The non-preferential statement carries the marking and the statistical record. The learner's first question for any origin issue: which purpose is the origin asked for? The answer picks the family, the rules and the evidence.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: What a preference rule tests
BODY:
The preference regimes' rules test the goods' claim to the regime's origin, and the tests' types are the lesson's map — stated conceptually, with the thresholds reserved to the instruments.

The wholly-obtained test: the goods are grown, produced or mined entirely within the regime's territory — the section 70(3) concept, applied by the regime. The change-of-classification test: the goods are produced from inputs of other origins, and the manufacturing changes them enough that the outputs classify differently from the inputs — the transformation measured by the tariff's own headings. The process test: the goods undergo a specified process — the regime names the processes that qualify. And the value-based tests measure the inputs' share against the outputs' value.

Each regime writes its own combination of these tests, and the tests' thresholds are the instruments' texts — quoted from the instruments or the fact sheet, never from memory (spec C4.7). The learner's concept-level skill: recognise which test type a regime is applying, and know that the threshold's wording is the instrument's, verified.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: How the claim changes the duty outcome
BODY:
The preferential claim's effect is the duty outcome: the same goods, the same classification, the same customs value — different duty, because the regime's preference applies.

The mechanism: the goods' classification lands on its tariff row; the row's rate is the duty the goods would pay under the general regime; the preference regime's rate replaces or reduces that duty where the origin claim succeeds. The claim's evidence — the invoice declaration or the certificate — is what makes the preferential rate's use lawful; without the evidence, the general rate applies.

The learner's picture: two consignments of identical goods, one with the regime's evidence and one without — the first pays the preferential duty, the second pays the general rate. The goods did not change; the evidence did. And the evidence's validity is the origin rules' question — which is why the rules are tested, and why the unverified claim is the professional's refusal.

The discipline: the preference's duty outcome follows the evidenced claim — and the claim's evidence is only as good as the origin rules' test behind it.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The instruments Ghana trades under
BODY:
The regimes Ghana's goods claim under are the agreements the Republic has concluded — section 70(7)'s own reference — and the verified facts this programme carries are the learner's anchors.

The ECOWAS Common External Tariff: implemented in Ghana from 1 February 2016, with five bands — 0 percent for essential social goods, 5 percent, 10 percent, 20 percent, and 35 percent for specific goods for economic development (WEB-verified 2026-11-16, GRA/ECOWAS reporting). The Revised Kyoto Convention: Ghana acceded on 29 June 2019 (the WCO's contracting-parties list). The WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement: Ghana's acceptance deposited 4 January 2017, the agreement in force from 22 February 2017 (the WTO's TFA facility). The continental agreement's annexes are among the supplied sources (the AFU file), and their rule patterns are quoted with annex references where used.

The learner's discipline: each instrument's status is cited with its source; each instrument's thresholds are quoted from the instrument or the fact sheet; and no regime's benefit is asserted without both.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The non-preferential side: marking and statistics
BODY:
Section 70(2)'s non-preferential family serves the purposes that apply by origin without preference — and the deck-teaching examples are the country-of-origin marking and the statistics.

The marking: goods carry their origin's statement for the market's information and the measures that apply by origin. The statistics: the trade's records state the origin the non-preferential rules determine. The non-preferential origin also feeds the measures that apply by origin — the restrictions, the conditions, the treatments that particular origins carry.

The learner's discrimination: the non-preferential origin is not a lesser origin — it is a differently purposed one. The preferential origin earns a benefit; the non-preferential origin states a fact for the measures and the records. A consignment may have no preference claim at all — and its non-preferential origin is still determined, still stated, and still provable under section 70(5)-(6)'s machinery.

The learner's practice: the origin's purpose is named before the origin is stated — because the purpose picks the family, and the family picks the rules.

=== KEY CONCEPT ===
HEADING: The documentation: section 70(6)'s two forms
BODY:
Section 70(6) names the origin claim's documentary forms: the invoice declaration of origin, and the Certificate of Origin where applicable — provided to the proper officer on request.

The invoice declaration is the exporter's origin statement on the invoice itself — the regime's approved wording, made by the exporter whose goods carry the claim. The Certificate of Origin is the attested document — the issuing body's authentication of the origin claim. Which form applies is the regime's own: the agreements and their instruments state the form their claims use, and the learner quotes the regime's requirement rather than assuming one.

The two forms' common discipline: both are evidence of an origin determination made under rules — the exporter's declaration or the issuing body's attestation — and both are checked by the officer against the goods and the file. The forms' content is the claim's handle: the goods described, the origin stated, the reference to the invoice — the fields the officer's verification tests.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: The same goods under two regimes
BODY:
Step 1. The goods. A Ghanaian processor exports processed shea butter to two buyers: one in a market whose agreement grants Ghana's goods a preferential rate; one in a market with no agreement, where the general rate applies.

Step 2. The preferential consignment. The first buyer's market grants the preference — and the claim's machinery runs: the goods' origin is determined under the agreement's rules (the transformation tests this Module 9.1 taught conceptually); the invoice declaration or certificate evidences the claim; the goods enter at the preferential rate.

Step 3. The non-preferential consignment. The second buyer's market has no agreement to claim: the goods enter at the general rate, with the origin stated for the marking and the statistics under the non-preferential rules. The origin statement is still made — and still provable under section 70(5)-(6) — but the claim carries no preferential outcome.

Step 4. The comparison. The goods are the same; the classification is the same; the customs value is the same. The duty differs — because one consignment carried the regime's evidenced origin claim and the other carried none.

What does this example demonstrate? The preferential claim's duty outcome follows the evidenced claim — and the same goods without the evidence pay the general rate.

Key lesson: the evidence is the preference — the origin rules test the goods, the documents prove the test, and the duty follows the proof.

=== WORKED EXAMPLE ===
HEADING: A preference claim abroad, tested
BODY:
Step 1. The scene. A European importer claims a preferential rate for garments under a trade agreement: the declaration states the preference, and the file carries the origin evidence the regime requires.

Step 2. The test. The customs administration tests the claim against the agreement's rules: the goods' transformation meets the rule's threshold; the documentation's form matches the regime's; the exporter's declaration is within the regime's machinery.

Step 3. The outcome. The claim succeeds, and the preferential rate applies. Had the transformation test failed, the preference would be denied — the general rate applies, and the machinery for denial and re-assessment (Module 9.4's subject) follows.

Step 4. The transfer. The test's shape is the one Module 9.1 taught conceptually: the wholly-obtained or transformation question, asked by the regime's rules, answered by the regime's evidence. Ghana's exports face the same testing in the destination markets — and Ghana's imports of preferential goods are tested by the same shape under the regimes Ghana has joined.

What does this example demonstrate? The preference's mechanics — rules test, evidence proves, rate follows — are the same in every regime; the thresholds are the local layer.

Key lesson: the preference is tested, evidenced and granted — and the professional's file is what the testing reads.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: No thresholds from memory
BODY:
The preference regimes' tests have thresholds — the change-of-classification's scope, the value-add percentages, the named processes — and the lesson's discipline refuses every threshold quoted from memory. The spec's C4.7 states it: the instruments' content is quoted from the instruments or the fact sheet, matched exactly. The reason is the regimes' negotiating precision: a threshold paraphrased is a threshold changed, and the changed threshold is a claim the instrument's text can falsify at verification. The learner's practice: the concept-level tests are this lesson's; the thresholds are quoted from the instrument — with the annex or article reference — or withheld until the instruments fact sheet (customs-instruments-and-standards.md, planned) supplies them with citations, and the client is told which case their claim is in, because the preference and the client's money ride on the wording.

=== IMPORTANT NOTE ===
HEADING: The CET's bands are structure, labelled
BODY:
The ECOWAS Common External Tariff's five bands — 0 percent for essential social goods, 5 percent, 10 percent, 20 percent, 35 percent for specific goods for economic development — are the tariff's structure, and the lesson states them with their verified source (WEB-verified 2026-11-16, GRA/ECOWAS reporting) and their implementation date (1 February 2016). The bands are the tariff's shape, not the goods' rates: a specific good's band is read from the tariff's row, verified against the rate in force. The learner's discipline: the bands are quoted as the structure they are, with the verified source cited; the goods' rates are the schedule's rows, labelled per spec C4.2; and the structure and the rates are never confused — the bands organise the tariff, the rows price the goods.

=== MCQ 1 ===
QUESTION:
A consignment of goods is exported with no preferential claim — the destination market grants no agreement benefit. The exporter still states the goods' origin on the shipment's papers. Which option best states which origin family and purpose that statement serves?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The preferential family, because every origin statement is a preference claim.
2. The non-preferential family: section 70(2) provides that non-preferential rules determine origin for all other purposes, including the country-of-origin marking and statistical purposes — the statement serves the marking and the records, without a benefit claim.
3. Neither: origin is stated only for preference claims.
4. The preferential family, because the goods are exported rather than imported.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. Section 70(2) of Act 891 provides that non-preferential rules of origin are the rules used to determine the origin of goods for all other purposes, including the country-of-origin marking and for statistical purposes. The scenario's shipment carries no preference claim — and its origin statement serves the non-preferential family's purposes: the marking, the statistics, and the measures that apply by origin without preference.

Answer 1 makes every origin statement a preference claim; the families are distinguished by purpose, and the scenario's purpose is the non-preferential one. Answer 3 abandons the non-preferential family — whose purposes run whether or not a preference is claimed. Answer 4 invents a direction-of-trade split that the families do not carry.

The takeaway: the origin's purpose picks the family — preference claims run the preferential rules; marking and statistics run the non-preferential rules — and the statement's purpose is named before the rules are chosen.

=== MCQ 2 ===
QUESTION:
A preference regime's rule requires the goods to have undergone a specified process in the regime's territory. Which option best states the learner's approach to the rule's threshold?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. The threshold is quoted from the learner's memory of the regime.
2. The threshold is quoted from the regime's instrument — with the annex or article reference — or withheld until the instruments fact sheet (customs-instruments-and-standards.md, planned) supplies it with citations; the lesson's concept-level map names the test type, and spec C4.7 forbids paraphrasing the wording from memory.
3. The threshold is decided by the importer's commercial needs.
4. The threshold is the same in every regime and can be stated generally.
CORRECT: 2
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 2. The spec's C4.7 rule governs the instruments' content: standards and instruments come from a fact sheet or the anchor, matched exactly, never paraphrased from memory. The lesson's concept-level map — wholly obtained, change of classification, process test, value-based tests — names the test's type; the threshold's wording is the instrument's, and the learner quotes it with the reference or withholds it until the fact sheet supplies the verified text.

Answer 1 substitutes memory for the instrument — the regime's negotiated wording is exactly what a remembered threshold cannot reproduce. Answer 3 makes the trader's commercial position the rule's source; the threshold is the instrument's, applied to the trader's goods. Answer 4 dissolves the regimes' differences — the tests' types are shared, and the thresholds are each regime's own.

The takeaway: the concept-level map is the lesson's; the thresholds are the instruments' — quoted with references, or withheld until the fact sheet carries them.

=== MCQ 3 ===
QUESTION:
Two identical consignments of Ghanaian goods arrive in the same foreign market the same week: one carries the destination agreement's certificate of origin; the other carries none. Which option best states the duty outcome?

POINTS: 1
OPTIONS:
1. Both pay the preferential rate, because the goods' origin is the same.
2. Both pay the general rate, because certificates are optional.
3. The certificated consignment claims the preferential rate — the evidenced origin claim — and the uncertificated consignment pays the general rate: the goods are the same, the evidence is the difference, and the duty follows the proof.
4. Both pay the general rate, because certificates apply only to imports into Ghana.
CORRECT: 3
EXPLANATION:
The correct answer is Answer 3. The lesson's mechanics are the evidence's mechanics: the preferential rate follows the evidenced claim. The certificated consignment's evidence — the certificate of origin or the invoice declaration the regime requires — proves the origin claim under the agreement's rules, and the preferential rate applies. The uncertificated consignment's origin is asserted but unevidenced — and the general rate applies, because the preference's machinery runs on proof.

Answer 1 grants the preference without the evidence — the claim's machinery does not run on sameness of origin. Answer 2 calls the certificate optional; section 70(6) names the certificate and the invoice declaration as the origin claim's forms, provided on the proper officer's request — and the regime requires its form before the claim is granted. Answer 4 invents a direction-of-trade limit on the certificates.

The takeaway: the evidence is the preference — the same goods with the proof pay the preferential rate, and without the proof pay the general rate. The professional's file is the duty's difference.
