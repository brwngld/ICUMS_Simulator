# Ghana Trade & Customs — Verified Fact Sheet

Last verified: 2026-10

Purpose: a source-checked reference for curriculum writers. Every fact below was
verified against the cited page during October 2026. Items that could not be
confirmed are listed in the final **DO NOT CLAIM** section and must not appear in
learner-facing material.

---

## 1. ICUMS (Integrated Customs Management System)

Verified facts:

- ICUMS stands for the Integrated Customs Management System and is described by
  Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) as a single-window e-Customs platform for
  Ghana's international trade. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/icums/)
- The system was rolled out in 2020 (the GRA page dates the rollout to June
  2020), when the Government of Ghana, the GRA Customs Division, UNIPASS-Ghana
  and other agencies "joined up to usher Ghana's trade to a single window
  system". (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/icums/)
- UNIPASS-Ghana is identified on the GRA page as the partner behind ICUMS. The
  company name "Ghana Link Network Services" does not appear verbatim on the
  pages fetched, so treat it as background knowledge, not a quotable fact.
- ICUMS succeeded the Ghana Customs Management System (GCMS) and the
  Pre-Arrival Assessment Report System (PAARS), which had been managed by GCNet
  Ltd and West Blue Consulting; the change was driven by high port transaction
  costs, delayed clearance, revenue leakage and the burden of multiple online
  platforms. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/icums/)
- ICUMS covers processing of documents and payments through a single window,
  assignment of a Unique Consignment Reference (UCR) for tracking cargo,
  valuation, classification, risk management and payment on one platform, with
  end-to-end tracking of each consignment. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/icums/)
- Under current import procedure, the customs declaration is submitted in ICUMS
  by the clearing agent; ICUMS generates the tax bill (covering classification,
  valuation and approval stages) and the importer pays duties and taxes at
  participating banks against that bill. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "ICUMS — the Integrated Customs Management System — is Ghana's single-window
  e-customs platform, operated under the UNIPASS arrangement in partnership with
  GRA Customs."
- "ICUMS replaced the earlier GCMS and PAARS systems, which were run by GCNet
  and West Blue Consulting."
- "In ICUMS a consignment is classified, valued, assessed and paid for on one
  platform, and every consignment carries a Unique Consignment Reference (UCR)."

## 2. GRA Customs Division

Verified facts:

- The Customs Division of the GRA has a three-fold responsibility: mobilisation
  of revenue from imports and exports, facilitation of legitimate trade, and
  provision of public security and safety. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/)
- Customs operates as a "first line of defence" protecting Ghana's territorial
  integrity through preventive operations at approved and unapproved borders,
  and operates at harbours, airports, land borders and parcel posts.
  (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "GRA Customs Division collects revenue on imports and exports, facilitates
  legitimate trade, and protects Ghana's security at harbours, airports, land
  borders and parcel posts."

## 3. Ghana National Single Window

Verified facts:

- GRA describes ICUMS as having moved Ghana's trade to "a single window system",
  i.e. the single-window concept is now embodied in ICUMS. (source: Ghana
  Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/icums/)
- The Ghana Shippers' Authority is an active trade-facilitation stakeholder: its
  site links to regulatory agencies and reports collaboration with the Ghana
  Standards Authority and the Environmental Protection Authority on permit
  matters. (source: Ghana Shippers' Authority, https://shippers.org.gh/)

Not verified: the 2015 introduction date of the Ghana National Single Window and
the transfer of its operation to the Ghana Shippers' Authority (reported year
~2022). The Shippers' Authority homepage fetched does not mention the Single
Window at all.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana operates a single-window approach to trade declarations: importers and
  agents lodge documents and payments through one system (ICUMS) rather than
  multiple platforms."

## 4. Ports and terminals (GPHA, Tema, Takoradi)

Verified facts:

- The Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA) manages the Port of Tema and
  the Port of Takoradi, plus a fishing harbour at Tema; a Keta port project is
  also on its agenda. (source: Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority, https://ghanaports.gov.gh/)
- The Port of Tema site includes port terminals (including the Golden Jubilee
  Terminal) and a dry dock among its facilities. (source: Ghana Ports and
  Harbours Authority, https://ghanaports.gov.gh/)

Not verified on the pages fetched: Tema's status as "the main container
gateway", the role of Meridian Port Services (MPS), the deep-water Terminal 3
and its ~2019 opening, and Takoradi's standing as one of Ghana's oldest ports
(1928).

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana's two seaports — Tema and Takoradi — are managed by the Ghana Ports and
  Harbours Authority (GPHA)."

## 5. Import permit agencies

Verified facts:

- Ghana's import procedure requires permits "from any of the regulatory
  agencies, dependent on the type of goods", with the Food and Drugs Authority
  (FDA) and the Ghana Standards Authority (GSA) explicitly named on the GRA
  import-procedures page. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- The Import Declaration Form (IDF) is obtained from the Ministry of Trade.
  (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- The Ghana Standards Authority (gsa.gov.gh) and the Food and Drugs Authority
  (fdaghana.gov.gh) are listed among official stakeholder links by the Ghana
  Shippers' Authority, and a Shippers' Authority news item refers to the
  Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) working with GSA on permit delays.
  (source: Ghana Shippers' Authority, https://shippers.org.gh/)

Not verified individually: the Plant Protection and Regulatory Services
Directorate (MoFA), Veterinary Services Directorate, National Petroleum
Authority, Energy Commission, Fisheries Commission, and Ghana Export Promotion
Authority as import/export permit agencies — the general "regulatory agencies"
statement covers the concept, but do not attribute specific functions to these
named bodies without further checking.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Depending on the goods, an importer may need permits from regulatory agencies
  such as the Food and Drugs Authority (FDA) for food and medicines, or the
  Ghana Standards Authority (GSA) for standards conformity."
- "An Import Declaration Form (IDF) is obtained from the Ministry of Trade."

## 6. Customs brokers, clearing agents and GIFF

Verified facts:

- Goods are imported "with the assistance of Customs Agents"; the customs
  declaration is submitted in ICUMS by the clearing agent, who also handles duty
  payment on behalf of the importer. (source: Ghana Revenue Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- The Ghana Institute of Freight Forwarders (GIFF) appears on the Ghana
  Shippers' Authority "Useful Links" list (ghanafreightforwarders.org),
  confirming it exists as a recognised Ghanaian freight-forwarding institution.
  (source: Ghana Shippers' Authority, https://shippers.org.gh/)

Not verified: GIFF's exact status as "the professional body" for freight
forwarders (its own website could not be fetched), and any licensing regime for
brokers.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "In practice, importers work through licensed customs clearing agents, who
  lodge the declaration in ICUMS and arrange duty payment."
- "The Ghana Institute of Freight Forwarders (GIFF) is a recognised Ghanaian
  institution for the freight-forwarding profession."

## 7. Components of an import duty assessment

Verified facts:

- The GRA import-procedures page confirms that the amount payable is generated
  by ICUMS as a tax bill and depends on the cost and type of goods and the rates
  specified for them, paid at participating banks. (source: Ghana Revenue
  Authority, https://gra.gov.gh/customs/import-procedures/)
- GRA maintains a separate "Customs Tariffs and Levies" resource (linked from
  the import-procedures page), confirming that levies exist alongside duty —
  but the individual levy names were not captured within this run's fetch
  budget.

Not verified: the exact names VAT, NHIL, GETFund levy, ECOWAS levy, EXIM levy,
AU import levy, special import levy, processing/discount charges as line items
of a Ghana assessment. NEVER state rates for any of these.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "An import assessment in Ghana consists of import duty plus additional taxes
  and levies, all computed when ICUMS assesses the declaration."

## 8. Border posts and transit corridors

Verified facts:

- The Trans-West African Coastal Highway is a regional road corridor serving
  (west to east) Mauritania, Senegal, The Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra
  Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and Nigeria; Ghana's section
  runs through Cape Coast and Accra to the border with Togo. (source: Wikipedia,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans%E2%80%93West_African_Coastal_Highway)
- The same article notes Ouagadougou is connected by paved highways to Abidjan,
  Accra and Lomé — i.e. a paved Accra–Ouagadougou axis exists. (source:
  Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans%E2%80%93West_African_Coastal_Highway)

Not verified: Aflao, Elubo and Paga as named border posts; the Tema–Ouagadougou/
Bamako/Niamey transit-corridor framing; the coastal highway's deviation inland
between Monrovia and Abidjan is documented and worth reflecting if the corridor
is described in detail.

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "Ghana sits on the Trans-West African Coastal Highway, which links it with
  Togo and Côte d'Ivoire to the east and west, and paved highways connect Accra
  northwards to Ouagadougou."

## 9. AfCFTA Secretariat

Verified facts:

- The AfCFTA's negotiations and implementation are overseen by a permanent
  secretariat based in Accra, Ghana. (source: Wikipedia,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Free_Trade_Area)
- Trading under the AfCFTA officially commenced on 1 January 2021 (described as
  a largely symbolic launch). (source: Wikipedia,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Free_Trade_Area)

SAFE PHRASINGS:

- "The AfCFTA Secretariat is headquartered in Accra, Ghana, and trading under
  the agreement commenced on 1 January 2021."

## DO NOT CLAIM

The following were NOT verified in this run and must not appear in
learner-facing material without fresh sourcing:

- The specific go-live date "28 April 2020" for ICUMS (the GRA page dates the
  rollout to June 2020; use "2020" or "mid-2020" only).
- "Ghana Link Network Services" as the verbatim operator name (only
  "UNIPASS-Ghana" was confirmed on GRA's page).
- The Ghana National Single Window's introduction in 2015.
- The transfer of Single Window operation to the Ghana Shippers' Authority in
  2022 (or any other year).
- Tema as "Ghana's main container gateway"; Meridian Port Services (MPS) as
  container-terminal operator; Terminal 3 as a deep-water terminal opened in
  2019; Takoradi as "one of Ghana's oldest ports" or opened in 1928.
- Any specific function attributed to: Plant Protection and Regulatory Services
  Directorate (MoFA), Veterinary Services Directorate, National Petroleum
  Authority, Energy Commission, Fisheries Commission, Ghana Export Promotion
  Authority.
- GIFF as "the professional body" for freight forwarders (existence confirmed
  only as a Shippers' Authority listed link).
- The named components of a duty assessment: VAT, NHIL, GETFund levy, ECOWAS
  levy, EXIM levy, AU import levy, special import levy, processing/discount
  charges.
- Any tax, duty or levy RATES, free-time periods, penalty amounts, or discount
  percentages — never in scope.
- Aflao, Elubo and Paga as named border posts on the Togo, Côte d'Ivoire and
  Burkina Faso borders; transit corridors framed as "Tema to Ouagadougou /
  Bamako / Niamey"; the Coastal Highway passing through those specific towns.
- Ghana's tariff being based on the ECOWAS Common External Tariff extended to
  8-digit national lines.
